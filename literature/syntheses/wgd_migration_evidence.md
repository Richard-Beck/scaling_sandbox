# Published migration data on enlarged and polyploid cells

Serves Fig 5 and the motivation for Fig 6. Drafted 2026-10-23 from the existing dossiers and `evidence/studies.csv`. Cards for the six studies are to be written by the restructure (keys to be assigned); until then, cite the dossiers.

## The comparisons

| Study | Cells and comparison | Migration assay | What it is not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Xuan 2018 | MDA-MB-231; polyploid giant cancer cells (nuclear area ≥2.5× the population mean) versus the rest of a heterogeneous culture | Sparse random migration, single-cell tracks, movie available | Not a defined 4N versus 2N comparison |
| Xuan 2020 | MDA-MB-231; paclitaxel-enriched PGCCs | Wound healing in a confluent monolayer, 10-min imaging reduced to 3-h coverage | Not sparse migration; no per-cell persistence |
| Mallin 2023 | PC3; cisplatin-induced polyaneuploid cancer cells (PACCs), 10 days after treatment, versus untreated parentals | Single cells, 30-min cadence, 24 h; path length, displacement, directness, chemotaxis | Not WGD-isogenic; PACCs very large, non-dividing; unequal densities |
| Jemaà 2017 | Sarcoma lines; near-tetraploid versus near-diploid clones selected from heterogeneous cultures (4 diploid, 5 tetraploid clones) | Low-density single cells, 30-min imaging, about 20–24 h; only example tracks and mean speed published | Tetraploidy selected, not induced |
| Jemaà 2023 | RKO; tetraploid clones generated by cytokinesis failure versus diploid clones | Single cells imaged hourly for 24 h; speed and endpoints | Closest to WGD, but coarse in time and velocity-based |
| Hosny 2026 | PC3; PACCs defined by area (≥1500 µm² at 16 h, ≥3-fold growth) under an oxygen gradient | 30-min cadence, 16 h, Cellpose-SAM tracking; distance, directness, aerotaxis | Hypoxia-specific; size defines the group, so size and PACC state are inseparable |

From the dossiers' provenance sections.

## What they show, as we have re-analysed them

From the narrative (Section 5, Fig 5):
- Enlarged groups were not consistently faster: slower in Xuan 2018 and Mallin, faster in Jemaà.
- They were more direct (Mallin about 3.1-fold, Hosny about 1.7-fold) or travelled further at long lags (Xuan about 2.1-fold).
- In Xuan 2018 the enlarged group's MSD starts lower and overtakes the reference group beyond about 2.3 h, the profile of slower but more persistent motion.
- Conditional OU fits infer longer velocity memory in all five comparisons (pooled fold about 16). The fit does not reproduce the Xuan crossover, so the fold is conditional on the OU form.

## How the source papers explain the difference

The authors attribute the phenotype to cytoskeletal or signalling changes: actin organization and stiffness (Xuan 2018), vimentin (Xuan 2020, Mallin), and hypoxia and HIF-1α (Hosny). None attributes it to size as such. Jemaà 2017 propose experimentally generated tetraploids to test whether WGD itself is causal; Jemaà 2023 is the closest to that design.

## Our interpretation (I)

- **The data fit our hypothesis but cannot isolate size.** Every comparison confounds size with ploidy state, stress history, cytoskeletal changes or culture conditions. That is the gap the SUM-159 WGD+/− pair is meant to fill.
- **Speed and persistence move apart in two studies.** Slower but more persistent enlarged cells (Xuan 2018, Mallin) run against the speed–persistence coupling of [@maiuri2015ucsp]. Where enlarged cells are faster (Jemaà), speed alone could explain any persistence gain. See `persistence_and_migration_theory.md`.
- **Within-population size associations are available.** Mallin Fig 2E–F (distance and directness against cell area) and the Hosny size–motility scatterplots (`evidence/hosny_2026/extraction/size_associations.csv`) relate persistence-type measures to size within groups. That is a test of size as such, partly free of the group confound, and worth summarizing in Fig 5 or its supplement.
- **No study measures protrusion or polarity dynamics.** The dossiers note that Mallin, Jemaà 2017 and Jemaà 2023 report no turning-angle distributions, velocity autocorrelation, protrusion frequency or polarity reversals, although the Mallin movies and Jemaà imaging could support some of these.

## Implications for the manuscript

- **Evidence role:** Fig 5 shows that published data are consistent with greater persistence in enlarged cells. It cannot show that size is the cause.
- Say in the Fig 5 text that the source papers propose cytoskeletal and signalling explanations, so a reviewer does not raise it first.
- Fig 6 should report per-cell size, so persistence can be regressed on size within the WGD+ and WGD− populations as well as between them.
