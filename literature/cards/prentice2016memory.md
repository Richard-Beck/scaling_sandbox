# Prentice-Mott et al. 2016: a slow cytoskeletal asymmetry stores direction

**Why it matters to us.** Cellular evidence for the "slow non-activity memory" route in Fig 2 (deBelly, Spring), and a protocol for measuring it.

## What they did
Neutrophil-like cells confined to 1D microfluidic channels with controlled chemoattractant exposure. A minimal model couples membrane signalling and cytoskeletal polarization lifetimes.

## Results
- [R] The cells show directional memory, which lets them orient in weak gradients and even in uniform environments.
- [R] Memory is modelled as a long-lived intracellular asymmetry that decays more slowly than membrane phospholipid signalling.
- [R] Moesin at the cell rear is long-lived; inhibiting it reduces memory.
- [R] ROCK inhibition stabilized moesin and memory. Microtubule depolymerization disoriented moesin deposition and also reduced memory.

## What transfers
- The separation of a fast signalling layer from a slow structural layer is the architecture of our mechanochemical models.
- The 1D-channel stimulus protocol is a template for the pulse-train and gap experiments that Fig 2F proposes.

## Relation to our claims
- Precedent for Fig 2: challenger gains retained across gaps without visible activity (deBelly, Spring). Present the concept as established and the size dependence as ours.
- (I) A slow memory layer would multiply persistence on top of drift geometry. It may help close the magnitude gap between α ≈ 2 (about 1.6-fold per WGD) and the OU fold of about 16 (`syntheses/persistence_and_migration_theory.md`).
