# Vaidžiulytė et al. 2022: persistence predicted from protrusion competition and axis alignment

**Why it matters to us.** Precedent and ready-made measures for the secondary-protrusion readout we propose for SUM-159. They infer persistence from the number of competing protrusions.

## What they did
hTERT-RPE1 cells (persistent) and HeLa cells (less persistent), with Golgi, membrane, microtubule and optogenetic Cdc42 lines. A minimal model places protrusion events at random over time and couples them to an internal polarity axis through two feedback parameters: the probability that a protrusion forms in front of the axis, and how strongly protrusions pull the axis towards them.

## Results
- [R] Protrusive unicity index, defined as 1 over the mean number of simultaneous protrusions: 0.65 ± 0.15 in RPE1.
- [R] Alignment index between polarity axis and direction of motion: 0.72 ± 0.21 in RPE1. The nucleus–Golgi axis follows the direction of migration.
- [R] Rab6 vesicle trafficking into protrusions precedes them, with a cross-correlation lag of about 19 min. Secreted cargo accumulates towards new protrusions.
- [R] Persistence time from direction autocorrelation: 2.3 ± 1.4 h in RPE1.
- [R] The model's persistence, unicity and alignment phase diagrams intersect at the measured values, giving both feedback strengths high in RPE1 and front-protrusion probability about half as large in HeLa.

## What transfers
- Unicity and alignment indices map directly onto our multiple-domain fraction and onto domain–heading alignment.
- The inference strategy (measure two structural quantities, then read persistence off a model) is the pattern our proposed SUM-159 pilot needs.

## Relation to our claims
- Method precedent for Fig 4 observables and Fig 6. Adopt their indices so the pilot is comparable with published values.
- (I) If larger cells show lower unicity at similar alignment, our multiple-domain account would predict a smaller size advantage than drift alone.
