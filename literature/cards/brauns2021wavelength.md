# Brauns et al. 2021: mass competition drives coarsening

**Why it matters to us.** General theory of how domains in mass-conserved systems compete and merge, independent of the reaction kinetics.

## What they did
Phase-space ("local equilibria") analysis of two-component mass-conserving reaction-diffusion systems, extended to weak source terms that break mass conservation.

## Results
- [R] Coarsening in mass-conserving reaction-diffusion systems is driven by self-amplifying mass transport between neighbouring high-density domains.
- [R] They derive a general coarsening criterion and show that coarsening in two-component systems is generically uninterrupted, whatever the reaction term.
- [R] Weak source terms can interrupt coarsening, by balancing mass competition with production and degradation, and can reverse it through domain splitting.

## What transfers
- Coexisting domains in our deterministic two-component models should eventually resolve to one; the question is how fast.
- Holmes model 3 has more components, and deBelly adds mechanics, so the uninterrupted-coarsening result may not apply to them directly.

## Relation to our claims
- Background for Fig 3 (resolution of coexisting domains) and Fig 4 (secondary domains).
- (I) The coarsening rate is set by mass exchange through the cytosol. With fast cytosolic diffusion that exchange is close to global, which would make competition only weakly dependent on separation. That fits Fig 3, where separation mattered mainly by setting the turning regime. Check against the paper's coarsening law before stating it.
