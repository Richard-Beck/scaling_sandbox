# Chiou et al. 2021: more polarity protein, more polarity sites

**Why it matters to us.** Experimental test of the saturation rule, including large yeast cells that start with several polarity sites.

## What they did
Budding yeast. They raise the amounts of key polarity proteins and score the number of polarity sites and buds, and analyse the mechanism in mass-conserved activator–substrate models.

## Results
- [R] Theory predicted that competition slows sharply as GTPase concentrations at the sites approach a saturation point, allowing sites to coexist. Increasing key polarity proteins produced multiple polarity sites and simultaneous budding, as predicted.
- [R] They describe a design principle by which cells can switch from competition to equalization among sites.
- [R] With total protein fixed, accumulation at the membrane depletes cytoplasmic substrate. This limits the size, peak concentration and potentially the number of active regions. Peaks with different protein content differ in how well they recruit substrate.
- [R] Figures include large cytokinesis-defective diploid cells that start with two or three polarity sites, showing either competition or coexistence.
- [R] A reviewer asked whether larger cells carry more polarity protein or a higher effective concentration, and noted the size difference between haploid and diploid cells.

## What transfers
- Substrate depletion limiting a site is the mechanism we invoke for Goryachev's slower initiation in larger cells (Fig 2B).
- Large cells starting with several sites is a cellular counterpart of the multiple-domain fraction rising with size in Fig 4E.

## Relation to our claims
- Precedent for Fig 2 (depletion route) and Fig 4 (coexistence in larger cells).
- (I) Their large cells conflate size with protein amount, the same confound WGD carries. Our models hold concentration fixed while size changes; say so when citing.

## Caveats
Abstract, figure captions and review only. Check the size and ploidy analysis in the full text before citing it as a size result.
