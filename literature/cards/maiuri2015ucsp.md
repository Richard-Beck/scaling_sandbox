# Maiuri et al. 2015: faster cells are more persistent (UCSP)

**Why it matters to us.** The standard explanation for differences in migration persistence. Any claim that enlarged cells are more persistent for a size-based reason has to be set against it.

## What they did
Migration data from many cell types in vitro and in vivo, with a theoretical model validated by modulating actin flow speed and by optogenetic control of an actin regulator's binding to filaments.

## Results
- [R] Cell persistence is robustly coupled to migration speed across systems: the universal coupling between cell speed and persistence (UCSP).
- [R] The proposed origin is advection of polarity cues by cell-scale actin flows.
- [R] The model gives a phase diagram of trajectory types covering the observed range of migration patterns.

## What transfers
Persistence differences between populations can arise from speed differences alone, through actin flow, without any change in the polarity circuit.

## Relation to our claims
- Competing explanation for Fig 5.
- (I) In Xuan 2018 and Mallin 2023 the enlarged cells are slower yet more persistent. Under UCSP slower cells should be less persistent, so these data run against that trend. That supports a speed-independent, possibly size-based, contribution. State it in the Fig 5 text, as our inference.
- (I) Jemaà shows the enlarged group faster. There, UCSP alone could account for any persistence gain, so Jemaà is the weakest test of our hypothesis.

## Caveats
Abstract only. Check whether UCSP is claimed within a population or only across conditions; the argument above assumes it applies within.
