# Weyer, Roth & Frey 2025: protein domains behave as if they had interfacial tension

**Why it matters to us.** An interface-level description of mass-conserved reaction-diffusion domains. Our Fig 3–4 edge analysis treats domains as pairs of interfaces, so this is the natural theoretical language for it.

## What they did
Theory of interface dynamics in mass-conserved reaction-diffusion systems, tested in simulations and in the in vitro Min protein system.

## Results
- [R] Protein patterns are governed by an effective interfacial tension that arises from cyclic attachment and detachment at the pattern interface, with no mechanical forces between molecules.
- [R] They derive non-equilibrium analogues of interface laws: a Gibbs–Thomson relation, a Neumann angle law and Plateau vertex conditions, and introduce "Turing mixtures" and "Turing foams".
- [R] Unlike liquid foams, these patterns can stop coarsening and select an intrinsic wavelength. They observe this in Min protein patterns.

## What transfers
A way to write domain-edge dynamics in terms of a few interface quantities, independent of the reaction details.

## Relation to our claims
- (I) Candidate theory for the Fig 3C–D edge-pulse response and for edge-limited drift. If edge motion obeys an interface law with a size-independent coefficient, drift at fixed physical diffusivity (α = 2) follows. Departures from α = 2 would then have to come from coupling between the two edges through the shared cytosolic pool.

## Caveats
From the arXiv version, press release and conference abstracts only. Mostly 2D patterns. Check whether the 1D two-interface case and noise are treated.
