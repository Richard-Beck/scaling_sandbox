# Bosgraaf & Van Haastert 2009: split protrusions sustain runs, de novo protrusions break them

**Why it matters to us.** A protrusion-event classification that maps onto our two turning regimes, and a template for the SUM-159 protrusion readout.

## What they did
About 2,000 protrusions tracked in randomly migrating Dictyostelium. Each growth episode recorded by its start and end positions and times, its origin (splitting from an existing protrusion or de novo), direction, and subsequent maintenance or loss.

## Results
- [R] Alternating split protrusions support persistent runs.
- [R] De novo protrusions tend to interrupt runs.

## What transfers
- Event-level measures to record: initiation site, extension width and rate, growth duration, overlap with existing protrusions, maintenance or retraction, eventual directional commitment, and recent local protrusion history (from the repo note).
- These are assay-design transfers. They are not evidence that the same event classes or size effects hold in mammalian cells.

## Relation to our claims
- (I) Splitting at the edge of an existing protrusion resembles our overlapping regime (Fig 3), where input at the incumbent's edge displaces it. De novo protrusion resembles the separated regime, where a new domain nucleates and may take over. This gives a cellular readout for Fig 4's two channels: edge drift and secondary-domain births.
- Method precedent for Fig 6.
