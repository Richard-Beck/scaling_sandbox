# Thüroff et al. 2019: in a mechanical cell model, larger cells are less persistent

**Why it matters to us.** A counterexample. Here size reduces persistence, so the sign of the size effect is not generic.

## What they did
A computational cell model for single cells and collectives, in which persistence depends on cell polarizability and contractility. Cells grow and divide above a threshold area.

## Results
- [R] Persistence time, speed and aspect ratio depend on polarizability. More strongly polarizing cells adopt crescent shapes and move more persistently.
- [R] Persistence depends mainly on the ratio of polarizability to contractility. An intermediate signalling radius gives the longest persistence.
- [R] With all else held constant, larger cells become rounder, slower and less persistent (their Appendix 2). The authors attribute this to the energy cost of adding membrane segments, which makes larger cells harder to polarize.

## What transfers
Whether size raises or lowers persistence depends on how size enters the model: through polarity-domain competition, as in ours, or through shape and mechanical constraints, as here.

## Relation to our claims
- (I) Counterpoint for the discussion. Our claim should be scoped: with polarity biochemistry on a fixed 1D geometry, larger size increases persistence in all five models. Shape and mechanics can push the other way.
