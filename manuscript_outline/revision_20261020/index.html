Figure 3. Separation affects native competition differently across models

Test whether greater incumbent–challenger separation slows competition at fixed circumference, then examine its geometric translation into measured activity direction.

Under continuous forcing, Goryachev handover increases with separation, while Holmes 3 is nearly flat over valid two-region placements. Wave-pinning and Spring show near-edge increases followed by broad plateaux. These are model-dependent responses under the declared cue, not a universal absence of distance effects in Wave-pinning.

Cue-free competition has a different distance response: Wave-pinning, Holmes 3 and Spring can coarsen very slowly, with many large-size resolution lower bounds. Goryachev includes a nonmonotone near-edge response at ratio 1; the full curve and every ratio are retained.

Actual global-heading measurements qualify the proposed geometry-to-persistence inference. Complete-region handover typically occurs at about half the requested cue angle. Cue-free fields can resolve to an intermediate heading, so a potential center step is not always the realized angular event. These data do not establish an unconditional geometry-driven persistence gain.
