# Vision-aligned checkpoint: enlargement, competition and activity-polarity memory

This checkpoint develops the original framework: larger incumbents contain more active material, with model-specific consequences for local input response and recovery; sustained competition measures what those responses actually accompany. Rings then test challenger placement within an unchanged complete incumbent state. A separate replicated experiment asks how activity polarity persists under declared local fluctuations.

These are working figure numbers within the mechanism-and-measurement component of the [manuscript vision](../MANUSCRIPT_VISION.md). Existing migration evidence and the reserved new WGD motility-assay results remain separate. WGD-associated enlargement and greater observed migration persistence motivate the questions; these figures concern activity organization and experimentally measurable activity dynamics.

## Figure 3 — Activity-polarity memory under declared local fluctuations

![Figure 3: replicated noisy-ring activity memory, magnitude and vector correlation, with exact selected local histories](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/vision_aligned_checkpoint_20261017/vision_aligned_figure3_noise.png)

**Message.** Under the higher declared input, larger rings retain greater activity-vector memory, with different local meanings. At lag 8000, full-window vector correlations increase from C20→C60 as 0.593→0.972 in WP and 0.310→0.783 in Holmes. Their resultant magnitudes remain near the initial value, so much of the memory loss reflects reorientation. Goryachev additionally changes forced-state/amplitude regime: C20's final mean R is 0.231, compared with 0.841 at C60, and its C20 deterministic mean alone removes the initial resultant. This is not a clean noise-specific directional comparison. At the lower input, vector correlations remain near one and decay is mostly unresolved over the measured lag window.

**Why this presentation.** The stochastic experiment is separate from selected deterministic competition examples. Ensemble initial-axis memory, magnitude and full-vector lag correlation answer different questions. Mean-input controls expose changes in the forced polarized state. Native histories identify the local patterns behind a weak or changing global vector. Both declared strengths and every model/size group remain visible; illustrative histories use a reproducible condition-medoid rule.

**Caption and definitions.** Each WP/Goryachev/Holmes ring at C20/40/60 has 12 independent noise realizations at each of two means, plus its deterministic mean-input and zero-input controls: 216 noisy and 27 control trajectories, each 32,000 native units. The added input is positive in the existing activation channel,

\[
I(s,t)=\mu_m\exp\!\bigl[Z(s,t)-\tfrac12\bigr],
\qquad
\mu_m=fA_m,\qquad f\in\{0.001,0.01\}.
\tag{3}
\]

Here Aₘ is the model's native finite-cue peak (WP 0.1, Goryachev 6.4, Holmes 1). The Gaussian field has unit point variance and periodic spatial parameter 1.5 native length units. Stationary OU knots have native time constant 50 and spacing 5, with variance-normalized linear interpolation between knots. This is the declared interpolated process, not an exact continuous-time OU process. Local statistics are fixed across circumference within a model; there is no per-realization or whole-ring normalization, and larger rings contain more independently fluctuating boundary. Inputs and clocks are not calibrated between models.

A shows ensemble mean Re P(t), memory of the initial activity axis, at both strengths. B shows mean R at the higher strength alongside deterministic mean and zero controls. C shows the uncentered full-vector lag correlation over the entire 0–32,000 interval, pooling within-realization time-origin cross-products and normalizing by both endpoint energies. Magnitude contributes; it is distinct from initial-axis memory and from direction-only correlation. Bands resample the 12 independent realizations (1000 whole-seed bootstrap draws, 95% intervals). Time origins are not uncertainty replicates. All native five-unit samples enter these curves.

D uses exact C20, f=0.01 native profiles: the WP, Goryachev and Holmes condition medoids, plus the Goryachev deterministic mean control. The declared medoid minimizes scaled distance to the within-condition median of final memory, minimum/mean magnitude and multisite fraction; it is not the most persuasive-looking realization. Both Goryachev histories share the same zero-based native concentration scale. The cut keeps the initial peak intact. All 18 medoids and the weakest-vector diagnostic remain in the full noise report.

**Interpretation limits.** The same physical displacement produces a smaller angular change on a larger circumference. Longer angular memory alone therefore cannot establish resistance to physical site drift. The conditional physical displacement statistic does not decrease uniformly with size in Holmes, and its eligible-pair coverage falls from 92% at C20 to 37% at C60. Goryachev's higher-input physical comparison has effectively no eligible pairs; this is unavailability under the declared single-region criterion, not evidence of no local activity. Read C alongside the [gated-direction/coverage, physical-displacement and all-medoid support](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/vision_noise_20261017/RESULTS.md). Dominant-site switches do not establish continuous site ancestry. First/last-half summaries expose continuing adaptation; restricted correlation integrals over 0–8000 are finite-window areas, not fitted lifetimes. Unresolved decay remains unresolved, negative correlations remain signed, and weak resultant magnitude is not silently removed from the vector analysis.

**Experimental readouts.** Estimate activity-polarity memory and lag correlation together with magnitude, weak-resultant intervals, local-site motion and event lifetimes. Declare upstream fluctuation statistics where controlled; in observational recordings the triggering input is unknown. Matched detected excursions measure subsequent establishment, not equal-input gain. Use common detection/follow-up rules and report event rates per cell-time and accessible-boundary-length–time.

**Evidence.** The [complete noise report](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/vision_noise_20261017/RESULTS.md), [closed 243-case/45-group analysis audit](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/vision_noise_20261017/supporting/metadata/vision_noise_analysis_audit.json), [full ensemble tables](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/vision_noise_20261017/supporting/data/) and [exact selected-source index](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/vision_aligned_checkpoint_20261017/supporting/data/vision_aligned_noise_examples.csv) preserve all conditions, controls, uncertainty and native history identities.
