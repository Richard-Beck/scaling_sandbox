# Manuscript figure descriptions

These descriptions pair an objective account of what is visible in each panel with a semantic interpretation of the modeled behavior. Percentages describe the fraction of simulated parameter conditions with the stated phenotype; they are not experimental sampling frequencies.

## Figure 1 — Cell enlargement preserves polarization but impairs polarity replacement

PNG: [figure_1_minimal_polarity.png](figures/figure_1_minimal_polarity.png)

- **A — Visual:** A two-compartment circuit places active polarity factor `u` on the membrane and inactive factor `v` in a rapidly mixing reservoir, connected by activation and inactivation arrows. Below it, three progressively longer cell icons represent 0.5×, 1×, and 2× volume while concentrations and kinetic parameters remain fixed. **Meaning:** The panel defines the minimal mass-conserved polarity model and makes cell size the controlled perturbation, so downstream differences can be attributed to geometry rather than retuning the chemistry.
- **B — Visual:** Three same-scale kymographs show a bright active-`u` domain forming at the left edge and persisting for 0.5×, 1×, and 2× volumes. A matched endpoint-state matrix is strongly diagonal, with 847 of 867 conditions retaining the same class between 1× and 2×. **Meaning:** A stable single cue can still generate and maintain a polarized front after enlargement, and most of the screened parameter space keeps its terminal state.
- **C — Visual:** Three parameter maps in baseline Λ–μ space contain nearly identical narrow single-polarized and dual-polarized bands at the three volumes; stars mark the representative condition. The adjacent line rises only from 3.75% to 4.24% to 4.39% persistent two-domain outcomes. **Meaning:** Enlargement has only a small effect on basic polarization competence or two-front persistence in the minimal model.
- **D — Visual:** After the cue switches from the left edge to the right, the 1× kymograph loses the old left domain and establishes a right domain, whereas the 2× kymograph retains substantial activity at both edges. **Meaning:** The enlarged system can respond to the new cue but has difficulty eliminating the old polarity front, converting a clean reversal into competition between fronts.
- **E — Visual:** An alluvial plot tracks the same 76 reversal-eligible conditions through 0.5×, 1×, and 2× volumes. The green clean-reversal band narrows from 83.0% to 71.9% to 48.6%, while the magenta dual/multiple band expands; smaller gray, blue, and orange bands show other outcomes. **Meaning:** The reversal defect is a population-level transition across matched parameter sets, not a peculiarity of the representative trajectory.
- **F — Visual:** Solid 1× and dashed 2× traces compare mean activity at the old left and new right endpoints around the shaded reversal interval, with a secondary step trace for detected domains. The 1× old-front signal collapses as the new front rises, while the 2× old-front signal remains elevated and the domain count reaches two. **Meaning:** Enlargement specifically weakens competitive elimination: front generation remains intact, but the old and new states coexist instead of resolving by winner-take-all replacement.

## Figure 2 — A Cdc42–Rac–Rho network reveals stronger size-dependent multipolarity

PNG: [figure_2_rho_network.png](figures/figure_2_rho_network.png)

- **A — Visual:** A three-node polarity schematic connects Cdc42, Rac, and Rho through activating and inhibitory interactions, followed by the same 0.5×, 1×, and 2× geometry-only size change used in Figure 1. **Meaning:** This panel adds Rho-family crosstalk while preserving the controlled size perturbation, allowing the effect of network richness to be separated from parameter rescaling.
- **B — Visual:** Rac kymographs at the three volumes retain a bright left-edge front under a stable cue; insets show matched terminal Cdc42, Rac, and Rho profiles. The endpoint-state matrix is predominantly diagonal, with 487 of 507 conditions unchanged between 1× and 2×. **Meaning:** The richer network also preserves single-cue polarity competence across enlargement, despite its additional regulatory structure.
- **C — Visual:** Λ–kR maps show a blue single-polarized region along the upper parameter boundary and a magenta dual-polarized region that expands markedly with volume. The summary curve rises from 5.0% to 8.6% to 13.6% persistent two-domain conditions. **Meaning:** In contrast with the minimal circuit, Rho-family crosstalk makes sustained competing fronts substantially more sensitive to cell enlargement.
- **D — Visual:** A large-cell Rac kymograph contains two persistent bright bands at opposite edges. The adjacent terminal profiles show Cdc42 and Rac concentrated at the candidate fronts and Rho redistributed in the opposite spatial pattern, with solid 0.5× and dashed 2× profiles. **Meaning:** The large-cell two-front state is a coherent network configuration rather than an isolated Rac fluctuation: the interacting species reorganize together around both fronts.
- **E — Visual:** A 1× reversal kymograph loses left-edge Rac and gains right-edge Rac, whereas a 2× kymograph retains activity at both sides; terminal three-species profile insets accompany both examples. **Meaning:** Network crosstalk preserves the same replacement defect seen in the minimal circuit and embeds it in a coordinated Cdc42–Rac–Rho state.
- **F — Visual:** An alluvial plot follows 231 matched reversal-eligible conditions across the three volumes. Clean reversal falls from 62.8% to 52.7% to 40.9%, while dual/multiple outcomes increase and smaller bands record retained, depolarized, and other outcomes. **Meaning:** The richer network amplifies size-dependent indecision: more enlarged cells sustain competing fronts and fewer complete a clean polarity switch.

## Figure 3 — Increasing model complexity preserves a common size-response fingerprint

PNG: [figure_3_size_response_fingerprint.png](figures/figure_3_size_response_fingerprint.png)

- **A — Visual:** Three linked boxes form a model-richness ladder from one-species polarity to Cdc42–Rac–Rho polarity to polarity plus mechanics. Small labels underneath identify the common challenges: single cue, competing cues, cue reversal, and migration. **Meaning:** The panel establishes a common comparison framework in which progressively richer models face the same phenotype tests.
- **B — Visual:** Six small line plots compare the minimal and Rho-family models across 0.5×, 1×, and 2× volume. Single-cue competence changes only modestly, persistent dual fronts rise—especially in the three-species model—and clean reversal declines in both models. **Meaning:** Added biochemical detail changes the magnitude of the response but preserves its qualitative fingerprint: front establishment is robust, front competition increases, and replacement becomes less reliable.
- **C — Visual:** A horizontal effect plot shows paired 1×→2× changes for both models on a common percentage-point axis. The single-cue changes lie near zero, dual-front changes are positive, and clean-reversal changes are negative, with larger multipolarity growth in the three-species network. **Meaning:** The central size effect is selective rather than a general loss of activity; decisiveness changes much more than the ability to polarize.
- **D — Visual:** Side-by-side alluvial plots show matched reversal outcomes for the minimal and Rho-family models. The annotations report that 68.3% and 63.0%, respectively, of conditions that reverse at 1× still reverse at 2×. **Meaning:** Both circuits lose roughly one-third of their previously successful reversals after enlargement, demonstrating a shared replacement vulnerability across model classes.
- **E — Visual:** A synthesis diagram separates front generation, front selection, and front replacement, labeling them as largely retained, size-sensitive, and consistently impaired. **Meaning:** The combined evidence supports a mechanistic distinction between producing a polarity front and selecting which front survives; enlargement primarily erodes the latter decision process.

## Figure 4 — Coupling polarity to mechanics connects signaling state to migration

PNG: [figure_4_mechanochemical_coupling.png](figures/figure_4_mechanochemical_coupling.png)

- **A — Visual:** A one-dimensional moving-cell cartoon places a polarity-driven protrusive force at the front and an inward/rear force at the opposite side, coupled through a Kelvin–Voigt spring and dashpot. The reference length `L₀` is identified as the explicit mechanical size variable. **Meaning:** The panel translates biochemical front location into deformation and movement, making the consequences of polarity states measurable as migration.
- **B — Visual:** Four representative columns compare one- and three-species cells at `L₀=10` and `40 µm`. Each column combines a laboratory-coordinate kymograph with front, center, and rear trajectories and a length-versus-time trace; the examples range from sustained translation to expansion, arrest, or delayed motion. **Meaning:** The same signaling phenotype can produce distinct physical trajectories depending on model architecture and mechanical size, so polarity state becomes consequential only after it is coupled to force balance.
- **C — Visual:** Six binary response plots show single-cue competence, persistent dual fronts, and clean reversal versus reference length for soft and stiff mechanics in the one- and three-species models. Some responses switch sharply at particular lengths, and the three-species reversal response is non-monotonic under stiff mechanics. **Meaning:** Mechanical size and compliance gate which polarity phenotypes are accessible; the response cannot be summarized as a uniform monotonic loss or gain across models.
- **D — Visual:** Three line charts report absolute net displacement, cumulative travel, and polarity-projected speed versus reference length for both species models and both mechanical settings. One-species curves are comparatively flat over an intermediate range, whereas three-species curves turn on sharply and then grow strongly with size in selected settings. **Meaning:** Mechanical coupling exposes large model-dependent differences that are not obvious from fixed-domain polarity alone, especially in the conversion of front organization into directed motion.
- **E — Visual:** Box-and-point distributions separate net displacement by terminal state: single pole, dual/multiple, failed reversal, and depolarized/other. Single-pole trajectories generally travel farther, dual/multiple trajectories remain near zero, and failed reversals show broad, heterogeneous displacement. **Meaning:** Directional migration is tied to polarity organization: a unique selected front is the most reliable basis for net motion, while competing fronts cancel or destabilize directional output.
- **F — Visual:** A cross-model heatmap summarizes the size effect for fixed one- and three-species polarity and moving one- and three-species cells. Fixed models show preserved competence but reduced reversal; moving models show distinct changes in dual fronts, reversal, and directional displacement. **Meaning:** Adding mechanics does not erase the biochemical size fingerprint, but it converts changes in front selection into large and model-specific migration consequences.

## Figure 5 — Tension feedback creates a mechanochemical constraint on polarity and migration

PNG: [figure_5_tension_feedback.png](figures/figure_5_tension_feedback.png)

- **A — Visual:** The moving-cell schematic is extended by a purple inhibitory arrow from positive tension back to front activity. Two labeled boxes distinguish a single global tension value from a spatially propagated local tension signal. **Meaning:** This closes the mechanochemical loop: deformation no longer only reports polarity output but can feed back to suppress the polarity machinery that generated it.
- **B — Visual:** Three global-feedback examples at `αₜ=0`, `5`, and `20` pair activity kymographs with length/tension and center-displacement traces. Increasing feedback shortens the lifetime of front activity, produces a transient tension pulse, and progressively curtails displacement. **Meaning:** Stronger tension-dependent deactivation converts sustained migration into a brief mechanical response followed by arrest.
- **C — Visual:** A four-column by four-row atlas compares one- and three-species models under global and spatial feedback. The first three rows map the fraction of soft/stiff conditions that end polarized, reverse cleanly, or retain dual fronts over `L₀` and `αₜ`; the bottom row maps mean absolute displacement. **Meaning:** Feedback reshapes polarity and movement jointly, with effects that depend on network, cell size, feedback strength, and whether the tension signal is global or spatial.
- **D — Visual:** Two displacement plots compare no feedback, global `αₜ=20`, and spatial `αₜ=20` over reference length. No-feedback cells retain or gain substantial displacement, global feedback suppresses it strongly, and spatial feedback reduces it nearly to zero. **Meaning:** The spatial feedback implementation is the stronger mechanochemical brake on migration across both model classes.
- **E — Visual:** A signed effect matrix asks whether each model/mechanics/feedback combination changes the `L₀=10→20 µm` size response. Most polarization entries remain near zero, selected reversal or dual-front entries shift by 50 percentage points, and displacement effects range from strong amplification to reductions exceeding 50–90%. **Meaning:** Tension does not simply weaken every phenotype; it selectively rewires how size affects front competition and, most dramatically, how polarity is translated into displacement.
- **F — Visual:** Normalized traces for a large cell with spatial feedback show a rapid front-activity pulse, rising strain and tension-associated signal, and displacement that approaches an early plateau as front activity decays. **Meaning:** The temporal ordering supports the proposed chain: size-dependent deformation builds tension, tension suppresses the front, and the loss of sustained front activity curtails further migration.

## Figure 6 — Integrated model-richness summary

PNG: [figure_6_integrated_summary.png](figures/figure_6_integrated_summary.png)

- **A — Visual:** A four-by-four matrix crosses modeled functions—establish one front, suppress competing fronts, reverse polarity, and sustain directional migration—with increasing model richness. Each cell contains the signed enlargement effect; blue denotes improvement, orange denotes impairment, near-white denotes little change, and the two purely biochemical models are marked “not modeled” for migration. **Meaning:** Across model classes, enlargement leaves front establishment almost unchanged but weakens competition and reversal in fixed chemistry; passive mechanics can create large migration gains, whereas spatial tension feedback reverses that gain and strongly suppresses directional output.
- **B — Visual:** Two cell cartoons contrast a stable external cue, which supports one front and sustained displacement, with changing or competing cues, which support separated old and new fronts and curtailed directional output. **Meaning:** The integrated interpretation is that large cells remain capable of polarization, but their greater spatial separation makes winner-take-all front selection slower or weaker; feedback then converts that indecision into impaired migration.

## Figure S1 — Single-species calibration and numerical validation

- **A — Visual:** Stable and unstable local reaction roots form an S-shaped relation between inactive `v` and active `u`. **Meaning:** The minimal reaction system contains the bistability needed for low and high membrane states, providing the local basis for wave-pinning polarity.
- **B — Visual:** At cytoplasm depth `h=1`, the Λ–μ map contains a narrow blue single-polarized band and a magenta dual-polarized segment within a larger homogeneous background. **Meaning:** Polarity is confined to a specific mass/length regime rather than appearing throughout parameter space.
- **C — Visual:** At `h=2`, the polarized band shifts upward in μ and extends broadly in Λ, with a smaller dual-polarized region at high Λ. **Meaning:** Changing reservoir depth shifts the polarity-permissive regime while retaining the same qualitative state organization.
- **D — Visual:** The `h=4` map is uniformly gray and explicitly labeled “homogeneous throughout.” **Meaning:** A sufficiently deep cytoplasmic reservoir removes polarization over the scanned range, demonstrating that compartment geometry is a genuine model control parameter.
- **E — Visual:** Four curves for small seed asymmetries show similar dual-domain incidence at `h=1–2` and a fall to zero at `h=4`. **Meaning:** The disappearance of dual fronts at large reservoir depth is robust to modest differences in the initialization asymmetry.
- **F — Visual:** A histogram of log10 relative mass-conservation errors lies many orders of magnitude left of the dashed `10⁻⁷` acceptance limit. **Meaning:** The single-species campaign conserves the modeled total mass to numerical precision, so the reported phenotype structure is not explained by integration drift.

## Figure S2 — Full single-species size-response maps

PNG: [figure_s2_single_species_size_maps.png](figures/figure_s2_single_species_size_maps.png)

- **A — Visual:** Nine single-cue Λ–μ maps are arranged with cytoplasm depth `h=1,2,4` by rows and volume `0.5×,1×,2×` by columns. Polarized bands persist with modest size-dependent shifts for `h=1–2`, whereas every `h=4` map is labeled homogeneous throughout. **Meaning:** Enlargement largely preserves the location of the single-cue competence regime; reservoir depth has a much stronger effect than volume on whether polarization is possible.
- **B — Visual:** Nine competing-seed maps use the same layout. Magenta dual-polarized regions occupy most of the polarization-permissive bands at `h=1–2` and tend to replace blue single-polarized regions as volume increases; `h=4` remains homogeneous. **Meaning:** When two fronts are seeded, enlargement modestly favors their persistence, but only inside the biochemical regime already capable of supporting polarity.
- **C — Visual:** Nine reversal maps show green clean reversal, blue old-front retention, magenta dual/multiple fronts, gray depolarization, orange other outcomes, and light-gray ineligible conditions. Across `h=1–2`, the green region contracts and magenta regions expand toward 2× volume; all `h=4` conditions are explicitly marked ineligible. **Meaning:** The exhaustive sweep confirms that the size defect is concentrated in front replacement: enlarged conditions increasingly retain multiple fronts rather than losing polarity altogether.
- **D — Visual:** Three histograms show successful reversal times at 0.5×, 1×, and 2×. Most reversals occur shortly after the challenge, but the 2× distribution is broader and includes a longer tail. **Meaning:** Even among conditions that eventually reverse, enlargement can slow resolution of the old and new fronts.

## Figure S7 — Tension-feedback diagnostics

PNG: [figure_s7_tension_diagnostics.png](figures/figure_s7_tension_diagnostics.png)

The curves show distinct size dependence, including strong growth for one-species low-feedback conditions and smaller or non-monotonic three-species responses. **Meaning:** Global tension exposure is an emergent function of cell size and signaling dynamics rather than a fixed penalty.
- **B — Visual:** Spatial-feedback exposure forms four nearly horizontal bands across reference length, separated by species and feedback strength. **Meaning:** In the spatial implementation, the integrated inhibitory load is set mainly by network and `αₜ`, with little residual dependence on size over this campaign.

- **E — Visual:** Spatial tension-associated signal profiles at each trajectory’s peak mean signal decay from left to right; the `L₀=40 µm` profile has a larger, more localized left-side peak than the `10 µm` profile. **Meaning:** Larger cells can generate a stronger spatially localized inhibitory signal near the active front, providing a mechanism for size-dependent suppression.

- **H — Visual:** Four conservation-error distributions for species and feedback mode lie well left of the dashed `10⁻⁷` limit. **Meaning:** The feedback-dependent phenotypes are not artifacts of numerical loss or gain of conserved molecular mass.
