# Mallin et al. 2023 — structured data summary

**Mallin MM et al.** *Cells in the polyaneuploid cancer cell (PACC) state have increased metastatic potential.* **Clinical & Experimental Metastasis** 40:321–338 (2023). ([Springer][1])

* **R** = explicitly reported in text/caption.
* **P** = numerical value printed directly on the figure.
* **F** = approximate value read visually from the figure.

I keep the measurements separate from the authors' interpretations.

---

## Experimental system and definition of the comparison

The study uses **PC3-luc prostate cancer cells** throughout. Cells were cultured in RPMI + 10% FBS. The PACC population was generated by plating **625,000 cells/T75 flask**, then exposing them 24 h later to **6 μM cisplatin for 72 h**. Drug was then removed and the surviving treated cells were maintained for a further **10 days**; by day 10, surviving cells are termed definitive PACCs. The authors report that these cells do not divide during this recovery period but continue increasing in 2D area. ([Springer][1])

For bulk molecular experiments, remaining smaller cells could additionally be removed using **15-μm filters**. Migration experiments used the day-10 PACC population but were not described as relying on this filtration step. ([Springer][1])

This is therefore **not a clean WGD+/WGD− comparison**. The contrast is:

> untreated PC3 parental cells versus **cisplatin-induced, stress-selected, endoreduplicated polyaneuploid cells 10 days after treatment**.

PACC status is consequently inseparable from prior cisplatin exposure and the prolonged post-treatment state in this design.

The paper defines the PACC state by a combination of:

* markedly enlarged cell and nuclear size;
* increased DNA content;
* lack of mitotic division;
* continued endoreduplication/endocycling.

It is not a uniform 4N population.

# Figure 1 — induction and DNA-content validation of the PACC state

## 1A — morphology over the transition

**Phase-contrast images, 20×** are shown for:

* untreated cells;
* **1 day after cisplatin treatment**;
* **10 days after treatment**, definitive PACC state.

The untreated field is densely populated by ordinary-sized PC3 cells. One day after cisplatin, the field is visibly much sparser and contains enlarged surviving cells. At day 10, the illustrated cells are extremely large and occupy a large fraction of the field individually. White outlines demarcate representative PACC borders. No quantitative cell-area data are presented in this panel. 

The authors state that cell death continues until approximately day 10 and that treated survivors do not divide during the 10-day observation period. ([Springer][1])

This is measured **1 day after treatment**, not at definitive day-10 PACC status.

The percentages are printed directly on the figure:

| DNA-content category | Untreated | Cisplatin-treated |
| -------------------- | --------: | ----------------: |
| **2N+**              | **42.6%** |          **4.5%** |
| **4N+**              | **36.8%** |         **30.4%** |

# Figure 2 — single-cell motility in uniform serum

This is one of the most informative migration experiments in the paper.

### Assay conditions

The authors use an **Ibidi μ-Slide Chemotaxis system**, but for this experiment both reservoirs contain uniform **20% FBS** (“+/+”).

Seeding concentrations differ substantially to compensate for PACC size:

* non-PACC parentals: **3,000,000 cells/mL**
* PACCs: **500,000 cells/mL**

Cells adhere overnight and are then imaged:

* **10× objective**
* **every 30 min**
* for **24 h**
* 37 °C, 5% CO₂, 20% O₂.

Tracks are generated in ImageJ using Manual Tracking/Chemotaxis and Migration macros. Cells that divide, apoptose or leave the field are excluded. ([Springer][1])

The caption explicitly states **n=50 parental tracks and n=49 PACC tracks** for the spider plots. The same experiment underlies panels A–D, although the caption only attaches those exact n values to panel C. ([Springer][2])

---

## 2A — accumulated distance

**Scatter plot**, y-axis 0–1250 μm.

Accumulated distance is defined as the **total path length**, i.e. the sum of all frame-to-frame movement.

**F approximate central values:**

* non-PACC parentals: **~300–310 μm**
* PACCs: **~160–170 μm**

**R:** PACCs travel significantly **less accumulated distance**, **p=0.0002**. 

The distributions contain useful detail:

* parental cells are mostly ~150–450 μm, with some reaching ~700–750 μm;
* PACCs have a lower dense cluster, largely ~75–250 μm, but also a small high-distance tail extending to roughly **700–1100 μm**.

So the difference is not that every PACC is slow; there is a heterogeneous PACC tail with very large total path lengths.

---

## 2B — Euclidean distance

**Scatter plot**, y-axis 0–300 μm.

This is defined as the **straight-line displacement from the starting point to the endpoint**.

**F:**

* non-PACC: **~40 μm**
* PACC: **~65–70 μm**

**R:** PACC Euclidean displacement is greater, **p=0.0002**. 

Distributionally:

* most parental cells finish within roughly **20–70 μm** of their starting point;
* PACCs have a broader upper tail, with many ~75–150 μm and some approaching **200–280 μm**.

Thus the paper directly shows the apparently paradoxical combination:

* **lower accumulated path length**
* but **greater net displacement**.

---

## 2C — spider trajectories

All trajectories are translated so that the initial position is \((0,0)\).

The non-PACC tracks form a dense, approximately isotropic cluster around the origin. Many paths are visibly tortuous and repeatedly traverse the same local region.

The PACC trajectories occupy a visibly much larger spatial footprint, with several tracks extending **>100–200 μm** from the origin. 

The published spider plots contain much richer trajectory geometry than is numerically summarized in the paper; the authors do not report turning-angle distributions, velocity autocorrelation, pause lengths, etc.

---

## 2D — directness

Directness is explicitly defined as:

$$
\text{Directness} =
\frac{\text{Euclidean displacement}}
{\text{Accumulated path length}}
$$

so:

* **1** = perfectly straight movement;
* values approaching **0** = highly indirect/tortuous movement.

**F:**

* non-PACC: **~0.13–0.14**
* PACC: **~0.40–0.42**

**R:** **p<0.0001**. 

The PACC distribution is also considerably broader:

* parental population: mostly ~0.05–0.2, with a few values up to ~0.5;
* PACCs: roughly ~0.05–0.8+, including multiple values around 0.5–0.7 and an upper point near ~0.9.

So the roughly threefold shift in the mean/central value is accompanied by a substantial high-directness subpopulation.

---

## 2E — Euclidean distance versus cell area

The plot is informative about the actual size ranges.

### Non-PACC parentals

* cell area mostly **~1,000–4,000 μm²**
* approximate total range ~700–7,000 μm²
* Euclidean displacement mainly ~25–100 μm.

**P:** \(R^2 = 0.007051\).

### PACCs

* most cell areas approximately **15,000–80,000 μm²**
* some cells exceed **100,000 μm²**
* one plotted cell is approximately **140,000 μm²**
* Euclidean distances extend to ~450 μm in the most extreme point.

**P:** \(R^2 = 0.008724\). 

Both regressions are reported as nonsignificant.

---

## 2F — directness versus cell area

**P:**

* non-PACC: \(R^2 = 0.00008172\)
* PACC: \(R^2 = 0.0594\)

The PACC fitted line slopes downward slightly visually, but the relationship is reported as nonsignificant. 

So within each population, the measured variation in size does not account for appreciable variation in either Euclidean displacement or directness.

## 3E — forward migration index

The authors calculate:

* **parallel FMI** — displacement along the gradient axis;
* **perpendicular FMI** — displacement orthogonal to it.

A positive parallel FMI indicates net movement toward higher FBS.

**F:** Most population means in control conditions sit very close to **0**. PACC −/+ shows the clearest positive parallel FMI, approximately **~0.1**, while its perpendicular FMI remains around zero/slightly negative. Individual values are very widely distributed, extending approximately from −0.7 to +0.8 or more. 

No exact pairwise FMI p-values are printed in the panel. The authors state that the FMI criteria for chemotaxis are fulfilled only for the PACC gradient condition. ([Springer][1])

## 4B — AFM perinuclear Young's modulus

Cells were plated to approximately **25% confluence**:

* non-PACC: **80,000 cells/60-mm dish**
* PACC: **10,000 cells/dish**.

AFM indentation used a maximum force of **1 nN** in the perinuclear region. ([Springer][1])

The scatter plot has a y-axis of **0–30 kPa**.

Most cells in both groups lie at roughly **<2–3 kPa**, and the horizontal central bars appear around approximately **1–1.5 kPa**.

The distributions differ visibly in their upper tails:

* parental cells mostly remain below ~5–8 kPa;
* PACCs include rare observations around **~10–12, ~20 and almost 30 kPa**.

**R:** group difference **p=0.5765**, i.e. not significant.

The custom PDMS device contains channels:

* **3, 6, 10, 20 and 50 μm wide**
* **200 μm long**
* **10 μm high**
* coated with **20 μg/mL collagen I**.

Cells are loaded at **1,000,000 cells/mL**, with a **0→20% FBS gradient** across the device. ([Springer][1])

The main figure shows PACC examples only for:

* **10-μm channel**
* **20-μm channel**.

### 10-μm example

Displayed frames:

* **3.5 h**
* **6 h**
* **16.5 h**
* **28.5 h**
* **30 h**

The cell progressively enters and passes through the narrow channel. 

### 20-μm example

Displayed frames:

* **5 h**
* **15 h**
* **17 h**
* **22 h**
* **32.5 h**. 

This experiment is **demonstrative rather than comparative**:

* no non-PACC control is shown;
* no transit success fraction is reported;
* no speed is reported;
* no deformation ratio is quantified;
* no results are presented for the 3-, 6- or 50-μm channels.

It therefore establishes that individual PACCs **can** remain motile while deforming through 10- and 20-μm-wide, 10-μm-high channels.

### Internal timing discrepancy

There are three incompatible duration statements:

* Fig. 4 caption: **36-h time lapse**;
* Methods: imaging every 30 min for **24 h**;
* Supplementary video descriptions: **48 h**.

The presence of a **32.5-h frame** in Fig. 4C itself rules out 24 h as the duration of the specific displayed experiment. ([Springer][4])

# Important provenance / measurement qualifications

### 1. This is not a WGD-isogenic experiment

The comparison is untreated PC3 versus a **cisplatin-selected, stress-induced PACC state** generated by endoreduplication. The PACC group is:

* substantially larger;
* non-dividing;
* highly polyploid/polyaneuploid;
* 10 days post chemotherapy.

Therefore the study cannot isolate the causal effect of one whole-genome-doubling event.

---

### 2. The migration experiment is genuinely single-cell and reasonably high frequency

The main motility experiments use:

* **30-min cadence**
* **24 h**
* individual trajectories
* path length
* endpoint displacement
* directness
* chemotactic directionality.

This is substantially more information than an endpoint wound-healing assay.

However, the paper still does not calculate:

* framewise velocity distributions;
* turning-angle distributions;
* velocity autocorrelation;
* persistence time;
* MSD;
* pause/run state occupancy;
* protrusion frequency;
* morphology as a function of time.

Those could in principle be extracted from the released movies.

---

### 3. Cell densities are deliberately unequal between populations

For migration:

* parental **3×10⁶ cells/mL**
* PACC **0.5×10⁶ cells/mL**.

For MTC:

* 30,000 versus 3,000 cells/well.

For AFM:

* 80,000 versus 10,000 cells/dish.

These adjustments are explicitly made to accommodate the enormous size difference. ([Springer][1])

Actual local confluence at the start of the migration movie is not numerically reported.

---

### 4. PACC size is extreme

From Fig. 2E, parental cells mostly occupy approximately **10³–10⁴ μm²**, whereas individual PACCs commonly occupy **several ×10⁴ μm²**, with some approaching **10⁵–1.4×10⁵ μm²**. 

So this is a much more extreme size/ploidy phenotype than a conventional stable tetraploid clone.

---

### 5. Directness is clearly defined

Unlike some papers using vague “persistence” terminology, this study provides a specific formula:

$$
D/A
$$

where \(D\) is endpoint Euclidean displacement and \(A\) is accumulated path length.

It is therefore essentially a **track straightness ratio**, not a persistence time or MSD exponent. ([Springer][1])

---

### 6. Confined migration is not a comparative experiment

The microchannels demonstrate that PACCs can pass through 10- and 20-μm channels while moving up an FBS gradient.

They do **not** establish from this experiment that PACCs:

* pass channels more frequently than parentals;
* pass them faster;
* tolerate narrower channels;
* deform more than parentals.

No parental channel data are shown.

---

### 7. The vimentin perturbation has a useful but limited causal structure

Acrylamide reduces:

* parental and PACC Euclidean displacement;
* **PACC directness**, but not parental directness.

However:

* acrylamide is a chemical perturbation rather than VIM-specific genetic knockdown in this paper;
* endpoint VIM abundance itself is essentially uncorrelated with motility among individual PACCs.

Both observations need to be retained together. 

---

### 8. One cancer line

All mechanistic experiments are performed in **PC3-luc**. The authors explicitly acknowledge this as a limitation. ([Springer][1])

---

# Authors' interpretations and hypotheses — separate from measurements

The authors interpret the Fig. 2 results as showing that PACCs are **more effectively migratory despite moving less overall**, because their motion is more direct and yields greater net displacement. They regard Euclidean displacement as more relevant to metastatic escape than accumulated path length. ([Springer][1])

They interpret the chemotaxis experiments as evidence that PACCs acquire enhanced **environment/resource sensing**, because only PACCs show statistically nonuniform orientation toward the 0→20% FBS gradient. They further speculate from the unusually large movement in zero-serum conditions that PACCs may engage a resource-search strategy analogous to optimal foraging. ([Springer][1])

The authors interpret the combination of:

* reduced peripheral MTC stiffness;
* maintained average perinuclear AFM stiffness;
* observed movement through confined channels

as evidence that PACCs possess a **“hyper-elastic” cytoskeletal phenotype**: highly deformable under tension but able to maintain nuclear/perinuclear mechanical integrity under compression. This is their mechanistic synthesis of several different assays, rather than a directly fitted hyperelastic stress–strain measurement. ([Springer][1])

They propose that increased VIM contributes both to PACC mechanical properties and directed migration. At the same time, they explicitly conclude that VIM **cannot independently explain** the motility phenotype because VIM abundance shows essentially zero correlation with individual-cell motility metrics. ([Springer][1])

Finally, the paper argues that the combined motility, chemotaxis, deformability and anoikis-resistance phenotypes predict increased metastatic competence. The study itself does **not perform an in-vivo metastasis test**, which the authors identify as a major limitation. ([Springer][1])
