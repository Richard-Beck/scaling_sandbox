# Bosgraaf and Van Haastert (2009): protrusion events in random migration

Leonard Bosgraaf and Peter J. M. Van Haastert (2009). **The Ordered Extension of Pseudopodia by Amoeboid Cells in the Absence of External Cues.** *PLoS ONE* **4**(4): e5253. Published 22 April 2009. DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0005253](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0005253). PMID: 19384419; PMCID: PMC2668753.

[Publisher full text](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0005253) · [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2668753/) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19384419/)

The study follows about 2,000 protrusions in randomly migrating *Dictyostelium*. It represents individual growth episodes by their start/end positions and times, and records origin (splitting versus de novo), direction, and subsequent maintenance/loss. Alternating split protrusions support persistent runs; de novo protrusions tend to interrupt them. This motivates measuring the sequence and outcome of local shape events alongside the centroid trajectory.

For the proposed HT1080 size comparison, retain event initiation site, extension width/rate, growth duration, overlap with existing protrusions, maintenance/retraction, and eventual directional commitment. Also retain recent local protrusion history to distinguish renewed activity from activity at previously quiet sites. These are assay-design transfers, not evidence that the same event classes or size effects hold in HT1080.
