# Xuan et al. 2018 — structured data summary

**Xuan B et al.** *Dysregulation in Actin Cytoskeletal Organization Drives Increased Stiffness and Migratory Persistence in Polyploidal Giant Cancer Cells.* Scientific Reports 8:11935 (2018). ([Nature][1])

## Experimental system and definition of PGCC

The study uses **MDA-MB-231 triple-negative breast cancer cells** throughout. Cells were maintained in 50:50 DMEM/Ham's F12 + 10% FBS at 37 °C, 5% CO₂. ([Nature][1])

PGCC classification was based principally on **nuclear area ≥2.5× the average nuclear size of the parent population**, corresponding to approximately **≥250 μm²** in MDA-MB-231. Flow-cytometric DNA-content measurements were used as corroboration. The authors report that PGCCs constitute approximately **2–5%** of untreated cultures and span a broad excess-DNA distribution, including cells above 4N and extending through approximately 8N/16N+. Thus this is **PGCC versus non-PGCC within a heterogeneous parental culture**, not a defined 2N-versus-4N isogenic comparison. ([Nature][1])

The paper-wide statistical methods state **3 biological replicates, ≥3 technical replicates and ≥15 cells unless otherwise specified**, Student's *t*-test for two-condition comparisons, with results reported as mean ± SEM. ([Nature][1])

# Figure 4 — random migration

This is the most directly relevant migration experiment.

**Experimental conditions:** type-I rat-tail collagen at **20 μg/mL** in 24-well plates; **5,000 cells/well**, then **24 h attachment/growth before imaging**. Hoechst-labelled nuclei were imaged **every 10 min for 12 h** at 37 °C/5% CO₂ and their x-y coordinates tracked computationally. The actual cell density/confluence at the start of imaging is **not reported**. The paper therefore provides initial seeding density, but not a measured migration-time density. ([Nature][1])

## Authors' interpretations and hypotheses — kept separate from measured results

The authors interpret lower cytoplasmic and nuclear particle MSD amplitudes as evidence for **increased stiffness** in PGCCs. They propose that the larger/longer actin stress fibres contribute to this mechanical difference and use the inhibitor experiments to argue for involvement of the **RhoA–ROCK1/actin network**. ([Nature][1])

For migration specifically, they describe PGCCs as exhibiting a **“slow but steady” / persistent migratory phenotype**. They further speculate that PGCCs have an **apparent loss of conventional polarity**, associated with abnormal microtubule organization and multiple MTOCs, and propose that abnormal actin organization/turnover could reduce their ability to change direction and thereby produce increased persistence. That proposed link between polarity defects and persistence is **an author hypothesis, not directly tested with a polarity reporter or reorientation experiment**. ([Nature][1])

For Fig. 5, they interpret the increasing PGCC nuclear MSD following scratch as migration-associated **nuclear softening/chromatin reorganization** and propose that this facilitates migration. ([Nature][1])

They also speculate from the qualitative S5 observations that PGCCs may express increased cell-cell adhesion and adopt a **stromal-like role**; again, adhesion expression/function was not quantitatively tested in this study. ([Nature][1])
