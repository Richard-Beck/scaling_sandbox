# Xuan et al. 2020 — structured data summary

**Xuan B, Ghosh D, Jiang J, Shao R, Dawson MR.** *Vimentin filaments drive migratory persistence in polyploidal cancer cells.* PNAS 117(43):26756–26765 (2020), DOI 10.1073/pnas.2011912117. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][1])

* **R** = explicitly reported in text/caption.
* **P** = numerical value printed directly on the figure.
* **F** = approximate value read visually from the figure.

I am treating **authors' mechanistic interpretations separately from measurements**.

## Experimental system and definition of PGCC

The principal system is **MDA-MB-231 triple-negative breast cancer cells**. MDA-MB-453 and SKBR-3 are additionally used to test whether the chemotherapy-resistance phenotype generalizes to other breast-cancer lines. MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-453 were cultured in 50:50 DMEM/Ham's F12; SKBR-3 in modified McCoy's 5A, all with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin at 37 °C, 5% CO₂. ([ResearchGate][2])

For many experiments, the authors generated what they call a **“purified” PGCC population** by treating MDA-MB-231 with **500 nM paclitaxel for 18 h**, followed by **7 d recovery**. PGCCs were defined as cells containing **>2.5× the DNA quantity of the average population**, determined by DAPI/Hoechst fluorescence. ([ResearchGate][2])

This therefore remains an important qualification:

**PGCC vs non-PGCC is not a clean isogenic 2N vs 4N comparison.** PGCCs are heterogeneous, highly polyploid/multinucleated cells enriched by severe paclitaxel exposure. The treatment history is consequently coupled to the PGCC state in experiments using the purified population.

The migration assay has another important distinction from Xuan 2018: it is a **fully confluent scratch-wound assay** rather than sparse random migration. MDA-MB-231 cells were seeded “at high concentration,” grown to **100% confluence** in collagen-coated 24-well plates, scratched with a 10-μL pipette tip, and imaged every **10 min for 12 h**. ([ResearchGate][2])

# Figure 3 — scratch-wound migration

This is the central direct migration experiment.

### Assay conditions

* **MDA-MB-231**
* 24-well plates.
* **20 μg/mL type-I rat-tail collagen**, 1 h pretreatment.
* Cells seeded at high density and grown to **100% confluence**.
* Cross-shaped scratch made with a **10-μL pipette tip**.
* Washed 3× PBS.
* **1 μg/mL Hoechst 33342**, 30 min.
* Imaging at 37 °C/5% CO₂.
* **10-min cadence for 12 h**.
* CellProfiler nuclear segmentation; nuclei were computationally dilated to estimate monolayer coverage.
* PGCCs classified from segmented nuclear area.
* **n ≥ 500 cells per condition.** ([ResearchGate][2])

The paper reports measurements at **3-h intervals** despite acquisition every 10 min.

DAPI images at 12 h for:

These panels visibly establish a **very dense/confluent starting population**, unlike the sparse-cell migration experiment in the 2018 paper.

### What is *not* extracted from the high-frequency movie in the publication

Although the experiment was acquired at **10-min temporal resolution**, the main published quantitative analysis does **not** report:

* instantaneous cell velocities;
* complete single-cell trajectories;
* turning-angle distributions;
* persistence times;
* MSD;
* protrusion/retraction dynamics.

The “migratory persistence” terminology largely carries over from the authors' 2018 trajectory analysis. The direct 2020 readouts are primarily **wound coverage and population composition within the wound**.

# Important provenance / measurement qualifications

There are several things worth preserving explicitly in a structured database for this paper.

**PGCC identity.** These are not defined 4N WGD+ cells. PGCC is operationally **>2.5× average DNA content**, and the principal enriched PGCC population is generated by **500 nM paclitaxel exposure**.

**Migration context.** The migration phenotype in this paper is measured from a **100%-confluent, wounded monolayer**, so cell-cell crowding/jamming is inherent to the assay. This is fundamentally different from the sparse random-migration experiment in Xuan 2018.

**Temporal resolution versus published metric resolution.** Images were acquired every **10 min**, but the paper mostly reduces them to **3-hourly wound-coverage/population-composition measurements**. The potentially much richer per-cell temporal information is not reported.

**“PGCC fraction migrated.”** Figs. 3E/F and 7C report the **composition of cells found in the migrated region**. They are not explicitly normalized to each cell type's abundance in the starting population and therefore are not direct estimates of \(P(\mathrm{migration}\mid\mathrm{PGCC})\) versus \(P(\mathrm{migration}\mid\mathrm{nonPGCC})\).

**Vimentin polarity.** This is a **fixed-cell spatial classification**, not a live reporter of polarity dynamics. Migration direction is inferred from position relative to the scratch plus cell morphology/lamellipodia.

**Persistence.** Unlike Xuan 2018, this paper does not directly calculate an MSD exponent, persistence time, turning rate or trajectory straightness. Its direct measurements concern wound entry, vimentin distribution and vimentin polarization; “persistent migration” is partly inherited from the authors' previous work.

**Statistics.** Figure captions state Student's *t*-tests and SEM. Some figures contain single-star significance symbols even where the caption only defines ** and ***, creating ambiguity in the exact corresponding threshold.

# Authors' interpretations and hypotheses — separate from measurements

The authors interpret the higher total and more spatially distributed vimentin network in PGCCs as providing **structural support for their enlarged morphology**, and interpret the cell-volume reduction after acrylamide as supporting this role. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][1])

They argue that **polarized VIF organization helps PGCCs transition from jammed to unjammed states and supports directional/persistent migration**. In particular, they attribute the reduction in PGCC migration after acrylamide/VIM-siRNA treatment to the accompanying loss of leading-edge vimentin polarization. This is their causal interpretation of the coupled perturbational observations. ([ResearchGate][2])

In the Discussion they further **speculate** that stable vimentin filaments may help PGCCs resist axial strain and align their multinucleated structure along the migration axis, and that vimentin could coordinate actin and microtubule networks through cross-linkers such as **plectin**. They also propose that vimentin's protective role could become still more important during migration through confined **3D** environments; that is not experimentally tested in this paper. ([ResearchGate][2])

Finally, the therapeutic suggestion—targeting vimentin or vimentin cross-linking proteins to suppress PGCC dissemination—is explicitly a **proposed implication**, not an experimentally tested therapeutic result. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][1])
