Figure 2 revision: signature assays
Oct 20, 2026 · @Richard 
Figure 2 should show that measurable challenge responses distinguish the mechanisms behind size-dependent replacement resistance. Neither the 19 October plate nor this morning's signature matrix does this for Wave-pinning. Two pilots on 20 October suggest that an equal-area width series and a cue train do. This note sets out the problems, the pilot results and a proposed figure for the HPC campaign.
Conceptual issues
Both current versions answer a cue-design or model-labelling question, not the mechanism question in the success criterion.
19 October consolidated plate
• Panel D compares broad and narrow cues at equal peak. Broader cues therefore also deliver more integrated input, so width and dose are confounded.
• The readout, 50% loss of incumbent excess, is not replacement. Its ratio trends distinguish named models, not explanations.
• The mechanism × observable matrix is absent from the main figure. Panels E–G are one deBelly example that disclaims cause.
• The width/strength/duration grid ran as a near-factorial survey (3,492 protocols). Duration entered only as withdrawal time, and no designed contrast of shape or timing was extracted.
20 October signature matrix
• Goryachev initiation and deBelly dwell-gated commitment are sound, positive signatures.
• Wave-pinning is identified only by exclusion. A flat activation time and "length, not separation" are both shared with deBelly.
• The one Wave-pinning-specific feature, the post-withdrawal stall, is weak:
    ◦ It is a finite-horizon observation; D still drifts up to 0.03 over the last 7,500 units.
    ◦ It requires withdrawal at an exact share.
    ◦ Noise would likely resolve it.
    ◦ Slow two-peak coarsening is generic to mass-conserved, saturating reaction–diffusion models.
• The "shared-pool competition" label is unsupported. The length series changes pool size, incumbent extent and separation together.

Wave-pinning is strongly penalised by concentrating the same input, and the penalty grows steeply with size. This fits saturating feedback: challenger strength follows the width that reaches the plateau, not the dose delivered. Goryachev and deBelly count area and are nearly width-neutral. That contradicts the expectation that deBelly would also saturate, so this assay separates Wave-pinning from deBelly directly.
Cue train at fixed total ON time. Four equal pulses were given, with total ON = 1.5× the continuous duration that commits at that length. Gaps were 0.1, 1 or 4× the pulse length, then the cell ran cue-free for 10⁴ units (4 × 10³ s for deBelly).

Wave-pinning acts as a ratchet. The challenger share holds through each gap from the first pulse, so the outcome depends only on total ON time. Goryachev resets in every gap. deBelly's activity collapses in early gaps yet later pulses still commit, so something other than activity accumulates. The Wave-pinning–deBelly contrast is therefore visible exactly where a biosensor reads: activity share at the end of each gap.

Open questions
• Does Holmes 3 share Wave-pinning's narrow-cue penalty and ratchet? If so, the signature identifies a class of mass-conserved, saturating models, not Wave-pinning alone.
• Is deBelly's mild narrow-cue preference at L39 (0.77) robust to the challenger-zone definition?
• Does Wave-pinning's gap hold survive weak noise over gaps of 16× the pulse length?
• Is an equal-area comparison realistic optically? Beam width at fixed photon dose needs a native-to-optical calibration, which the repo does not have.
• What should define commitment for total ON time: the shortest committing continuous duration, or a share threshold? The two differ for deBelly.
• The pilots used three lengths, one area, one pulse count and gaps of up to 4×. Every number above is provisional until the campaign completes.
