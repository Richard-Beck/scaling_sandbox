# Brief: Figures 2–4 revision, round 2

## 0. Shared narrative and rules

**Thread across the three figures**
1. Enlarged cells resist imposed replacement through class-specific mechanisms (Fig. 2).
2. Larger cells can hold active regions farther apart, and in mass-conserved models, far-apart regions resolve very slowly (Fig. 3).
3. Under fluctuating input, large cells gain heading persistence. Where multiple fronts coexist, that gain falls below the geometric expectation (Fig. 4, pending the reanalysis in section 1).

Each figure's title and the first sentence of its legend state that figure's part of this thread.

**Presentation rules for all three figures**
- **Titles** state the message. Remove "AUDITED FINAL", hashes, slot IDs, seeds and atlas references from main figures; they belong in metadata.

- **Colours:** fixed model colours across all figures (WP blue, Goryachev red, deBelly purple, Holmes green, Spring gold). Size is encoded by lightness (small light, large dark) or line style, the same in every figure.
- **Panel count:** at most six per figure. Robustness, sensitivity and numerical panels go to the supplement.
- **Bounds:** censored values use one convention everywhere (open triangle at the bound, with an arrow). They are never omitted.
- **No retuning of models.** Existing supplements stay complete. Superseded main panels move to the supplement rather than being deleted.

**Order**

## 1. Figure 4: reanalysis and recomposition

### 1.1 New persistence estimand (existing outputs only, no new runs)

The 1/e persistence time τ_p is censored wherever memory stays near 1, which is exactly where the size effect was clearest. Replace it as the primary estimand.

- **Heading autocorrelation:** C(ℓ) = ⟨cos Δθ(ℓ)⟩, keeping the existing coverage rule (both endpoints R ≥ 0.1) and the whole-seed bootstrap.
- **Rotational decay rate at lag ℓ:** k(ℓ) = −ln C(ℓ) / ℓ. For rotational diffusion, this is the angular diffusivity D_θ.
- **Lag set:** chemical models 250, 500, 1000, 2000, 4000 and 8000; Spring at one tenth of those.
- **Usable lag:** coverage ≥ 0.5 in both sizes, and the bootstrap CI of 1 − C excludes 0 in at least one size.
- **Primary size ratio:**
  - Ρ_k(ℓ) = k_small(ℓ) / k_large(ℓ), at the shortest usable lag where C_small ≤ 0.98.
  - As a secondary estimate, regress −ln C on ℓ over lags where 0.2 ≤ C ≤ 0.98, separately for each size, and report the ratio of slopes.
- **Censoring:** if the large cell's 1 − C is indistinguishable from 0 at every usable lag, report a one-sided lower bound using the CI upper limit of k_large.
- **Lag consistency:** report Ρ_k at every usable lag. A ratio that drifts by more than 1.5× across lags marks the condition "non-exponential". Keep it, flagged.
- **Geometric normalization:** Ρ_geo = Ρ_k / (C_large / C_small)².
  - Ρ_geo = 1: pure geometry with size-independent physical mobility.
  - Ρ_geo > 1: site stabilization beyond geometry.
  - Ρ_geo < 1: erosion of the geometric advantage.
  - Label this as relative to the single-front geometric expectation. It is not a site diffusivity.
- **Noise tolerance f½(C):** the input where C at a fixed reference lag (chemical 2000, Spring 200) falls to 0.5, interpolated in log f across all designed inputs, including 0.005, 0.015, 0.02 and 0.05. Report CIs and bounds.

## 2. Figure 3: recomposition (no required new runs)

**Message:** distance matters most for resolving coexisting regions, not for driven takeover. At matched distance, size dominates takeover time. Larger cells reach larger separations, where coexisting regions in mass-conserved models persist.

### 2.1 New analysis: decompose the size effect

For each model, for driven handover (A1) and for cue-free resolution (B, ratio 1):

- **Matched gap g_m:** the largest gap reached at both the smallest and largest sizes. This is normally the smallest size's antipode; interpolate within each size's measured curve.
- **Size at matched gap:** T_large(g_m) / T_small(g_m).
- **Extra separation:** T_large(antipode_large) / T_large(g_m).
- **Total:** T_large(antipode_large) / T_small(antipode_small). This equals the product of the two terms above.
- Repeat for the middle size against the smallest.
- Use the same lower-bound convention throughout. Where T is censored, the corresponding term is a one-sided bound. Never substitute the horizon cap.

### 2.2 Figure 3 main figure

- **Title:** "Separation slows the resolution of coexisting regions more than it slows driven takeover".
- **A. Design schematic.** Keep the current schematic, compressed. Add one small inset: the same gap on small and large rings. This replaces the translation page's geometry content.
- **B. Native kymographs, two contrasts:**
  - B1: Goryachev driven handover at g = 2 against g = 8 (distance slows takeover).
  - B2: Wave-pinning cue-free resolution, near against far withdrawal gap at one size (far regions coexist).
- **C. Driven handover against initial edge gap.** The current C1, one subpanel per model, with borderline placements open and the antipode of each size marked by a tick.
- **D. Cue-free resolution against withdrawal gap.** The current C2 promoted; ratio 1 in the main figure.
  - Lower bounds drawn clearly. Holmes is mostly bounds; show them, and state the fraction in the legend.
  - Mark each size's antipode.
- **E. Decomposition** (section 2.1). Per model: horizontal bars of log₁₀ time ratio, split into size-at-matched-gap and extra-separation, for A1 and B side by side. Bounds are drawn as open-ended bars.

**Legend points to state:**
- Goryachev handover rises with gap. Holmes is flat. Wave-pinning and Spring rise near the edge and then plateau. Do not describe Wave-pinning as having no distance effect.
- Resolution in Wave-pinning, Holmes and Spring rises steeply with gap and is often unresolved at large sizes.
- Size at matched gap dominates handover.

## 3. Figure 2: recomposition plus targeted Holmes runs

**Message:** replacement resistance in enlarged cells arises through three mechanism classes, and the shape and timing of a cue tell them apart.

| Class | Models | Initiation slows with L | Narrow-cue penalty at equal area | Share held through gaps | Commits after in-gap collapse |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Initiation-limited | Goryachev | yes | no | no (resets) | no |
| Mass-conserved, saturating | Wave-pinning, Holmes | no | yes, grows with L | yes (ratchet) | — |
| Slow non-activity state | deBelly, Spring | no | no | no (collapses) | yes |

Verify every cell of this table against the final data before it goes into the figure. Change the class membership if any model departs, and say so.

### 3.1 New runs: Holmes at large L

- Holmes L31, L35 and L39 have no calibration, because no continuous cue commits by 10⁵.
- Run 4-pulse trains with pulse length equal to one quarter of 1.5× the Holmes L27 calibration duration, at gaps 0, 0.1, 0.5, 1, 4 and 16× the pulse length.
- **Primary readout:** D at the end of each pulse and each gap; this measures retention and does not need commitment. Fate is secondary.
- Add one bounded-noise retention check at L39, matching S3.
- Report "no continuous commitment by 10⁵" as a finding: extreme replacement resistance at large L.
