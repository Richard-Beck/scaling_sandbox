# Proposed program of work: Figures 2–4

|   | Figure 2 — Mechanisms | Figure 3 — Geometry | Figure 4 — Stochastic dynamics |
|------------------|------------------|------------------|------------------|
| **Central question** | What mechanisms make established polarity difficult to replace? | How does cell geometry alter spatial competition and the directional consequences of polarity organization? | How do size and geometry influence spontaneous polarity dynamics and directional memory? |
| **Existing evidence to preserve** | Incumbent organization (width, concentration, amount); local activation gain; incumbent recovery; cue-driven competition; selected causal interventions across five models | Ring geometry and illustrative site configurations; placement-dependent outcomes; Holmes merger/coexistence examples | Representative stochastic field histories; angular-memory curves; physical site displacement; polarity magnitude and observation coverage |
| **Main new work** | Identify mechanistically discriminating observational signatures using existing deterministic results | Quantify physical versus angular separation; evaluate whether ring competition results establish additional insights beyond the linear models | Characterize spontaneous polarity dynamics; quantify geometric contributions to angular memory; extract passive signatures supported by actual trajectories |
| **Illustrative panels** | Selected polarity profiles and time courses showing distinct mechanisms or failure modes of reversal | Small/large rings showing identical physical separation but different outward-normal angles; selected placement-dependent competition histories | Representative stochastic space–time histories showing observed modes of polarity evolution |
| **Quantitative panels** | Challenger activation; incumbent recovery; challenger survival/replacement; selected mechanistic intervention contrasts | Physical versus angular site separation; competition outcomes versus site placement; potentially physical displacement versus angular change | Directional correlations; physical site displacement; angular-increment distributions; other empirically supported passive signatures |
| **Key analyses** | Construct mechanism × observable matrix; distinguish passive from perturbation-dependent signatures; test whether candidate signatures discriminate mechanisms across sizes and stimulus conditions | Test whether physical separation, angular separation, or other spatial descriptors predict outcomes; evaluate whether geometric and competition results form a coherent figure | Examine continuous wandering versus abrupt reorientation; inspect multiple-region configurations without assuming functional competition; compare physical and angular reorientation statistics |
| **Success criterion** | Show that measurable responses distinguish competing explanations for size-dependent reversal resistance, rather than merely distinguishing named models | Establish a useful consequence of geometry that cannot be demonstrated in linear models | Explain differences in polarity-memory curves and identify one or two genuinely discriminating passive signatures |
| **Potential scope reduction** | Move exhaustive model-by-model documentation and secondary sweeps to supplement | If spatial competition and directional geometry do not form a coherent argument, move competition results toward Fig. 2 and use geometry as a bridge to Fig. 4 | Prioritize validated observables over speculative event classifications; move extensive sensitivity and eligibility details to supplement |

## Guiding principles

-   Preserve strong existing results and illustrative panels rather than rebuilding the figures unnecessarily.
-   Prioritize passive observational signatures, while retaining perturbation-dependent signatures where informative.
-   Treat the division between Figures 2-4 as provisional until analyses are complete.
-   Prefer reanalysis of existing simulations; commission targeted new simulations only when necessary to answer a specific question.
-   Keep Figures 1 and 5 largely unchanged for now.
-   Remove encyclopedic model descriptions and excessive qualifications from the main narrative.
-   Identify and focus on a small number of claims - these claims are the north star for the figure.
