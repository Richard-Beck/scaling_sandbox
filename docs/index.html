How does cell enlargement change transport, signalling, and polarity?

A reproducible collection of controlled size-scaling experiments. Each report identifies the source model, the modification made here, the resulting behaviour, and the boundary between published evidence and new computation.

Scientific premise

Doubling volume does not merely make a cell “more of the same.” For similar shapes, length grows as V1/3 and surface area as V2/3. Sources, sinks, molecular pools, membrane access, and transport distances can therefore scale differently. These reports hold selected biochemical quantities fixed while changing geometry or pool scaling so the responsible mechanism remains auditable.

How to read the catalogue. “Reproduction” reconstructs a published formulation; “ported” transfers a released model; “extension” marks an experiment or assumption added here; “exploratory” marks a generic mechanism rather than a paper reproduction.

Eroumé–Marée Rac/Cdc42/Rho polarity

Model. Exact nine-PDE reaction system parsed from the Eroumé et al. VCell model, descended from Marée et al.; fixed shape omits the parent cytoskeletal module.

Changed here. Two teardrop aspect ratios at matched 0.5×, 1×, and 2× implied volumes; concentrations, kinetics, diffusion, and relative stimulus position are fixed.

Main result. Larger cells polarize later, and both tested 2× geometries retain two late Cdc42 peaks rather than meeting the predefined stable unique-state criterion.

Single-cell oxygen transport

Model. Sedlack-inspired 3D oxygen transport with medium, flattened adherent cell, membrane, and Michaelis–Menten consumption.

Changed here. Reconstructed mitochondrial geometry is replaced by a calibrated uniform sink; 1×/2× scaling is new.

Main result. Enlargement matters little when oxygen is replete but exposes stronger gradients and limitation in hypoxic/high-demand regimes.

Synthesis

Across these models, size acts through longer diffusion distance, declining membrane-area:volume, competition between area-localized targets and volume-distributed sinks, nonlinear spatial-state selection, dilution, and increased path/junction complexity. The shared conclusion is not one universal enlargement phenotype: the scaling assigned to each source, sink, pool, and geometry determines which constraint appears.

The strongest current mechanistic result is the Eroumé-derived port: under tightly controlled biochemical assumptions, the 2× geometries select persistent multi-peak Cdc42 states. The main unresolved extension is mechanics. De Belly et al. provides a distinct long-range mechanochemical mechanism; it is documented as a candidate, not blended into the biochemical port.

Source, scenarios, build instructions, and report snapshots are in the GitHub repository. Plot-level CSVs are the data frames used by the reports.
