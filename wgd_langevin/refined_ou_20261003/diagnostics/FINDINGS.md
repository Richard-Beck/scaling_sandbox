# Final refined-fit movie findings

The representative movie is only partly consistent with stationary Gaussian OU fluctuations. Its seven accepted smaller-nucleus tracks contain prolonged low-motion episodes and longer step-vector correlations than the corresponding fitted population model. The apparent giant-nucleus path is broadly compatible with that model's finite-trajectory correlation range, but the inference is weak: it is one visually classified nucleus, its centroid uncertainty matters, and movie timing remains unresolved. These checks do not justify a new mechanism or a population refit.

Under the displayed **conditional 10-minute cadence and assumed 3-pixel per-coordinate Gaussian centroid sensitivity**, the giant nucleus averages 2.292 µm per frame, compared with the WGD+ model's 2.875 µm. Its whitened residual RMS is 0.889 (unit RMS expected), and its one-frame vector correlation is 0.415 versus model expectation 0.381; the simulated individual-track 2.5–97.5% correlation range is 0.150–0.578. Its longest model-defined slow episode is four intervals, within the simulated range of two to six.

The smaller nuclei average 1.346–4.325 µm per frame, compared with the WGD− model's 4.292 µm. Six of the seven are slower than the model expectation, but their spread includes both quiet and more active tracks. Their mean one-frame vector correlation is 0.319 versus model expectation 0.180 and the matched seven-track simulated range 0.098–0.237. Several longer lags also exceed the fixed-parameter simulation envelope. Six of seven have longest slow episodes longer than the simulated upper 97.5% point of five intervals: lengths are 7, 21, 9, 19, 18 and 50. The seventh has five. These model-quartile slow episodes are not the publication's experimental slow-mode classification.

The standardized prediction residuals do not uniformly show the unit Gaussian fluctuations expected by the assumed observation model. With the 3-pixel sensitivity and 10-minute scenario their RMS ranges from 0.343 to 1.091 across all eight accepted tracks, and residual lag-one correlations remain appreciable in some smaller-nucleus tracks. Without assumed localization noise, or with different physical timing, their standardized magnitudes change substantially; across the six timing/localization sensitivity combinations the per-track RMS ranges span 0.287–2.140. This sensitivity is especially relevant to the giant nucleus, whose centroid deformation can mimic or mask velocity fluctuations. The 12-hour and 16-hour scenarios and zero-localization comparisons are exported for every track, not silently collapsed into one acquisition-time result.

Movie-to-population differences can reflect representative-field selection, biological heterogeneity, nuclear deformation, structured measurement error, acquisition timing or specimen drift. No fixed specimen landmarks support drift estimation; no drift was removed. The approximately 1.8% scale reading sensitivity is much smaller than the observed track heterogeneity, but the three-pixel position allowance was not experimentally calibrated. The finite-trajectory shaded ranges are model predictive ranges, not uncertainty intervals for model parameters or biological group differences. Steps and lags are correlated, and the one giant nucleus and seven smaller nuclei are not established WGD experimental cohorts. Accordingly, the movie reveals diagnostic departures from a uniform stationary OU description without demonstrating the source of those departures, estimating new OU times or replacing the published population MSD and speed targets.

Values above correspond to the final selected primary refined fit. `movie_provenance.json` records its exact content hash and the diagnostics generation time; `movie_track_checks.csv` and `movie_correlations.csv` provide the full numerical evidence.
