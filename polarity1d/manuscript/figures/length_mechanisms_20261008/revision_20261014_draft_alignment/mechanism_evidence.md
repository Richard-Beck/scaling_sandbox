# Endpoint MCA evidence for the user-aligned revision

Generated 14 October 2026 from retained corrected independent-endpoint trajectories. No new integrations, perturbations, selections, or native data changes. All comparisons below use the native peak-3.6 right countercue with support 20% of nominal length, started from each exact qualifying basal checkpoint. Endpoints mean the first and last finite-volume cell centres, x=L/402 and x=L−L/402. All MCA values are native concentrations unless amount units are stated explicitly.

## Main scientific conclusion

The proposed incumbent-refill narrative is supported as a **qualified account of the intermediate-length increase**, and is more directly tied to local Rac suppression than the cortical midpoint or whole-pool centroid. Longer cells from 23 to 37 µm begin with more depleted old-end bound MCA and stronger outward old-end protrusion. Over the common first 180 s, 23–35-µm cells accumulate similar amounts at the old endpoint, so the longer cells remain more depleted in absolute concentration. Old-end Rac also falls later as length increases. However, the accumulation is not length invariant at every time: initial refill is slower at longer lengths, followed by a later acceleration at intermediate lengths. At 37 and especially 39 µm, slower net refill becomes essential. At 39 µm, basal old-end MCA is slightly higher than at 37 µm, so greater initial depletion cannot explain its sharp delay by itself.

A common MCA concentration that terminates the incumbent is **not** supported. The MCA feedback midpoint 0.068 is a smooth activation-law parameter, not a measured reversal threshold. Selected successful cues at every length from 23 through 39 µm withdraw while old-end MCA is still below 0.068. The bound-MCA concentration at a descriptive 10% Rac decline also changes systematically with length. Endpoint MCA should therefore occupy centre stage as a local variable connected to incumbent weakening, while the text distinguishes that relationship from an exact sufficient threshold or a complete causal explanation.

For 23→31 µm, basal old-end MCA decreases 9.82%, whereas the old-end outward command rises 2.65-fold. The first-120-s accumulated MCA increases only 2.86%; the first-180-s amount increases 10.75%. Despite this slightly greater refill, absolute old-end MCA at 180 s decreases 6.31%. The native MCA activation factor consequently remains larger in the longer cell. The descriptive 10% old-Rac decline is delayed from 123.49 to 142.97 s (+15.8%), while the selected finite-cue duration rises 22.8%. These clocks are different observables and should not be equated.

Across 23–35 µm, the first-180-s net old-end rate lies between 6.125e-5 and 6.870e-5 concentration/s (coefficient of variation 4.4%). This is a legitimate narrow-window description of similar overall accumulation. In contrast, the first-30-s rates fall from 7.784e-5 at 23 µm to 3.543e-5 at 35 µm, and the first-60-s rates fall from 7.243e-5 to 3.719e-5. From 60 to 180 s the pattern reverses: the old-end rate rises from 5.566e-5 at 23 µm to 7.320e-5 at 31 µm. Thus “old-end accumulation is length invariant” loses the time structure needed to understand the mechanism.

At 39 µm the old endpoint gains only 0.004005 by 180 s, 67.2% less than at 31 µm. Old Rac changes from 0.902205 at cue onset to 0.902212 at 180 s: no early incumbent decline accompanies substantial new-end MCA depletion. Its first 10% Rac decline occurs at 637.39 s. This agrees with the retained old-end outflux evidence: during 60–180 s, 81.24% of bound-MCA boundary loss occurs through the old end at 39 µm, compared with 0.00958% at 31 µm and 3.92% at 37 µm. The new front does not immediately remove the old protrusive state.

The full length-response curve remains nonmonotone. The low-length selected cue decreases 472.17→225.93 s from 18→21 µm, and it also decreases 285.28→275.18 s from 31→33 µm. In contrast, the descriptive 10% old-Rac-decline clock increases across every sampled length. The incumbent clock is therefore useful evidence of slower local suppression, but cannot by itself explain all finite-pulse outcomes or the entire response curve.

## Required-change and threshold questions

The full basal endpoint contrast increases from 0.045455 at 23 µm to 0.051510 at 31 µm and 0.054769 at 37 µm. Actual basal-to-final old-end MCA increases after the 10000-s recovery are 0.045487, 0.051442 and 0.054734, respectively. Thus **complete local repolarization** entails a larger MCA concentration change across those lengths. The 39-µm value is lower, 0.052165. These final amounts include recovery after cue withdrawal; they are not MCA amounts that had to accumulate while the cue remained on. It would be misleading to divide them by a common cue rate and identify the result with the minimum finite cue.

At selected withdrawal the old-end MCA concentrations are 0.066818 at 23 µm, 0.065277 at 31 µm, 0.063034 at 37 µm and 0.059333 at 39 µm. Their cue-on increments are 0.013335, 0.017045, 0.017153 and 0.012678. Neither the final concentration nor the increment is a length-independent cutoff.

For an explicitly illustrative common concentration, old-end MCA first reaches 0.060 at 94.43, 172.43, 218.37 and 291.10 s at 23, 31, 35 and 37 µm. It does not reach 0.060 during the selected 809.08-s cue at 39 µm. This may be shown as an optional descriptive clock, but the value is chosen by the analyst and is not a biological or assay threshold.

At a 10% old-Rac decline, old-end MCA is 0.061661 at 23 µm, 0.058114 at 31 µm, 0.056721 at 35 µm and 0.056009 at 39 µm. Different local Rho, diffusion and mechanical states remain relevant; the native Rac equation must accompany the interpretation. A 20% decline often occurs only after the selected cue is withdrawn, so a cue-only 20% clock is unavailable in most lengths. Missing crossings here are within-cue observations, not failed reversals.

Over 23–31 µm, common first-180-s outbound amount rises 57.4% for a 34.8% length increase. A straight line with free intercept fits that restricted interval closely (R-squared 0.985), but the amount/L ratio increases 16.8%, so exact proportionality is false. More importantly, first-30-s throughput **decreases** with length over the same interval, and first-180-s throughput decreases above 33 µm. Whole-own-cue amount increases toward 39 µm partly because cue durations differ. Therefore the report should say “outbound throughput increases over the intermediate lengths during the established response” and name the common window. A proportional-to-length law is not justified.

The native uniform source does have an exact inverse-length conversion: spatially integrated amount replacement divided by nominal length gives concentration replacement. However, it compensates the deficit in **bound plus unbound MCA** after the sequential native pool updates, including boundary transport and the sequential-exchange residual. It is not equal to bound endpoint outflux/L alone, and 78.125% of the total correction is allocated to the bound pool. First-180-s total amount replacement is 0.003690 at 23 µm and 0.005832 concentration×µm/s at 31 µm, so the bound concentration source still rises 17.3% over that interval. The exact division by length does not establish constant concentration replacement or constant old-end net accumulation.

## Figure 9 snapshot sign and magnitude checks

Independent readout of the accepted 31-µm spatial CSV confirms the user's explanation with narrow corrections:

- Basal cortical velocity is leftward in all 201 native cells, while MCA increases toward the right. Profile advection supplies 200 cells and is exactly zero in the last boundary-adjacent cell under the native upwind donor rule. It is appropriate to say it supplies the interior; “positive throughout” needs the endpoint qualification.
- At 30 s cortical velocity is rightward in 179/201 cells (89.1%), with 22 cells near the old end still moving left. Profile advection removes MCA in 159/201 cells (79.1%), while end-region donor effects and local profile changes yield positive supply in 41 cells. The profile is largely retained, rather than exactly unchanged.
- At 180 s all 201 cells have rightward cortical velocity. Profile advection remains negative in 148 cells and supplies 52 cells near the challenged end, where it opposes depletion. In the outer old 20%, its negative mean grows from −2.20e-6 at 30 s to −7.56e-6 concentration/s at 180 s; in the outer new 20%, its mean changes from −6.31e-7 to +2.20e-4 concentration/s.
- Flow-gradient dilution is negative in every cell at all three displayed snapshots. Its spatial RMS is 2.06e-4 at 30 s and 3.60e-4 at 180 s, the largest displayed contribution. Net binding and uniform replacement remain substantial (roughly 0.38–0.56 of gradient RMS). Bound diffusion RMS is only 0.47% of gradient RMS at 30 s and 0.16% at 180 s.
- Endpoint-forcing flow is the largest RMS mechanical allocation at each snapshot. At 180 s its RMS is 0.06158 µm/s, compared with 0.01491 for Rho-gradient forcing and 0.000155 for membrane forcing; Rho opposes the mean rightward flow then. At 30 s, endpoint-forcing RMS is 0.03135, Rho 0.004318 and membrane 0.001738 µm/s. The major point is supported; the Rho share is time dependent.

## Recommended close-to-draft narrative after Figure 9

“Taken together, these contributions show how the newly driven end can redistribute MCA in the direction required for reorientation. Outward cortical flow dilutes bound MCA at the new pole, whilst net binding and the spatially uniform replacement distribute MCA back through the cell. At the old pole, the latter contributions can exceed the continued loss caused by cortical stretching, enabling bound MCA to increase and thereby weakening Rac activation. Profile advection partly opposes both changes. Redistribution therefore depends on the balance of outward transport and replenishment, rather than outward transport alone.”

“This balance also suggests a local explanation for part of the length dependence. The basal old pole is more strongly depleted of bound MCA in the longer intermediate cells, and its outward protrusive command is greater. During the common first 180 s of countercue, old-end MCA increases by similar amounts from 23 to 35 µm, leaving its absolute concentration lower in the longer cells. Old-end Rac correspondingly declines later. Thus greater initial depletion can contribute to a longer period of incumbent persistence, even when the overall amount of early MCA accumulation changes comparatively little with length.”

“The longest cells add a further difficulty. At 37 and 39 µm, old-end MCA accumulation itself becomes slower; at 39 µm, old-end Rac is still effectively unchanged after 180 s, and most bound-MCA boundary loss during 60–180 s remains at the old pole. The initial depletion is slightly less severe than at 37 µm, so the particularly long response cannot be attributed to a larger initial MCA deficit alone. The persistence of old-pole activity and the slower change in its local MCA balance must also be considered.”

“There is no single endpoint MCA concentration that marks successful reorientation in these trajectories. The cue can be withdrawn before old-end MCA reaches the midpoint of the smooth MCA feedback law, and MCA redistribution continues during recovery. The observations therefore link length-dependent starting states and local MCA accumulation to incumbent weakening, while leaving the precise finite-cue requirement to the coupled Rac, Rho and mechanical dynamics.”

This is a proposed continuation, not authority to replace the user's draft wording around Figures 8–9. Preserve that wording where scientifically correct. Place the full nonmonotone cue curves alongside the local endpoint evidence and state their low-length exception once, before concentrating on 23–35 µm. A substantial but short final paragraph can reserve 39 µm without retaining cortical-midpoint arguments as the main mechanism.
