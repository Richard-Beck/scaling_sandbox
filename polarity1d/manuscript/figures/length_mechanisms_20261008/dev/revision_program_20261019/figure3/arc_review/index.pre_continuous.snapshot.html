Actual cue-on trajectories and rates

The broad size pattern is less target-relative reorientation during a given pulse in larger native rings. Farther placements often produce greater target-relative response in Wave-pinning, Holmes and Spring over the tested intermediate and longer pulses. Neither ordering is universal. Raw angular rate and the fraction of the requested turn per native unit are different observables.

Background update: the initial rate is largely a vector-geometry effect. The exact finite-width native cue contribution has now been measured at all six illustrated initial states. Wave-pinning's farther/nearer initial angular-rate ratio is 5.153; its sine-lever ratio is 5.126, with only a 0.53% difference in initial activated-amount rate. Goryachev's ratio is 1.414218, almost identical to its 1.414213 sine-lever ratio. These initial global-rate differences are therefore not, by themselves, evidence for slower incumbent remodeling.

For the Holmes size contrast, the larger/smaller initial-rate ratio is 0.348. It includes the inverse initial-resultant factor 0.487 and sine-lever factor 0.707, with nearly equal initial activation amount. Continued cue-on trajectories and local native profiles reveal the later state evolution; an initial angular rate alone cannot distinguish it.

Angular rate and target-relative rate are different

Goryachev, C = 40 and a 300-unit cue: d = 5 and d = 10 reach 29.59° and 33.48° during the cue. The farther condition has a larger absolute angular secant (0.1116 versus 0.0986 degrees per native unit), but a smaller target-normalized secant (0.001240 versus 0.002192 per native unit). Its larger angular target and weaker vector matter. The different post-cue outcomes are secondary context, not rate measurements.

Optional retained-outcome question — role in the main figure remains open

All 240 fixed-arc retained profiles are single-region states, including the newly tested 120° chemical placements. Some are translated only partway toward the target. These results establish finite retention and cue-free relaxation; they do not measure the cue-on angular rate. The selected Goryachev d = 10 case returns toward its original direction after withdrawal despite a substantial cue-on turn. No asymptotic attractor or site-ancestry claim follows from that endpoint.

What the activity vector measures

Each local active amount contributes along its outward ring normal. The physical first moment is a complex resultant; its phase gives activity orientation and its magnitude reports reinforcement or cancellation. Dividing by the initial complex moment rotates the reference axes once and sets P(initial) = 1. It does not impose a cell velocity law.

M1(t) = C(t) meanq[u(q,t)e2πiq],   P(t) = M1(t)M1(0),   R(t) = |P(t)| (1)
ω(t) = Im[M̅1(t) Ṁ1(t)]|M1(t)|² (2)

The illustrated instantaneous rates use the unchanged native right-hand side at the saved full states. Weak R < 0.1 masks heading and rate independently; a phase is not differentiated across a weak interval. The horizontal clock is compressed above one native unit, but the rate is dθ/dt in native time, not the slope with respect to the displayed logarithmic coordinate. Angular rate concerns the resultant, rather than a tracked local peak, molecular travel or cell movement.

Candidate panel possibilities — proposals, not settled layout

One experiment and its vector

Introduce the fixed physical cue, the larger incumbent and outward-normal summation. Show the same d at two sizes so the changing target angle and changing overlap are visible.

Cue-on response across the experiment

Foreground measured direction and angular-rate trajectories. Keep absolute angular rate distinct from target-relative rate. A compact complete-grid summary could accompany selected native histories.

Local organization explains the resultant

Use the Holmes size or Wave-pinning placement contrast to show local reinforcement versus activity outside the original core. Couple the profiles to θ, ω and R rather than assembling unrelated assays.

An informative exception

Goryachev separates absolute angular change, fraction of target turn, vector magnitude and cue-free retention. It can prevent an overly general “farther is faster” mechanism claim.

Open scientific choice: whether sustained post-cue outcomes add a necessary main-text claim or belong in supporting material. The cue-on rate illustrations stand independently of that decision.

Evidence and practical limits

The current artifacts preserve native basals, kinetics, cue channel, peak, physical support and sampled fields. Size comparisons also change native material and basal organization. Distance and normal angle are linked within each fixed circumference; these data do not isolate a causal geometry contribution. Prescribed input-coefficient exposure is distinct from activated biochemical amount.

Complete fixed-arc readouts and the paired graphic passed independent native/source/display checks. All 1,450 illustrated cue-on rates agree with an independent first-harmonic reaction projection to within 7.1 × 10−15; initial factors and ratios agree to within 2.84 × 10−14. The selected histories are non-antipodal and have no weak-vector interval. All report images are real exports from accepted data, not placeholders.
