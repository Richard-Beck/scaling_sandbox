# Two-state activity-vector teaching example

Holmes 3, fixed circumference 40, antipodal stationary native cue of duration 1000, followed by 13000 cue-free native units. Both rings show exact saved active-concentration profiles on one common zero-based native intensity scale. The circle represents the model ring, not a measured cell outline. Each coloured arc is one native cell; its physical segment length is 40/800=0.05. Activity is a real scalar. Multiplying it by segment length and the outward unit normal gives real x and y contributions, written together as a complex number x+iy. Their sum is the first physical activity moment M₁; the scalar total activity is M₀. Complex division by the initial M₁ rotates and scales the initial resultant to P(0)=1. It is not division by current total activity.

Outer blue arrows sum the exact native complex contributions in twelve 30° sectors after that initial normalization. Bin populations need not be equal; native cell weights and directions are retained. Arrows are translated to their sector positions for illustration. The red arrow is their vector sum drawn at the origin. Blue and red arrows have one shared vector scale, identical in both states. The final short red arrow is the measured weak resultant; its heading is not interpreted because R<0.1. Initial and final physical total active amounts are 33.1293111 and 33.0450821; their ratio is 0.9974576, whereas final R is 0.0399348. Thus nearly retained total activity can accompany nearly cancelling activity directions. M₀ is total active amount, not the conserved active-plus-inactive pool. No velocity, cell displacement, site ancestry or asymptotic-stability claim is assigned.
