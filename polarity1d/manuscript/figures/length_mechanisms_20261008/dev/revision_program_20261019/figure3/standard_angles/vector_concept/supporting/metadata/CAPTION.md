# Stationary-cue concept caption

Stationary-cue complex-vector concept sketch from four selected conditions in the completed 80-condition experiment. A1–A4 show actual native active-concentration profiles at the initial time, the sampled minimum R, cue withdrawal and the final 13,000-unit cue-free endpoint. The minimum-R profile is taken from the exact same stored native frame as its moment. Shading marks the initial cue footprint. B1–B4 show measured P=M₁(t)/M₁(0) in the complex plane, with orange cue-on and blue post-withdrawal paths; circle/square/diamond mark initial/withdrawal/final values. Dividing by the initial complex M₁ rotates the initial direction to the positive real axis and scales by its initial magnitude; it does not divide by the current total active amount. Thus R can exceed 1, as during the Spring cue, or become small through cancellation of opposed contributions while M₀ remains substantial, as in Holmes. All planes have the same equal real/imaginary scales, a unit-circle reference and an R<.1 disk where heading is not interpreted. C1–C4 retain full native-time traces of R and physical total active amount M₀/M₀(0), with explicitly labelled logarithmic compression beyond the early linear interval. Physical M₁ and M₀ use C(t)/N quadrature, including Spring's changing circumference. Native concentrations, input peaks, widths and clocks differ across models. This review sheet does not replace existing Figure 3 art or characterize moving cues. Spring's sampled minimum R differs from 1 only at numerical roundoff, so that snapshot is bookkeeping rather than a weak-resultant episode. For its 90° input, 35.6% of the initial cue support overlaps the original half-contrast core; that case does not begin with two separate activity sites.
