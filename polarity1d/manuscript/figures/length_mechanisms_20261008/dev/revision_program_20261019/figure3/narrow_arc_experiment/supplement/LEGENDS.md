## Shared definitions and protocol

For material position q, physical moments are

$$
M_n(t)=C(t)\left\langle u(q,t)e^{2\pi i n q}\right\rangle,
\qquad P(t)=\frac{M_1(t)}{M_1(0)},
\qquad R(t)=|P(t)|. \tag{S3.1}
$$

Raw M1 components use fixed ring reference axes. Complex normalization rotates the P coordinate axes once by the initial M1 phase and sets P(0) to 1. Thus the plotted target includes the measured initial moment offset from the declared preparation axis. For heading φ and actual target θ*, the branch-safe error removal is

$$
1-\frac{|\operatorname{wrap}(\phi-\theta_*)|}
{|\operatorname{wrap}(-\theta_*)|}. \tag{S3.2}
$$

This is an angular endpoint readout, not trajectory speed. Native instantaneous phase rates, where actually measured, use

$$
\omega_{\rm deg}(t)=\frac{180}{\pi}\,
\frac{\operatorname{Im}\!\left[\overline{M_1(t)}\,\dot M_1(t)\right]}{|M_1(t)|^2}. \tag{S3.3}
$$

The finite-width cue contribution, its angular projection and the initial incumbent moment can all affect initial global angular rate. A faster initial resultant rotation does not alone establish faster incumbent remodeling. Later native profiles, magnitude and cue-on rate histories provide distinct context.

Chemical cue durations are 1, 10, 100, 300 and 1,000; Spring durations are 0.1, 1, 10, 100 and 1,000. The full physical raised-cosine supports remain 3 in chemical models and 0.2 in Spring. Peak input coefficients are 0.1, 6.4, 1 and 4 in Wave-pinning, Goryachev, Holmes and Spring, respectively. Equal prescribed input coefficients or coefficient integrals do not imply equal activated biochemical amount because native local substrate varies. Native kinetics, solver, cue channel and exact qualified starting states are unchanged. Spring retains its validated mean-force global-extension closure: the cue centre is fixed in material coordinates, so its initial physical arc changes in proportion to subsequent circumference, while full physical support remains fixed. This is not a moving-cue protocol.
