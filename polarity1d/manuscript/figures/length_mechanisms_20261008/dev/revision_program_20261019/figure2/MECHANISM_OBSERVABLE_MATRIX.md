**Limited challenger initiation.** Goryachev near-doubling gain 0.654; WP/Holmes/Spring near 1. Lower gain co-occurs with greater restoration and is not an independent causal partition. Concentration is not amount. Growing ramp peak or support changes initial production independently of this mechanism.

**Stronger local restoration of the incumbent.** Common restoration fold: Goryachev 3.44, Spring 1.61, WP/Holmes/deBelly about 1.01–1.10. All Spring primary exposures still replace, so larger restoration alone does not predict fate. Perturbation direction and clock matter. Positive deficit restoration is not generic stability, actual incumbent maintenance or an exhaustive cause of resistance.

**Transport and spatial redistribution limit replacement.** Robust activation does not discriminate challenger-only retention from both-core retention. The WP control supports a transport contribution to retained orientation at a fixed dose; Holmes nonrescue does not establish absence of transport effects. Transport, total material and initial organization covary with size. No intervention-specific threshold curve or reduction of panel A's strength threshold has been measured; no purely transport-isolated explanation is claimed.

**Mechanical or slower-field organization changes response.** Spring causal effect depends on stimulus geometry; corrected deBelly resting ablation is not a causal reversal-time result. A scalar high activity peak is insufficient. The Spring and deBelly mechanisms are different. Evolved intervention, passive temporal association and fixed-state budget are kept at their own inference levels.
