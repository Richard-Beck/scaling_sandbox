# Figure 3: polarity under spatially unbiased fluctuating input

**Design stage; no stochastic integrations launched.** This experiment belongs to
[the manuscript vision](../../../../../../MANUSCRIPT_VISION.md): enlargement may
change the persistence of a measurable polarity state. It does not predict a
cell trajectory or equate activity persistence with fitted velocity persistence.

## Question and native models

Does enlargement alter the temporal persistence of whole-boundary activity
asymmetry under ongoing local fluctuations? Use Wave-pinning, Goryachev and
Holmes on their native periodic reaction–diffusion rings. Read exact retained,
qualifying single-site basals at circumferences 20, 40 and 60 and mesh spacing
0.05. Their preparation, pool means, source hashes and complete state arrays
remain unchanged. Native time and input units are model-specific.

## Candidate noise law

The proposed primary construction perturbs the **same external activation
channel as the existing local cue**. Input is nonnegative and enters the native
active/inactive transfer with equal and opposite signs. It is spatially unbiased
in distribution, not constrained to sum to zero in every realization:

$$
I(s,t)=\mu_m\exp\!\left[\sigma Z(s,t)-\frac{\sigma^2}{2}\right],
\qquad \mu_m=f A_m,\qquad \sigma=1. \tag{1}
$$

Here $A_m$ is the retained long-cue peak (0.1, 6.4 and 1, respectively),
**a declared native-unit reference, not an equal biological dose**. Candidate
$f$ values are 0.001 and 0.01; both are retained rather than chosen for a desired
size trend. The rate mean is $\mu_m$ and its pointwise coefficient of variation
is $\sqrt{e-1}$. Uniform deterministic inputs $I=\mu_m$ and zero-input controls
separate fluctuations from the positive added mean. Mean controls are necessary:
a spatially uniform activation rate can directly change activity moments through
patterned substrate and can alter the polarized branch. Its own input harmonic
is zero, while the native transfer harmonic generally is not.

$Z$ has translation-invariant periodic spatial statistics, stationary OU knots
and unit-variance Gaussian marginals at every time. Its periodic spatial covariance
is generated by convolution with a periodized Gaussian kernel of standard
deviation $\ell/\sqrt{2}$ and a deterministic L2 normalization, with Gaussian
correlation width $\ell=1.5$ physical native length units.
This normalization fixes the ensemble variance at a location; **there is no
per-realization normalization, circumference normalization, or removal of the
spatially uniform mode**. Actual finite-ring covariance is exported. Larger
rings therefore contain more independently fluctuating boundary. The covariance
of the lognormal input is $\mu_m^2[\exp(\sigma^2\rho)-1]$ for Gaussian correlation
$\rho$; the input and Gaussian correlation lengths are distinct.

Gaussian temporal knots are exact stationary OU updates with correlation time
$\tau_n=50$ native time units and spacing $\Delta_n=5$. Between knots use linear
interpolation, divided by its deterministic analytic standard deviation
$[(1-a)^2+a^2+2a(1-a)e^{-\Delta_n/\tau_n}]^{1/2}$, where $a$ is the fractional
position in the interval. This freezes a **normalized interpolated OU-knot
process**, not a stationary continuous-time OU process: Gaussian point marginals
are standard normal at every phase, but between-knot temporal covariance depends
on phase. The normalized interpolation and its actual covariance are recorded.
Primary integration uses maximum BDF step 5; selected numerical checks use
maximum step 1 and tenfold tighter tolerances. A coupled temporal representation
check starts from a common exact OU path at spacing 2.5 and subsamples every
second knot for the coarse path; interpolation differences remain explicit.
Noise realizations are generated from independent declared seeds; no random
numbers are drawn inside the adaptive solver RHS. Whole-field Gaussian draws
are not restricted by initial incumbent position. Equal local noise statistics
are held fixed across circumference **within each model**.

## Bounded pilot and decisions before the ensemble

A fresh-context design critic must assess this plan before integration. Proposed
pilot: both strengths, C20 and C60 in all three models, one declared seed each,
4000 native time units, plus both deterministic means and zero-input controls
at the same sizes. This is 12 noise runs, 12 mean runs and six zero-input runs.
The pilot assesses numerical validity, runtime/storage, mean-control changes
and the response regime. A flat response or destroyed polarity is a result,
not permission to tune until enlargement increases persistence. The parent
orchestrator reviews actual pilot evidence before any main ensemble launch.
Numerical checks compare a retained seed under tighter ODE tolerances and
noise-discretization refinements using explicitly coupled source paths. A time
refinement cannot be called convergence if it silently substitutes independent
random noise.

Tentative main ensemble: all three circumferences, both declared strengths,
12 independent seeds per model/size/strength, horizon 32000. Actual pilot
runtime and degree of response may justify a clearly recorded bounded change,
but no unbounded amplitude or duration search. Deterministic controls have no
independent seed replicates. Every selected and rejected numerical case remains
in the status inventory; failed work is not censoring.

## Readouts and interpretation

For the native primary active field, retain $M_k=\int u(s)e^{2\pi i k s/C}ds$,
total activity $M_0$, $P(t)=M_1(t)/M_1(0)$, relative magnitude $R=|P|$ and
mass-normalized harmonic magnitudes $q_1,q_2$. No per-frame subtraction,
clipping or amplitude normalization. $R$ may exceed one. The initial complex
division aligns each initial vector to $(1,0)$.

Report initial-axis memory $\langle\operatorname{Re}P(t)\rangle$ with magnitude
and ensemble uncertainty. Also calculate vector lag correlation by pooling
within-seed time origins and ensemble cross-products, with the denominator
from both endpoint energies. Show the raw curve, a finite-window correlation
integral and whether decay is resolved; do not force an exponential persistence
time or extrapolate beyond the observation window. Replicate uncertainty uses
seeds, never time origins as independent experiments. First/last-half summaries
expose dependence on the continuing initial-to-forced-state transient.

Directional cosine correlations are supplementary and accompanied by the
fraction of usable pairs at declared magnitude gates 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2. Weak
resultants remain present in the unmasked vector analysis. Opposed active sites
can cancel without activity loss. Magnitude, total activity, second harmonic,
periodic local maxima/valleys and connected active-region diagnostics therefore
accompany the vector curves. Do not call global moment loss local depolarization.

Track dominant/local-site positions and periodic profile alignment; distinguish
continuous single-site displacement from abrupt dominant-site switches and
multisite intervals. Report physical displacement and angular displacement:
**the same physical site drift causes smaller angular change on a larger ring**.
Changing the dominant peak alone does not prove incumbent replacement.
Representative kymographs are selected transparently from declared seedwise
outcome descriptors, including a weak-response case if present, and linked to
the exact scalar/vector trace.

## Frozen statistical definitions (prospective appendix)

The statistical analysis uses the initial complex alignment
$P_r(t)=M_{1,r}(t)/M_{1,r}(0)$ for realization $r$. The initial-axis memory
is the ensemble mean of $\operatorname{Re}P_r(t)$; its decay can reflect
reorientation, loss of resultant magnitude, or both. It is not labelled a
stationary autocorrelation.

For a declared time-origin window $W$ and nonnegative lag $\lambda$, let
$W_\lambda$ contain sampled origins for which both endpoints lie in $W$.
The uncentered vector lag correlation is

$$
C_W(\lambda)=
\frac{\displaystyle \frac1N\sum_{r=1}^{N}
\left\langle\operatorname{Re}
\left[P_r(t+\lambda)P_r(t)^*\right]\right\rangle_{t\in W_\lambda}}
{\displaystyle\sqrt{
\left[\frac1N\sum_{r=1}^{N}\langle|P_r(t)|^2\rangle_{t\in W_\lambda}\right]
\left[\frac1N\sum_{r=1}^{N}\langle|P_r(t+\lambda)|^2\rangle_{t\in W_\lambda}\right]}}
.\tag{2}
$$

No ensemble or temporal mean vector is subtracted. All realizations in a
condition must have the same prescribed complete time grid. Equal realization
weight is used; time origins supply within-realization averages and are never
independent uncertainty replicates. Percentile uncertainty bands resample
complete independent realizations with replacement and recompute the full
ratio. A deterministic control has one realization and no replication interval.
A one-seed pilot likewise has no between-realization uncertainty estimate.

The maximum lag is fixed across all sizes and strengths within an experiment:
1000 for the 4000-unit pilot, and provisionally 8000 for the 32000-unit ensemble.
These horizons are native model time units. The raw signed restricted integral

$$
T_W^{\rm restricted}=\int_0^{\lambda_{\max}}C_W(\lambda)\,\mathrm d\lambda
\tag{3}
$$

is computed by the trapezoid rule on the common retained lag grid. Negative
values of the curve remain in this integral. The integral is not extended to an
unobserved zero, clipped at a first crossing, or converted into an exponential
fit. A sampled first crossing of $1/e$, if present, is a descriptive crossing
rather than an inferred exponential persistence parameter. If decay is
unresolved within the window, that limitation is stated explicitly.

The full interval $[0,T]$, its first half $[0,T/2]$ and its last half $[T/2,T]$
are analysed separately using the same maximum lag and no selected burn-in.
Their agreement is a diagnostic of continuing adaptation, not proof of
stationarity. Time-resolved magnitude, total activity and first/second harmonic
ratios accompany these comparisons. Mean-input and zero-input controls are
analysed identically and remain distinguishable in exported tables.

Direction-only cosine correlations are supplementary. For each magnitude gate
$g\in\{0.05,0.1,0.2\}$, both endpoint magnitudes must exceed $g$ relative to
the initial moment. Report the conditional direction correlation together with
the fraction of all available endpoint pairs that qualifies. The primary
uncentered vector correlation always includes weak resultants; a missing
heading cannot silently remove a weak-polarity state from that analysis.

Local-profile diagnostics retain periodic active-region and prominent-peak
counts, dominant-peak position and displacement in physical length units as
well as angle. At finite profile cadence, an abrupt dominant-peak change is
not enough to prove site replacement. The report therefore links summaries to
native kymographs and flags intervals with multiple regions or weak first
moments. Representative histories are selected by a frozen descriptive rule,
not by which realization best supports increased persistence with size.

The noise audit also exports the realized time-averaged local input mean and
variance, spatial covariance, the spatially uniform input mode and the first
angular input mode. These are checked separately at each circumference:
fixed local covariance does not imply equal fluctuations of a whole-ring
angular mode or equal total applied input across sizes.

The frozen primary scalar cadence is 5 native time units, including 0 and the
horizon; profile cadence is 20, also including both endpoints. Every retained
scalar lag from 0 through the fixed maximum lag is used, in increments of 5.
The correlation is explicitly **undefined** when either endpoint energy is
zero; a directional correlation with no eligible pairs is **unavailable**.
Neither case is replaced by zero or one. Output tables retain the status and
pair counts.

Profile diagnostics count periodic connected regions above each frame's
minimum plus 50% of its contrast, together with the frozen basal half-contrast
threshold. Periodic peaks must have prominence at least 0.05 in native activity
units, matching the producer's fixed diagnostic convention. The array is triplicated for peak detection, and
only peaks in the central copy are counted, so a peak crossing the plotting
cut is counted once. A contrast at most $10^{-12}$ is flagged as numerically flat. A current
half-contrast region is eligible for the single-region diagnostic only when
contrast is at least 0.05 and there is at most one prominent peak. The dominant
region position is its excess-mass circular center. Displacement uses the
shortest periodic step between adjacent retained profiles; steps
larger than 1.5 physical units are flagged as sampled large steps, without
being labelled replacement. Finite cadence can alias faster site motion.

The representative-history rule is one within-condition medoid for each
model/circumference/strength group. Its frozen descriptors are final initial-axis
memory, minimum relative magnitude, time-mean relative magnitude, and the
fraction of profiles with more than one current half-contrast connected region.
Distances are absolute deviations from the within-group descriptor median,
scaled by within-group interquartile range (unit fallback for a zero range).
The sum determines the medoid; ties use the declared case identifier. A separate
history with the globally lowest sampled resultant is included as an explicitly
labelled diagnostic, not a typical realization. Final panels may display a
subset of these declared candidates, with the selection explained.

Endpoint-conditioned site-displacement curves use the profile cadence and the
same fixed lag horizon. Both endpoint profiles must contain one resolved current
half-contrast region, at most one native-prominence peak, contrast at least 0.05
and relative first-moment magnitude at least 0.1. Report the mean squared
shortest physical displacement and angular cosine together with eligible-pair
fractions. These are finite-cadence endpoint comparisons, not a diffusion
coefficient, proof of continued site identity, or a migration displacement.

The vector-correlation table also exports both endpoint energies and their RMS
relative magnitudes at every lag. A nonzero denominator is insufficient to make
a weak-resultant axis informative: endpoint RMS below the declared 0.05
magnitude gate is flagged in the table and restricted-integral summary. Raw
correlations remain available, but these flagged windows are not interpreted
as directional persistence. This is a declared weak-polarity criterion, not a
claim that 0.05 is a numerical roundoff estimate. Direction-gated pair fractions,
profile contrast and both harmonic ratios expose loss of local activity
contrast versus opposing-site cancellation.

Prospective main-horizon revision after the completed bounded pilot: use
32000 native units and a maximum lag of 8000 rather than 16000/4000. Most pilot
conditions show weak decay within 4000, motivating one bounded extension rather
than an amplitude search or a horizon selected to favour a size trend. Scalar
cadence remains 5. Main profile cadence is proposed as 40 to bound storage;
pilot cadence remains 20. This is a proposed main configuration pending the
parent's actual-pilot review, not permission to launch from this document.

For the prospective main ensemble, site-displacement curves use the producer's
scalar descriptors every 5 native time units, even though saved primary
profiles are proposed every 40. The analyst independently reproduces region,
peak and circular-center descriptors at every saved primary snapshot before
using the scalar descriptor series. The pilot physical diagnostic remains at
its declared saved-profile cadence of 20. Every site table exports its actual
cadence and descriptor source; the two cadences are never silently conflated.
