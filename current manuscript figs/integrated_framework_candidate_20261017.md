## Main message and why both rows are needed

Across the five retained model preparations, roughly doubling nominal size increases the excess active material in the incumbent, with model-specific changes in width and peak concentration. A larger incumbent can therefore present a different competition problem. The functional consequences are demonstrated conditionally in Goryachev and Wave-pinning: changed remote substrate availability affects initial activation, and the disturbed incumbent can recapture redistributed substrate after exact return. The later interaction also depends on spatial arrangement, as the separate Holmes tests show. Separation is a modifier of this framework, not an alternative headline. All-five incumbent growth is established in these retained preparations; generalized greater stability is not. The spatial results cannot fill that gap. Organizing these ingredients into one figure does not prove that the causal mechanism is complete.

The top row makes the hierarchy explicit. **A is the common descriptive observation across all five models. B and C are model-specific functional exemplars**, not claims proved across the five. Marker shapes in A distinguish amount, width and peak concentration; Goryachev/Wave-pinning colors in B/C identify models. All folds retain raw values and exact references in the linked data. The bottom row preserves native spatial histories because activation or an original-core-loss clock cannot distinguish replacement, merger and retention of separate sites.

| Spring | L₀ 1→2 | 2.8868 | 2.5355 | 1.0170 |

Spring uses resting length L₀ 1→2; its measured physical domain changes 1.048990→2.049367, so amounts and widths use that physical domain. The other pairs are 19→39 or 20→40 in their native length units. These are convenient near-doubling comparisons, not a biological WGD fold calibration. Holmes peak concentration decreases slightly; “larger incumbent” means more active material or increased spatial scale, not universally higher peak amplitude.

Excess-active amount is the concentration integral above the field minimum inside the boundary-connected half-contrast core. Width and absolute peak concentration are separate observables, **not an exact width×peak factorization**; background changes matter, especially in deBelly. The amount is not divided by domain length, so its increase alone does not isolate a size-specific feedback mechanism. Full retained length series and native profiles remain in the existing PDF Figure 2 and linked source tables.

## B–C: conditional effects on competition

All 36 retained interval cases (18 per model, L = 9–39) appear. Both panels normalize each model to its own exact L = 19 readout, matching A’s 19→39 chemical-model pair; native values and references remain in [the functional CSV](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/integrated_framework_candidate_20261017/supporting/data/conditional_functional_readouts.csv). Open points mark L = 19/L = 39 to connect with A.

B measures initial extra activation per unit equal local opposing cue. Native kinetics give $f(u,v;s)-f(u,v;0)=s\,v$, so this readout is the remote inactive concentration. By L = 39, relative gain is 0.6536 in Goryachev and 1.0000 in Wave-pinning. It is an initial chemical response, not a front-establishment threshold or an evolved takeover probability.

C measures the **actual immediate net repair rate per active material lost**, including native reaction and diffusion in the original fixed core, relative to a matched sham. The retained experiment removes 0.5% of the entire original inactive profile for 0.1 native time unit and returns exactly that profile pointwise. By L = 39, this conditional gain is 3.41× its L = 19 value for Goryachev and 1.06× for Wave-pinning. Goryachev’s positive chemical rebound is mediated by endogenous inactive surplus; its active-field contribution at sham inactive concentration is slightly negative. This is conditional recapture after the specified disturbance, **not global or generic intrinsic stability**. The earlier chemical candidate retains actual damage/recovery histories and the detailed returned-state allocation. All raw native values and the original L = 9 reference/ratio columns remain unchanged in support; this revision changes normalization, not the experiment.

The visible conversion annotation links organization to chemistry over the **same 19→39 pair**. Goryachev’s peak concentration increases 1.9033-fold, and the surplus-weighted native conversion coefficient increases 3.5644-fold. Core inactive surplus per core damage changes 0.9548-fold, giving 3.4032-fold substrate contribution; actual net repair changes 3.4144-fold. Wave-pinning’s nearly unchanged peak concentration (1.00024-fold) accompanies 1.0246-fold weighted conversion, 1.0175-fold exposure, 1.0425-fold substrate contribution and 1.0630-fold net repair. The [matched factorization rows](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/integrated_framework_candidate_20261017/supporting/data/matched_L19_L39_factorization.csv), [folds](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/integrated_framework_candidate_20261017/supporting/data/matched_L19_L39_folds.csv) and [definitions/source JSON](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/integrated_framework_candidate_20261017/supporting/metadata/matched_L19_L39_factorization.json) retain full precision.

“Conversion” is the native coefficient A(u)=∂f/∂v, averaged with the actual positive returned inactive surplus as weight. This is not a new binding process or independently evolved ablation. The coefficient×exposure identity describes the substrate term; the net repair also includes active-field and diffusion changes. A broader incumbent can remain functionally important even if its local coefficient changes little. More material by itself is the common state observation, not the feedback mechanism.

## D–F: spatial arrangement modifies later interaction

These are separate native Holmes periodic-ring preparations at matched h = 0.025 µm, not the interval checkpoints in A–C. All three challenged regions activate. Through the displayed 1000-s cue-on plus 3000-s cue-free interval, C = 30, d = 12 loses the original site while the challenger remains; C = 40, d = 12 fills the intervening valley into a merged region; C = 40, d = 16 retains two separated sites. White/gold dotted lines identify original/cue positions and “Cue off” labels mark withdrawal at 1000 s. Concentration is raw µM on one shared color scale. Common old-at-zero coordinates retain the 30-µm versus 40-µm domain difference; gray space is outside C = 30’s displayed periodic interval.

D→E changes circumference while holding physical cue separation 12 µm. It also changes total material, incumbent width, local state, cue fraction and the complementary route (18→28 µm). E→F alone changes cue position at fixed C = 40 and the **exact same full basal state**, with routes 12/28→16/24 µm. These interventions do not isolate diffusion travel time or establish incumbent width as the sole cause. The common cue has support 3 µm, peak 1 µM/s and duration 1000 s, followed by 13,000 s without cue.

The [full spatial candidate and endpoints](main_candidate_2_20261017.md), [size-path report](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/holmes_size_bridge_20261017/supporting/metadata/RESULTS.md), [mesh checks](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/holmes_distance_robustness_20261017/supporting/metadata/MESH_RESULTS.md) and [N800 conserved-Rac imbalance continuation](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/holmes_distance_robustness_20261017/supporting/metadata/IMBALANCE_RESULTS.md) remain support. The latter uses h = 0.05 µm endpoint states, not the fine-grid states plotted here. The predefined C = 50, d = 20 case also retains two sites through the observed window; it is disclosed in the size report. No stable branch, universal distance law or precise merger timing is claimed.

## Relation to the current PDF and remaining gaps

A condenses the common material/organization evidence from current PDF Figure 2. B makes the initial cue-gain implication of Figure 3’s inactive-depletion evidence explicit. C uses the same removal/return evidence as Figure 4 but plots actual net repair including diffusion, while retaining its substrate-surplus interpretation. D–F add the separately reviewed circular-domain tests; they have no direct PDF counterpart. [Exact panel mapping](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/integrated_framework_candidate_20261017/supporting/metadata/pdf_to_integrated_mapping.json) records these distinctions.

The figure offers one framework with different evidence levels: common incumbent enlargement; demonstrated model-specific chemical consequences; spatial modification of later interactions. It does not prove all-five suppression of competitor activation, all-five repair enhancement, a causal partition, migration persistence or a biological WGD calibration. Whether these conditional mechanisms explain the complete length response remains open. [Evidence gaps](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/integrated_framework_candidate_20261017/supporting/metadata/evidence_gaps.json) and the original ingredient documents retain the limitations.
