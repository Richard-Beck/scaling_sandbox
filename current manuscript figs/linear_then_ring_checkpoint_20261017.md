## Experimental distinctions and remaining gap

Greater persistence in WGD-associated enlarged cells motivates the work. These figures organize measurements of local activity in migrating cells:

| Figure evidence | Experimental readout it motivates |
|---|---|
| 1A/D: amount, concentration and spatial extent | Incumbent extent, peak and integrated reporter signal; molecular amount requires appropriate reporter calibration. |
| 1B: incremental opposing-input response | Early challenger activation under comparable measured input. |
| 1C: specified conditional response | Measure actual incumbent local loss and rebound during competition separately from the model's hold/return coefficient. |
| 2: location-dependent spatial patterns | Times to extinction, replacement or merger, and two-site lifetimes, stratified by size and boundary separation. |

The [experimental-readout note](migration_consequences_bridge_20261017.md) develops the measurement logic, including testing whether activity sites correspond to local protrusions. deBelly participates here through its established corrected linear results; its ring development remains a separate sidecar pending a convincing demonstration.

For spontaneous events, use common detection criteria and compare rates per cell and per unit accessible boundary length and time; different upstream fluctuations can change event rates independently of input gain. This is an experimental activity framework, not a prediction of cell trajectories or proof that the assays explain WGD-associated persistence.

The common all-five result is incumbent enlargement. Enhanced conditional repair and reduced initial gain are demonstrated linear exemplars, not all-five stability claims. Size also changes total material and spatial organization together. The ring tests show a location effect within a declared geometry; they do not identify which transport route or basal-state feature causes it. Those limits remain explicit rather than being filled with untested common metrics.
