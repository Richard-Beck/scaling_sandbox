## Working message

Enlargement changes the amount and organization of the incumbent. In model-specific chemical examples, that organization changes substrate use: less initial remote activation and stronger conditional recapture in Goryachev accompany its taller incumbent. After a challenger activates, spatial arrangement can modify whether activity replaces the old site, merges with it or remains separated. Distance is a modifier of the incumbent framework, not an alternative explanation or a substitute for missing stability evidence.

## How the six panels form one argument

**A establishes the shared starting observation across all five models:** near-doubling nominal size increases excess-active incumbent amount, organized through different changes in width and concentration. Higher peak concentration is not universal; Holmes peak concentration decreases slightly. More material alone does not identify a feedback mechanism, and the three observables are not an exact width-times-peak decomposition.

**B and C expose conditional chemical consequences in Goryachev and Wave-pinning.** Their reference is now L = 19, matching A's 19→39 chemical-model pair, while all 36 L = 9–39 cases remain displayed. Over that matched pair, Goryachev's 1.90-fold higher peak accompanies a 3.56-fold larger surplus-weighted native conversion coefficient, a 0.955-fold surplus exposure per damage, and 3.41-fold stronger immediate net core repair. Equal local remote cue gain falls to 0.654. Wave-pinning mainly broadens, with much smaller local coefficient and repair changes. C measures reaction plus diffusion after the specified 0.5% inactive-profile withholding and exact return; there is no rival in that assay. These are model-specific functional examples, not generalized stability tests.

**D–F ask what happens after challenger activation in separate Holmes rings.** Changing circumference at fixed physical separation changes several properties together. Moving the cue in the exact same circumference-40 starting state isolates its position and changes merger versus finite-window retention of two sites. The full native fields distinguish those outcomes from replacement at the cue. This is not a tested causal continuation of B/C's interval assay.

## Critique, response and open scope

The [fresh cohesion critique](reviews/integrated_cohesion_r1.md) accepts this hierarchy for discussion and identifies the essential bridge: show which chemical property of the larger incumbent changes, using the same size pair. The revision aligns B/C to L = 19, makes the conversion-coefficient link visible, and retains the L = 9 native references and ratios in support. It keeps the specified disturbance and finite-window limits explicit.

All-five incumbent growth is established in these retained preparations. Generalized greater incumbent stability is not. The chemical exemplars and spatial modifier do not complete that missing causal link, establish migration persistence, or supply a biological WGD fold calibration.

## Follow-up evidence for the next figure discussion

The [systematic native-ring report](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/chemical_ring_functional_20261017/RESULTS.md)
and its [fresh critique](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/chemical_ring_functional_20261017/supporting/metadata/RESULT_CRITIQUE.md)
address the model-selection concern by comparing Wave-pinning, Goryachev and
Holmes throughout the same planned size/placement grid. Their gain, conditional
repair and spatial histories now come from the same ring preparations within
each model/size. This is additional evidence beyond the supplied PDF and the
interval assays shown above.

The scientifically useful contrast is that larger Goryachev incumbents can
persist through transient challenger activation, whereas larger Wave-pinning
and Holmes rings can retain two distinct sites. Conditional repair changes
strongly with size in Goryachev and much less in the other two. A positive
coefficient alone is insufficient: all early unperturbed original cores still
lose material. The figure discussion can therefore distinguish local restoring
response, actual incumbent retention and the fate of the challenger.

The [mixed-boundary deBelly pilot](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/debelly_shared_chemical_arcs_20261017/RESULTS.md)
also supplies a workable unequal-arc baseline under shared chemical diffusion
and separate mechanics. All three cued separations repolarize toward the
challenger; the pilot establishes no distance-dependent fate at this dose.
The [experimental-readout note](migration_consequences_bridge_20261017.md)
connects organization, site establishment and competition to possible local
activity measurements in migrating cells.
