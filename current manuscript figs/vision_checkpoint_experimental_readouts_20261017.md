# Experimental interpretation of the three-figure checkpoint

The [manuscript vision](../MANUSCRIPT_VISION.md) connects enlargement-dependent polarity dynamics to a migration-persistence hypothesis. The [three-figure plan](figure_revision_plan_20261017.md) can make that connection through activity measurements, while preserving activation and post-activation competition as distinct possibilities. This note proposes interpretation only; numerical effects and assay qualifications belong with the evidence owners' figures.

| Checkpoint figure | Simulated quantities | Possible experimental activity measurements |
|---|---|---|
| **1. All-five linear organization, gain/recovery and justified long-cue competition** | Incumbent extent, peak and active amount; incremental local-input gain; paired active-loss response versus control; transient and retained responses during/after the declared long cue. | Incumbent physical extent, peak and integrated excess reporter signal, with calibration before molecular-amount interpretation. Early challenger growth under characterized local stimulation separates input response from subsequent establishment. Actual local loss/rebound and site histories during stimulation and follow-up distinguish challenger collapse, replacement and recovery. Declare input support, amplitude, duration, perturbation region and recovery window; response to one small active-loss assay is conditional, rather than generic stability. |
| **2. Supported rings: circumference/separation, native histories and global activity vectors** | Linear/ring correspondence; gain and the common recovery assay across size/location; local histories and global vector magnitude/direction. | Follow both the initial incumbent region and tracked activity sites: fixed-region loss can be redistribution. Record extinction, replacement, merger and two-site lifetimes against measured size, site extent, center separations and edge gaps, including both boundary routes where meaningful. Relate these histories to global reporter-polarity reorientation, loss and recovery; low vector magnitude may reflect opposing sites rather than weak local activity. Organization remains a possible mediator of size effects. |
| **3. Separate replicated noisy-ring polarity persistence** | Under declared stochastic inputs, compare activity-vector correlation, magnitude and local site histories across sizes; distinguish wandering, replacement and cancellation. | Analogous activity-polarity correlations, near-zero-magnitude intervals and event lifetimes can describe spontaneous fluctuations. Use common detection/follow-up rules and retain censored events. Read correlation alongside magnitude and site histories; withhold vector direction near zero magnitude. Longer activity memory with enlargement would support the polarity-persistence hypothesis, without supplying a cell velocity law. |

For irregular cells, register signal to a segmented boundary and declare the vector embedding—boundary normals or centroid-to-boundary directions—with physical arclength weights, background subtraction and normalization. Check how shape/registration changes affect the vector; uniform-background cancellation on a circle does not automatically carry over. Equal ring angles must not silently become equal distances on an irregular boundary. Paired local boundary extension/retraction can assess activity–protrusion correspondence separately.

Controlled comparisons require declared input channel, local amplitude and spatial/temporal noise correlations. Fixed local statistics permit more fluctuating boundary segments as perimeter grows. In observational recordings, upstream triggers remain unknown: matched detected excursions estimate subsequent establishment, not equal-input gain. Report event rates per cell-time and accessible boundary-length–time.

Activity-polarity memory can be compared empirically with measured displacement/directness; observed migration summaries and conditional OU persistence estimates remain distinct. **New WGD motility assay results retain their separate reserved major section and figure.** Simulated polarity supplies neither those observations nor an assumed mapping to movement.
