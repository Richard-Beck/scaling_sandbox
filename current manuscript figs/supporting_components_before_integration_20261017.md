# Two main-text figure candidates

These candidates address different parts of our discussion. The first asks what
an enlarged incumbent does differently with a shared chemical pool. The second
asks how size and challenger position change the spatial outcome, including a
location intervention with the starting incumbent and circumference fixed. WGD-associated migration persistence
motivates both questions; neither figure directly measures migration persistence.
The [first editorial comparison](reviews/main_pair_final.md) states the strongest
claims and remaining conceptual gaps; candidate 2 has since gained the
three-outcome layout below.

## Candidate 1: enlargement changes substrate capture and availability

![Candidate 1: incumbent profiles, a specified inactive-pool disturbance and the chemical contributions to immediate recovery.](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/main_candidate_1_20261017/main_candidate_1.png)

**Message.** In the selected Goryachev regime, enlargement concentrates more
activity into a taller incumbent. This increases its response to available
inactive substrate while leaving less inactive material available for initial
activation elsewhere. Wave-pinning mainly broadens its incumbent and shows much
smaller changes in these responses. The distinction concerns the organization
and kinetics of the shared pool, beyond a larger total amount of material.

**Why this figure.** The profiles define what “larger incumbent” means. The
actual removal-and-return experiment then makes the response concrete, and the
rate allocation identifies the chemical change responsible for rebound. This
connects architecture to an observed functional response without treating a
reversal-duration curve as its explanation.

**What the critique changed.** The initial draft described stronger immediate
repair. A fresh critic showed that Goryachev's positive chemical rebound comes
from recapturing inactive material left available after the disturbance; its
active-field contribution at unchanged inactive concentration slightly opposes
recovery. A further retained-state check finds a 16.8-fold increase in the
conversion coefficient, while surplus exposure per unit damage decreases
slightly. The revised figure exposes that distinction. This supports a
substrate-capture mechanism under the specified disturbance, rather than a
general stability claim or proof that the incumbent defeats a rival.

## Candidate 2: activation can lead to replacement, merger or coexistence

![Candidate 2: changing cell size or challenger position produces replacement, merger or two retained sites after both sites activate.](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/main_candidate_2_v2_20261017/main_candidate_2_v2.png)

**Message.** All three Holmes challengers activate. Subsequently, the smaller
ring loses its incumbent and retains a cap near the cue; the larger ring with
the same physical challenger distance forms one merged cap between the sites;
moving the challenger farther around that same larger ring leaves two sites
through the recorded cue-free window. Initial activation and a single
original-core-loss clock cannot distinguish these outcomes.

**Why this figure.** The first two columns change circumference from 30 to 40 µm
at a fixed 12-µm challenger distance. The last two use the exact same
circumference-40 starting state and move the cue from 12 to 16 µm. Spatial
histories distinguish old-site extinction from valley filling and coexistence;
basal, withdrawal and late profiles show the scale of the activity involved.
All three have the same local cue and native kinetics. Enlargement also changes
material, incumbent architecture and the other arc length, so its effect cannot
be assigned to incumbent width alone.

**What the critiques changed.** The smaller-ring outcome is replacement near
the cue, not merger between the sites. The initial four-panel draft is preserved
in the detailed candidate. The broader residual incumbent in larger rings is a
useful physical clue, but its width does not by itself explain merger versus
separation in the two circumference-40 cases. Those widths are almost equal.

The circumference-40 outcomes recur on the finer grid. Both signs of a small
conserved-Rac redistribution also retain two caps over an additional 13000-s
continuation on the original coarser grid. These are finite-window checks;
direct refinement and perturbation tests of the new smaller-ring case have not
been performed. Retaining local activity is distinct from retaining a migration
direction.

## Where the other models fit

All five declared circular constructions retain a polarized preparation
([overview and fresh review](circular_polarization_result_20261017.md)).
The [Spring analogue](../polarity1d/manuscript/SPRING_RING_DISTANCE_PILOT_20261017.md)
shows near-site bridging versus a longer interval of separated activity followed
by old-site extinction. These cases support the need to distinguish activation
from later competition, but add less to the main layout than the Holmes pair.

The [deBelly two-arc pilot](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/distance_ring_protocol_20261017/debelly_two_arc_distance_result.md)
exposes an unresolved construction choice. Unequal cuts produce nearly uniform
Rac-poor and Rac-rich arcs even without a cue, rather than two localized active
sites. The short arc accumulates 94–97% of all MCA, whose late fields remain
unsteady. The [fresh critique](reviews/debelly_distance_r1.md) supports treating
this as evidence about the gap and coupling assumptions. Shared site dynamics
and chemical communication across the unmodelled protrusions need discussion
before this construction can carry the main distance message.

My current judgment is that candidate 1 has the closer mechanistic connection
to enlargement, while candidate 2 offers the clearer new spatial result. They
are alternatives for discussion; combining every model and diagnostic would
make the main figure harder to read without resolving the missing migration
link.
