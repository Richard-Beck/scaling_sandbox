# deBelly mixed two-arc result: fresh-context critique

17 October 2026. Reviewed the [result report](../../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/debelly_shared_chemical_arcs_20261017/RESULTS.md), actual main, chemical-trace and mechanical-flank figures, design and interface implementation. Independently read all six full-field archives and native interval ledgers under [the run directory](../../polarity1d/manuscript/data/runs/debelly_shared_chemical_arcs_20261017/). No simulations or parameter changes were made.

**Verdict: a sensible, disclosed first-pass realization of the proposed shared-chemistry baseline, and a material recovery from the preceding disconnected-arc failure.** All three cue trials select one broad Rac-high region near the challenger after 13000 s without the cue. Every matched gap-only control retains its high region near the incumbent. These are finite-window chemical repolarization results at one circumference and one cue protocol. They do not yet demonstrate a distance-dependent fate or a physical migration direction.

The recovery is visible in actual spatial fields. Independently reconstructing the periodic endpoint profiles gives exactly one connected region above each profile's half-contrast level. Its width is approximately 21.75, 20.98 and 20.67 µm at the three separations; corresponding cue/control widths agree at cell resolution. Both unequal arcs contain substantial Rac variation, so the former assignment of one flat high arc and one flat low arc has disappeared. “Localized” here means a broad domain within the 62-µm circumference, not a narrow peak fixed at the gap. The unequal-arc maxima lie about 2.08 and 1.47 µm beyond the challenger. The initial/control overlays expose the modest changes caused by installing the gap alone.

The result also survives a basic accounting check. Reconstructed total MCA differs from 6.2 by at most 2.67 × 10⁻¹⁵. Independently summing each recorded arc's signed advection, reciprocal unbound diffusion, sequential-exchange residual and uniform return reproduces its saved full-field MCA history to within 7.97 × 10⁻¹². Final short-arc fractions are 25.3%, 37.7% and 50%, close to their own starting values and far from the preceding 94–97% accumulation. Unequal-arc interface exchange is nonzero. These checks support implemented bookkeeping; mass conservation alone cannot validate local biological transport.

**The boundary qualification remains essential.** Rac, Rho and unbound MCA share evolving *diffusive face traces*. The adjacent cell averages are allowed to differ, as expected in a finite-volume description with finite gradients. Unbound endpoint advection retains the native one-sided cell-concentration reconstruction, including signed inward episodes, and global uniform MCA correction. A donor cell value is an ordinary first-order reconstruction at outflow; its difference from a linear diffusive trace is not, by itself, a failure of continuum concentration continuity. At this finite grid, however, the advective operator does not impose the same reconstructed face concentration on every boundary term. This is still a mixed closure, with neither resolved cap storage nor complete advective–diffusive cap flux matching.

My direct archive check finds a maximum saved unbound donor-minus-trace difference of 1.047 × 10⁻⁴; equal arcs agree to roundoff. The added fixed-state flux comparison quantifies the discrepancy but cannot establish that an alternatively integrated advective rule would select the same state, nor prove mesh convergence. That limitation does not block this explicitly disclosed pilot or require another campaign before discussing it.

Common chemical input also does not create a single mechanical protrusion. At the first cue trial's challenger, the endpoint Rac values average to 0.89548 while flank tensions are 2.90466 and 0.01969. Separate commands are therefore an intended consequence of the construction. Describing this as chemical incumbent replacement is supported; describing it as a force-balanced front, whole-cell turning, or increased migration persistence would require a separate physical argument. The brief coexistence of incumbent and activated challenger is directly present: at 5 s, challenger Rac exceeds 1.02 in all cue trials while incumbent Rac remains about 0.89546. Their subsequent selection does not isolate chemical exchange, MCA recycling, tension feedback or transport time as the cause.

**No mathematical or visual blocker to sharing the diagnostic result was found.** The main figure preserves all six controls, complete recovery histories and endpoint profiles. Its cue-period heatmap clipping is explicitly labeled, and the chemical-trace companion shows the larger cue-driven amplitudes. For discussion, lead with the endpoint profile row and its paired controls; the dense twelve-panel page is supporting evidence. Small late ranges across all fields are useful descriptive evidence, but the absence of a separately prescribed holdout prevents calling these newly qualified attractors. The report respects that distinction.

**Anticipated user critique:** “This now looks like a localized polarity domain rather than one active arc. But every distance succeeds, and the two flanks still have very different mechanics. What has changed physically?” The defensible answer is that transparent diffusive chemical communication removes the previous arc segregation under the same incumbent and prescribed cue tests, while allowing mechanical asymmetry. Because chemical transport and the Rac sensor changed together, this comparison does not identify which change is responsible. Its immediate value is a workable baseline for reasoning about spatial interaction; the six outcomes do not yet supply a distance mechanism.
