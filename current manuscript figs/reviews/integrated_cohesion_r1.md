Three points matter most:

1. **Make A's contribution architectural, rather than treating more material as the mechanism.** At fixed mean pool density, total material grows with domain size by construction. The roughly twofold incumbent-amount increases in Wave-pinning, Goryachev and Holmes largely track this imposed scaling; they remain a legitimate common starting observation. The informative distinction is how that material is organized: Goryachev mainly gains concentration, whereas Wave-pinning and Holmes mainly gain width. That difference gives the chemical examples a purpose. A should explicitly pair its common amount result with the fixed-density premise and direct attention to organization. A compact size-fold reference would help; another assay is unnecessary. Do not demand a size-normalized increase as a prerequisite for functional importance: a broader incumbent can matter even when its amount scales proportionally.

2. **C needs the substrate mediator and a matching comparison, not stronger stability language.** Immediate net repair after the specified removal/return is a valid conditional functional example. It is not an arbitrary proxy, but it contains no rival and does not measure general incumbent stability. The integrated panel removes the ingredient draft's decisive explanation: Goryachev's positive rebound comes from endogenous inactive surplus converted by the incumbent, while its active-field contribution at sham substrate is slightly negative. This makes B and C two aspects of shared-pool chemistry: less initial remote activation and stronger conditional recapture. Merely calling C “conditional” is insufficient explanation.
