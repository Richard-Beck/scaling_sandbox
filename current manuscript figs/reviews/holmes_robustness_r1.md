# Independent review: bounded Holmes distance verification

17 October 2026. Fresh-context scientific and figure critique of the completed
challenge-mesh and conserved active-Rac redistribution checks supporting
[candidate 2](../main_candidate_2_20261017.md). No integrations were run and no
authored result-package files were edited.

## Decision

**These checks sufficiently qualify the main figure's finite-window claim.**
The N800/N1600 comparison retains the observed d = 12 merger and d = 16 two-cap
response, using the same physical challenge protocol and a freshly qualified
fine-mesh basal state. The matched continuations show that the d = 16 pattern
also retains two caps for a further 13,000 cue-free seconds after either sign
of the specified small conserved redistribution.

There is no decisive numerical or accounting gap requiring another integration
before presenting that result. The important remaining boundaries concern its
meaning: this is a separation-dependent spatial response at fixed circumference,
not a stable-branch demonstration, a uniquely identified molecular mechanism,
or a demonstrated explanation of WGD-associated migration persistence. A broad
distance, duration, parameter or longevity campaign is unnecessary for this
scoped claim.

## What the mesh check establishes

The [mesh result](../../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/holmes_distance_robustness_20261017/supporting/metadata/MESH_RESULTS.md)
uses circumference 40 µm, peak-1 µM/s raised-cosine cues with total support
3 µm, 1000 s of stimulation and 13,000 s of basal recovery. Both fine challenges
start from the same exact saved qualified N1600 state. That state was prepared
afresh from native homogeneous conditions, rather than interpolated from the
coarse endpoint. The separately prepared coarse and fine basal states differ
by at most 3.084 × 10⁻⁵ on the frozen field scales at their shared relative
coordinates.

Independent inspection of the retained arrays confirms exact starts and phase
continuity, identical coarse/fine sample times, the prescribed cues, positive
fields and native Rac/Rho pool means of 7.5/3.1 µM. Both discretized cues have
integral 1.5 µM·µm/s to roundoff. The physical comparison uses arc from each
original incumbent peak: every second fine cell coincides with a coarse
relative coordinate. No optimized shift or concentration normalization is
used. This appropriately compares fresh native solutions of the same protocol;
the identical-array requirement applies within each mesh.

The [raw Rac profiles](../../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/holmes_distance_robustness_20261017/challenge_mesh_profiles.png)
and [all four terminal fields](../../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/holmes_distance_robustness_20261017/challenge_mesh_all_fields.png)
support the architectural contrast directly. At d = 12, both meshes end with
one broad cap centered near 5.85 µm and width 16.538 µm. At d = 16, both retain
two caps near −0.10/16.00 µm, with widths approximately 8.524/7.921 µm and peaks
near 1.485 µM. The result is not resting on a scalar original-core average or
an arbitrary component threshold.

The maximum terminal all-field discrepancy is 8.965 × 10⁻⁵ on frozen scales
7.5, 7.5, 3.1, 3.1 µM. **That endpoint bound must not be extended to the whole
trajectory.** The largest sampled discrepancy occurs during d = 12 bridge
formation, 440 s after withdrawal at arc 6.15 µm. Active Rho is
0.805661/0.857910 µM on N800/N1600: an absolute difference of 0.0522491 µM,
or 0.0168546 on the fixed Rho scale. Active Rac there is
1.177207/1.130861 µM. The new
[transient overlay](../../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/holmes_distance_robustness_20261017/challenge_mesh_largest_transient.png)
makes the localized discrepancy visible.

An independently recomputed prominence-0.05 peak diagnostic becomes
persistently single-peaked at retained samples 445 s for N800 and 450 s for
N1600. These are sampled diagnostic times, not an exact physical merger clock.
The candidate's 450-s profile still shows the same filled bridge on both meshes.
For d = 16, the largest scaled discrepancy over every saved cue/recovery state
is only 1.097 × 10⁻⁴. Thus the qualitative contrast and the chosen snapshots
are well supported; an exactly mesh-invariant merger time and an asymptotic
convergence order are not established. The result package now scopes this
distinction correctly.

## What the conserved perturbation establishes

The [imbalance result](../../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/holmes_distance_robustness_20261017/supporting/metadata/IMBALANCE_RESULTS.md)
starts at the exact original N800 d = 16 endpoint, after the first 13,000-s
cue-free interval. The unperturbed and two perturbed continuations share the
same added 13,000-s window and retained times. Each perturbed initial array
changes active Rac alone; inactive Rac and both Rho fields remain exactly
identical to the reference. There is no continued external cue.

The specified amount is 0.0816912 µM·µm: 1% of the smaller site's starting
above-background active-Rac amount inside its fixed half-prominence core.
Independent reconstruction reproduces the fractional-cell core weights and
unit-integral raised-cosine tapers. Equal material is added and removed.
The saved imposed delta integrates to at most 1.11 × 10⁻¹⁷ µM·µm; subtracting
the rounded perturbed array from its reference gives residuals below
5.0 × 10⁻¹⁷ µM·µm. All saved Rac/Rho pool means remain native to roundoff.

The denominator matters. This is **1% of a defined local excess amount**, not
1% of the whole conserved Rac pool or 1% at every point. The transferred
amount is approximately 0.02723% of the conserved Rac pool. The peak local
concentration change is approximately 0.02063 µM. Both perturbation signs
meaningfully change the local imbalance, but neither reverses which unequal
fixed core initially has more excess amount. These are two signs of one
specified perturbation direction, not a perturbation ensemble.

All three terminal raw profiles retain two resolved caps. Relative to the
concurrently evolved control, the signed fixed-core excess imbalance starts
at approximately ±0.00960338 and ends at −0.000211146/+0.000210912. Therefore
2.1987%/2.1962% of the imposed control-relative difference remains at the
endpoint. The terminal maximum full-field differences are
4.2149 × 10⁻⁴/4.2108 × 10⁻⁴ on the frozen scales. These conclusions reproduce
from the native arrays, not only from the summary table.

**Most of the reduction is rapid; the late residual is not steadily damping.**
By 100 s, its magnitudes are approximately 0.000210415/0.000210185; they then
increase slightly to the endpoint values above. The control itself also
continues to evolve: its maximum scaled start-to-end field change is
3.547 × 10⁻⁴. Consequently, “the endpoint retains only about 2.2% of the imposed
imbalance difference” is accurate. “The competitive mode decays throughout
13,000 s” or “the asymmetric state returns to a fixed stable equilibrium”
would overstate the evidence. The fixed unequal cores also make this a local
observable rather than a symmetry coordinate or partition of a conserved
active pool.

The [four-panel continuation figure](../../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/holmes_distance_robustness_20261017/imbalance_continuation.png)
now exposes the rapid response with its explicitly labeled linear/logarithmic
time axis. Raw profiles establish the retained architecture; the imbalance
trace quantifies the specified perturbation. Those are complementary pieces
of evidence. They do not identify the slowest mode, give an eigenvalue or
exclude subsequent competition.

## Main-figure implications

Keep the readable four-panel Holmes comparison as the candidate. The mesh and
perturbation figures belong in linked supporting evidence; appending all of
their diagnostics to the main figure would weaken the spatial message. Update
the candidate's pending-check wording and link the completed results. Use s,
µm and µM consistently when the support figures are prepared for publication.

A defensible leading statement is:

> At fixed circumference and conserved pools, the same local cue produces
> similar early Rac activation at the two tested positions. After withdrawal,
> the nearer regions merge into one broad cap, whereas the farther regions
> remain distinct through 13,000 s of recovery. This contrast persists on the
> finer mesh; the two-cap pattern also withstands the specified small conserved
> redistribution over a further 13,000 s.

The near-matched 1-s/10-s activation belongs in adjacent prose or a compact
linked trace, because the main heatmaps do not resolve those seconds. The main
profiles already convey the useful physical observation: nearly the same
whole-ring active amount is organized into one broad region or two narrower
regions. Active Rac is not itself conserved; active-plus-inactive Rac is.

This intervention holds the incumbent state, circumference and total material
fixed within each mesh while changing cue position. It tests spatial
interaction after activation and provides a useful separation counterpart to
the incumbent-scale candidate. Both periodic routes change, from 12/28 to
16/24 µm, and the cue samples a slightly different basal chemical landscape.
Nearly matched early activation constrains a simple activation-delay account;
it does not isolate a diffusivity, transported inhibitory signal or unique
reaction pathway responsible for valley filling.

The broader biological gap remains: two retained Rac caps do not demonstrate
a persistent single migration direction. This fixed-circumference experiment
also does not directly enlarge a cell. It can illustrate a physical candidate
for how separation changes spatial competition, but observed migration
summaries, conditional OU persistence estimates and 1D polarity patterns must
remain distinct. Selecting it as a main-text figure is a narrative choice
about that bounded mechanism example, not proof that increased separation
explains the WGD migration phenotype.

## Anticipated user critique

**Anticipated user critique — hypothetical, not an actual user quotation:**
“Fine, the grid is not creating the contrast, and a small transfer between the
sites has not made one disappear. But do not call that stable coexistence.
Most of the perturbation disappears immediately, and the small remainder is
still changing. The interesting point is that both places activate and then
the nearer ones fill the gap while the farther ones stay separate. Tell me
what physical process that supports, and why it belongs beside the enlargement
result. Two fronts by themselves do not explain more persistent migration.
Keep the numerical checks in the supplement and let me read the actual
spatial response in the main figure.”

The completed checks answer the numerical-artifact and bounded perturbation
questions adequately. The current fields support separation-dependent
reorganization after activation. They do not yet identify a unique chemical
cause or the behavioral link to migration persistence. State those boundaries
directly; another generic duration-versus-distance plot would not resolve them.
