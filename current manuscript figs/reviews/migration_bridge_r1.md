# Fresh review: proposed bridge to freely migrating cells

**Judgment: ready for discussion after three focused wording corrections.** The bridge offers a useful experimental distinction: enlargement could change the establishment of alternative activity, its subsequent competition with an incumbent, or both. Separation then modifies the later interaction. This fits the integrated figure and is more informative than another cue-duration prediction. It does not yet predict migration persistence from the modeled chemistry.

Reviewed the actual [bridge](../migration_consequences_bridge_20261017.md) and [integrated draft](../integrated_framework_candidate_20261017.md), with the repository status, four-assay README and migration evidence context. No author assets, code or experiments were changed.

## What is shown, and what remains proposed

The existing results show more incumbent active material in five retained preparations; model-specific equal-cue gain and conditional repair in Goryachev versus Wave-pinning; and distinct Holmes interaction outcomes, including a fixed-state position intervention. They do not show spontaneous site-birth rates, challenger-conditioned incumbent repair, free-cell turns or a general increase in incumbent stability. The bridge mostly preserves these distinctions correctly.

## Three corrections that matter

1. **Preserve the proposed mediating pathway.** Row 2 currently asks for conditioning on both sites' widths, peaks and integrated amounts. Incumbent organization is precisely how size is proposed to affect fate: matching it away and then requiring a remaining size effect would test a different hypothesis. First compare fate across size at comparable challenger burden and starting fractional incumbent damage; then ask whether measured incumbent amount/organization accounts for the association. Treat separation as a modifier. Such matching may have limited overlap; do not imply that every listed variable must be matched simultaneously. Likewise, matching detectable Rac excursions by amplitude can condition on an activation response already affected by substrate availability. It estimates establishment among those observed excursions, not activation probability under equal upstream triggers. Keep that distinction explicit without prescribing a full statistical study.

2. **Keep both framework branches in the lead.** “Persistence despite continued formation” is a compelling possible signature, but it prematurely selects the post-activation branch. Lead instead with: “Enlargement may reduce challenger establishment and/or reduce replacement after activation; separation may modify the latter.” Continued activity-site births with fewer replacements would specifically favor a post-activation contribution. Fewer detectable births alone would remain ambiguous between fewer upstream triggers, smaller responses and failed detection. The proposed physical-width detection rules and perimeter-time denominators are valuable; additionally state that spatial resolution, temporal sampling and detection sensitivity must be comparable across size. Report both exposure-normalized and per-cell rates, as the draft already does.

3. **Do not let conditional repair stand in for natural-damage recovery.** The global inactive-profile withdrawal and exact return contains neither an active challenger nor a local active-site injury. Its immediate repair-per-loss result motivates a rebound hypothesis; it does not establish recovery from spontaneous damage or predict replacement probabilities. Retain fractional damage and rebound as optional mechanistic observations, and explicitly label this translation untested. A null result in migrating cells would challenge that translation, without refuting the native chemical assay.

## The two most useful readouts

- **Challenger establishment:** incipient activity excursions per available nonincumbent perimeter-time, together with the fraction becoming distinct sustained sites under common detection rules. This separates available exposure from success after detection; upstream trigger rates remain unknown unless independently measured.
- **Fate after establishment versus site geometry:** replacement, extinction, merger and two retained sites, related to incumbent amount/organization and both physical boundary routes. Relate these outcomes to independently measured boundary motion and cell turns. This is discriminating because a retained incumbent, merged activity and two active regions can have different movement consequences; chemical survival alone is insufficient.

The draft handles geometry honestly: retain physical edge gaps alongside center separations, both circular routes and boundary arclength versus straight-line distance. The exact C40 comparison supports position-dependent fate over a finite window. It supplies neither a monotonic gap law nor a migration direction, and the ring has no privileged front-to-back axis.

**Suggested compact figure footer:** “In freely migrating cells, test whether enlargement changes challenger establishment, subsequent incumbent replacement, or both, and whether boundary separation modifies fate. Activity-site outcomes must be linked independently to cell turns.”

**Anticipated user critique:** “These readouts now fit the same framework, but show me which step changes: challenger establishment, incumbent replacement after activation, or movement after the chemical event.” This is the right remaining question; it does not require a new campaign to make the present figure coherent.
