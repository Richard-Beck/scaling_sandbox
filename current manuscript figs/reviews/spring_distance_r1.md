# Fresh review: Spring challenger-distance pilot

Reviewed 17 October 2026 from the [result note](../../polarity1d/manuscript/SPRING_RING_DISTANCE_PILOT_20261017.md), its embedded seven-panel PNG, the [declared closure](../../polarity1d/manuscript/SPRING_RING_CLOSURE_20261017.md), frozen operators, manifest, audits, and all three primary saved full-field trajectories. I independently recomputed local maxima, half-contrast components, early target readouts and final translated-field residuals from the saved states. No integration was run and no authored result was edited.

**Accept the pilot as evidence that challenger location changes the early route through polarity competition in this declared Spring analogue.** It supports prompt formation at every target, near-site bridging, and longer coexistence of separated maxima at the farther targets. It does not yet establish a pure distance law or explain increased migration persistence after WGD-associated enlargement. The most important wording correction is to distinguish a bridge joining two active regions from extinction of an old peak that stays near its original position.

**Anticipated user critique:** This is more useful: a new challenger appears straight away, and then the old site behaves differently depending on where we put it. But show me whether the two sites actually join or the original one disappears. The near cue is only just outside a very broad incumbent, so I would not call this a travel-time effect. And do not make the higher activity left in the old region sound like a surviving incumbent when the final cap has simply moved over part of that region. What does this tell us about a larger cell beyond this one competition transient?

## Decisive scientific points

1. **Separate connectivity, resolved maxima and old-site extinction.** The near case has two above-half-contrast components at the sampled time 0.1, but they are connected by 0.2. Nevertheless it retains two resolved maxima through 0.8. The old maximum's prominence is 0.0563 at 0.8 and 0.0477 at 0.9; the stated 0.05 criterion therefore changes its classification in that interval. At 0.4 and 0.5, the two half-contrast components remain separate through 6.6 and 6.9. Their subsequent loss is caused by the old maximum falling below the frozen threshold, rather than the two regions forming a bridge. At time 5 the old maxima are still at the original peak cell, with concentrations 1.0690 and 1.0812. Their resolved prominences persist through 7.7 and 8.2. These fields support **near-site bridging versus separated challenger growth followed by old-site extinction**. Blanket language about sites “merging” would lose the useful distinction. A resolved-peak lifetime is also not an exact time at which every local maximum ceases to exist.

2. **The shared 0.2 mean crossing does not establish identical activation kinetics.** The first recorded target-region mean above one is 0.2 in every case, bracketing each crossing in the sampled interval (0.1, 0.2]. All three already possess a challenger local maximum above one at 0.1: 1.0946, 1.0266 and 1.0233. Their target-region means at that sample are 0.9732, 0.8974 and 0.8932. Thus the robust observation is prompt challenger formation at the available resolution, with no activation failure under this dose. The target-mean readout is neither a takeover gate nor a test of stable challenger persistence after a brief pulse. It should not be used to claim exact separation-independent latency.

3. **Separation is coupled to the incumbent flank and initial local chemistry.** The original half-contrast core contains 95 of the 200 cells, nearly half of the ring. Avoiding its binary mask does not put each cue into the same chemical background. The initial target-region means are 0.7799, 0.7119 and 0.7082; the near cue samples appreciable incumbent tail. Initial inactive means differ only slightly, approximately 1.01454–1.01461, but the local active field and its gradients enter nonlinear feedback and diffusion. The principal qualitative difference is between the near site and the two farther sites; changing 0.4 to 0.5 adds only about 0.5 sampled units to the resolved two-peak interval. This is consistent with a near-flank interaction, without identifying its cause. The result is a valid same-state location intervention. It does not isolate molecular travel time, exclude initial-tail interaction, or establish a monotone distance law from three points. Changing location on a ring also changes both available arcs, of lengths approximately 0.613/1.431, 0.818/1.227 and 1.022/1.022 initially.

4. **Fixed original-core averages cannot identify incumbent survival or motion.** The core spans a broad material region. At the final time its mean is 0.8893, 0.7883 and 0.7316, while every final state has only one resolved maximum at its target. Independent comparison with the appropriately integer-shifted initial state reproduces maximum active-field residuals of 9.39–9.44 × 10⁻¹¹ and inactive-field residuals below 9.7 × 10⁻¹³. Circumference also returns within 7.1 × 10⁻¹⁴ of its original value. These are the same basal chemical profile translated to three targets within numerical tolerance. The different old-region averages mainly reflect how that translated broad profile overlaps the fixed mask. They do not show a residual second incumbent, greater intrinsic old-site strength in the near case, or molecular identity retained there. Use local peak position and prominence alongside these averages when discussing old-site loss.

5. **The closure permits a location effect but does not supply native spatial mechanics.** Circumference is coupled to the spatial mean of the nonlinear Rac force switch. Reordering an otherwise unchanged Rac distribution leaves that mean unchanged: this closure has no direct force-transmission distance or local deformation field. Location can change chemical interaction, which changes the field distribution and hence the global extension feedback. The dynamic circumference trace documents that coupled response; it cannot assign the observed coexistence difference to mechanical propagation. Its largest contraction is approximately 0.128% of the shared initial circumference. Keep “shared initial circumference” explicit rather than describing a clamped-size experiment. The declared global-extension analogue is scientifically reviewable on its own terms; native coefficients do not make it the authors' 2D mechanics.

6. **A long-cue transient is a candidate mechanism observation, not a WGD persistence result.** The 1000-unit maintained cue is much longer than the first approximately 10 units of old-site competition. Every target subsequently retains the same basal cap during the 13000-unit cue-free continuation. This establishes the common long-cue fate at one resting circumference, dose and mesh. It does not measure the minimum successful pulse, spontaneous polarity changes, directional migration memory or the effect of increasing cell size. The WGD link remains a hypothesis: enlargement could alter incumbent architecture or challenger separation, and those changes could alter competition. The observed migration summaries, conditional OU velocity-memory estimates and this polarity-site transient remain distinct readouts.

The existing saved-state audit is adequate for this bounded development reading: exact shared starts and phase continuity, conservation, complete fields, stationarity and the reflection control address substantive implementation concerns. They do not replace a spatial-convergence assessment before quantitative mesh-independent claims. No new parameter search or duration campaign is required to state the result above.

## What belongs in main-text candidate 2

Use this pilot as one clearly labeled example of **what changes after a challenger forms**, potentially alongside the other circular constructions. Candidate 1 can address the separate enlargement–incumbent-architecture hypothesis. Candidate 2 should ask whether moving a challenger on a connected domain changes its initiation or the subsequent competition. The Spring evidence contributes to the latter branch.

A compact main candidate would contain:

- A geometry schematic showing the same starting incumbent, common starting circumference, fixed physical cue support and the two routes around the circle. Distinguish the incumbent core from its nonzero flank.
- Early full-field histories through 10 native units for the near and opposite targets, with a common raw Rac scale. These are the observations that distinguish bridge formation, separate sites and stationary old-site decay.
- A small set of raw spatial profiles at 0, 0.2 and 5, or an equivalent readable peak/valley trace. The 0.2 snapshot exposes connected near activity despite two maxima; the time-5 snapshot exposes the separated old and new maxima in the far case. Mark the frozen half-contrast level and define the prominence convention in the caption.
- One compact quantitative comparison that keeps prompt target formation and the duration of two resolved maxima distinct. Show sampling brackets and a declared prominence threshold; do not label either as a reversal threshold.

The intermediate 0.4 case, complete 14000-unit fields, broad fixed-region averages, circumference trace, exact translated-state comparisons, physical-amount audit and reflection control belong in linked support. A brief explicit statement in the main caption can retain dynamic extension feedback without making the circumference panel compete with the early spatial evidence. Retain all three cases in support so selecting near and far does not hide the small 0.4–0.5 difference.

The current seven-panel figure is a useful development evidence sheet. Its main visual limitation is scientific emphasis: the very long continuation occupies most of the logarithmic history, while the decisive 0.1–8.3 interval and the distinction between connectivity and maxima receive little space. An early-time main layout would fix that. A new simulation is not needed for this figure decision.
