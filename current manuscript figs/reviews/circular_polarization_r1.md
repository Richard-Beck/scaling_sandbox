# Fresh review: circular polarization, first milestone

Reviewed 17 October 2026 from the [result package](../circular_polarization_result_20261017.md), its rendered five-panel PNG, source adapters, preparation audits and deBelly continuation records. This review uses the current manuscript and four-assay instructions. No simulations were run and no result package was edited.

**Accept this as the first feasibility milestone for the five declared constructions.** There is a qualified cue-free site in every case, with local parameters close to the existing interval work. The qualification is more substantial than a final Rac peak: the retained audits inspect the other fields, conserved amounts and a prospective cue-free holdout. The correct next question is whether a challenger can be introduced without changing the incumbent beforehand, and what response changes when that challenger moves. The current result does not answer that question or yet earn a main-text mechanism panel.

**Anticipated user critique:** Fine, they polarize. For the chemical models that was the expected part. What have we retained when we put the mechanical models on a circle, and can we now move the challenger without changing anything else? The useful figure would tell us what greater separation does to challenger formation or incumbent loss. Five successful preparation profiles do not yet give us that physical explanation.

## What the milestone actually establishes

| Construction | Supported result | Limit that matters next |
|---|---|---|
| Wave-pinning, Goryachev, Holmes 3 | Native local reactions, diffusivities and pool means support one persistent site with conservative periodic diffusion at circumference 40. Independent fresh preparations qualify on 400 and 800 cells. | This is one prepared branch at one circumference. It does not establish competitor formation, response accuracy on the two meshes, or robustness across parameters. |
| Spring analogue | Periodic chemistry coupled to the specified mean-force circumference equation supports one persistent site; circumference and both chemical fields settle. | The mean-force equation is a new closure. Native coefficients and uniform-field agreement do not establish native spatial mechanics. |
| deBelly point-cut loop | The specified retained cut and tangential mechanics support one stationary, two-flank incumbent with one full-loop MCA budget. The exact-state continuation qualifies it. | Evolution was restricted to reflection symmetry. A second cut and an asymmetric challenger require a new full-domain coupling implementation. |

This is a useful completion of the user's first step. Requiring the authors' complete 2D mechanics before accepting it would enlarge that step unnecessarily. Conversely, “all five native models polarize on periodic domains” would hide the two mechanical adaptations. The result package's phrase “five declared circular constructions” is the defensible common claim.

The preparation cues are sufficiently native-like for feasibility, but their differences must remain explicit. In particular, Goryachev's ring preparation uses peak 0.1, whereas the current interval patch **response** tests use 6.4. The raised-cosine support on a ring also differs from an endpoint patch. Success therefore supports unchanged local kinetics under a suitable preparation; it is not a matched-dose comparison of ease of polarization. The three chemical [400-cell](../../polarity1d/manuscript/data/runs/ring_preparation_pilot_20261017/results/independent_audit.json) and [800-cell audits](../../polarity1d/manuscript/data/runs/ring_preparation_pilot_N800_20261017/results/independent_audit.json) support that bounded reading.

## Spring: preserve the distinction between coefficients and mechanics

The [closure note](../../polarity1d/manuscript/SPRING_RING_CLOSURE_20261017.md) states the change clearly: endpoint force sampling becomes the spatial mean of the same nonlinear Rac-to-force law. Retaining extension feedback and dilution is a reasonable minimal construction. The amount conservation and complete homogeneous-derivative checks address real implementation risks. The [saved-state audit](../../polarity1d/manuscript/data/runs/spring_ring_preparation_pilot_20261017/results/audit.json) supports preparation feasibility despite the recorded internal BDF warning; that warning alone is not grounds to discard the finite, stationary trajectory.

The important scientific limitation is structural. At a given circumference, rearranging an unchanged distribution of Rac around the circle leaves the mean force unchanged. This closure has no direct mechanical measure of separation, no local deformation field, and no native traction-mediated spatial coupling. Location may still matter through chemical diffusion and the resulting changes in the activity distribution, which then feed back globally. A later Spring distance effect would be a result of that coupled analogue. It could not demonstrate distance-dependent transmission of native elastic force. The original 2D model motivates distributed forcing, but does not derive this particular circumference rule.

There is no need to try several alternative Spring closures now. Keep this one explicit and determine whether its bounded challenger response is informative before investing in a more elaborate model.

## deBelly: a valid preparation with an unresolved asymmetric continuation

The [protocol](../../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/distance_ring_protocol_20261017/debelly_single_incumbent_prototype.md) makes legitimate, consequential choices: a zero-width cut opens with the cue, removes no mass, persists after cue withdrawal, and retains local Rac/tension-dependent commands. Tangential displacement and velocity on a fixed reference circle reduce to the existing scalar arclength equations; the normal constraint supplies the missing normal reaction. This is a constrained line model embedded on a circle. Its success does not establish a curvature effect or normal contour dynamics. In this symmetric case, wrapping the interval around a circle is largely a geometric interpretation of the same bulk dynamics.

That is acceptable for the approved first pilot. The authors' [axisymmetric driver](https://github.com/VirtualEmbryo/mechanochemical_polarization/blob/cd51f0f91e039929abac2b6ce466ff2edbb11b67/mechanochemical_2D_axisymmetric/src/MechanochemicalAxisymmetricVector.jl) and [weak forms](https://github.com/VirtualEmbryo/mechanochemical_polarization/blob/cd51f0f91e039929abac2b6ce466ff2edbb11b67/mechanochemical_2D_axisymmetric/src/WeakForms.jl) support the cut-boundary interpretation and identify the curved mechanics being omitted. They supply a single leading-edge construction, axisymmetric weights and geometric terms; they do not already specify two movable planar gaps. Their leading-edge command references opposite-pole Rac. The prototype instead preserves the manuscript's fixed rest-based threshold. That declared choice is especially important when two sites can be active.

The [separate verification audit](../../polarity1d/manuscript/data/runs/debelly_planar_loop_preparation_20261017/continuation/independent_audit.json) resolves the original preparation's qualification without changing its parameters or tolerances. The 18–20 ks prewindows pass, the prescribed 20–30 ks prospective holdout passes, and all eleven restart fields agree exactly before evolution. Keeping the original 20 ks capped audit unresolved is correct. This is a verification extension of the same preparation, not a second parameter attempt.

Two qualifications remain essential:

1. **Stationarity is established within the symmetric subspace.** The half-loop solver excludes antisymmetric motion and flank differences by construction. The manufactured half/full operator agreement validates the reduction for symmetric states; mirrored output is not an independently evolved full-loop trajectory. Neither establishes stability to asymmetric disturbances. This does not invalidate the prepared state, but it matters directly once an off-axis challenger is added.
2. **The spatial full-loop export is not the complete restart state.** Direct inspection confirms that [full_loop_checkpoint.npz](../../polarity1d/manuscript/data/runs/debelly_planar_loop_preparation_20261017/continuation/full_loop_checkpoint.npz) contains seven reconstructed fields and geometry, while [half_loop_checkpoint.npz](../../polarity1d/manuscript/data/runs/debelly_planar_loop_preparation_20261017/continuation/half_loop_checkpoint.npz) also contains the four raw/relaxed boundary-controller fields. The next solver must import those controller values, with the correct reflected signs. Reconstructing Rac/MCA alone or resetting the boundary controller would change the mechanical incumbent at restart.

The **essential unfinished element before deBelly distance trials** is therefore the two-cut coupling rule, not another preparation search. Four arc ends represent two sites. Once the arcs have unequal lengths, two flanks belonging to the same site need not have equal Rac, tension or controller history. The implementation must state whether those quantities remain independent or how they are coupled, and how chemical fields communicate through the unmodelled protrusive region. Single-site symmetry cannot choose that rule for us.

One MCA correction must integrate both arcs with their physical quadrature weights. Correcting each arc to its own starting mass would prevent native competition for a common pool. The present symmetric half-loop correction correctly represents a shared full-loop budget, but that algebraic identity does not validate a future two-arc implementation. Moreover, uniform MCA replacement and quasi-static mechanics are global communication channels. A measured separation effect could be informative without representing a finite molecular travel time. Calling it a transport delay would require additional evidence.

## What a small distance test must distinguish

All challenger locations must restart the same complete prepared state. On an intact chemical ring, this is already well specified by cyclically shifting the same cue array. Use integer cell shifts so peak, support and integrated cue remain exactly equal; report both routes, of lengths *d* and *C − d*. The experiment changes their pair at fixed circumference, rather than varying a single isolated communication path. Exclude cue overlap with the incumbent's fixed basal core and report the edge-to-edge separation as well as the distance between centres. The broad Wave-pinning and Holmes sites make that distinction substantive.

For deBelly, opening the challenger cut is itself a mechanical and transport perturbation. Consistent cue-triggered opening satisfies the user's timing preference, but does not remove that perturbation. A gap-only control at each of a very small number of pilot locations is needed to interpret the cue-induced increment. Do not pre-install different cuts, settle separate baselines and call them a shared incumbent. A transparent pre-cue interface must reproduce the original one-cut dynamics before it opens.

The useful observations are early challenger activation, incumbent retention in the original fixed region, and complete spatial histories showing the number and motion of active regions. A peak moving towards the cue is a different outcome from a distinct competitor growing while the incumbent remains. Circular order or an endpoint score alone will not distinguish them. Use fixed-frame profiles and peak/valley trajectories, with translation alignment as a diagnostic only. An eventual finite-cue survival test answers another question and should follow a readable maintained-cue pilot, rather than start a duration-threshold campaign immediately.

## Figure decision and next action

The five-panel overview is readable and appropriate in this development record. Raw amplitudes and explicit model names help; the Spring and deBelly titles already signal the adaptations. Goryachev's cropped site view does not, on its own, demonstrate uniqueness around the full circumference, and the primary-field snapshots do not display full-field settling. Those are acceptable limitations when the linked preparation evidence carries those checks. If reused outside this record, add a compact geometry schematic and a whole-ring Goryachev view. There is no reason to redesign it into a main figure now.

For the intended one or two main-text candidates, the stronger current candidate remains **incumbent architecture linked to a measured consequence**: representative profiles showing width versus concentration, followed by the supported restoring-response and challenger-activation diagnostics. The supplied mechanism PDF provides that evidence. It does not yet show that greater incumbent material causes greater resistance, and its Wave-pinning/Goryachev contrast should remain visible. The circular experiment is new evidence and would justify a second candidate only if its profiles reveal an interpretable initiation, competition or drift outcome as separation changes. A flat distance response would also constrain the hypothesis; it should not trigger a search for parameters that manufacture the proposed story.

**Prioritized next action:** write the minimal asymmetric two-cut deBelly coupling and exact state-transfer specification, then verify that its transparent interface preserves the saved one-cut state before opening. After that, run only a bounded near/far maintained-cue pilot, using the existing checkpoints and a gap-only control for deBelly. Inspect full spatial histories before choosing any sweep or main-figure claim. More preparation lengths, alternative Spring closures and broad parameter searches are not prerequisites for that decision.
