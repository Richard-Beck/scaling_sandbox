# Independent review: chemical-ring separation candidate

17 October 2026. Fresh-context scientific and figure critique. This review
examines the completed chemical-ring pilot and the compact Holmes candidate.
No integrations were run and no authored result-package files were edited.
Any later perturbation or challenge-mesh results require a separate assessment.

## Decision

**The compact Holmes d = 12 versus d = 16 comparison is a credible main-text
candidate about spatial reorganization after similar challenger activation.**
Its result is merger into one broad cap versus two narrower caps that remain
distinct through the recorded 13,000-s cue-free window. The three-model grid
is useful supporting evidence. It dilutes the message when used as the primary
figure because it combines different concentration scales, cue strengths and
response modes.

Before selecting the compact figure, prioritize a bounded challenge-mesh check
at those two distances. A conserved-pool asymmetry perturbation is useful for
testing finite-window robustness, but a stable-branch investigation is not
necessary for the stated claim. No broad distance, duration or parameter scan
is needed to make this result reviewable.

This is not yet a demonstrated explanation of greater migration persistence
in enlarged WGD-associated cells. The ring experiment changes separation at
one circumference. Two surviving activity caps do not establish a persistent
single migration direction, and may require a different behavioral
interpretation. This distinction should remain visible when the figure is
placed beside a length-response or empirical migration figure.

## What the fields actually show

Every distance within a model starts from the same untouched N800 basal array
on a circumference-40 periodic domain. The cue has physical support 3 and
remains on for 1000 native time units, followed by 13,000 units without external
input. Holmes time is in seconds. Cue peaks are 0.1, 6.4 and 1 for Wave-pinning,
Goryachev and Holmes respectively; cross-model activation times and strengths
are not matched. The exact same initial checkpoint was independently confirmed
for the two selected Holmes workers during this review.

The decisive Holmes contrast occurs after activation. Raw means over the same
width-3 cue region give:

| Observable | d = 12 | d = 16 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Basal cue-region active Rac | 0.366669 | 0.365067 |
| Cue-region active Rac at 1 s | 0.659411 | 0.657735 |
| Cue-region active Rac at 10 s | 1.693813 | 1.688789 |
| Cue-region active Rac at withdrawal, 1000 s | 1.962386 | 1.962466 |
| Final distinct local maxima with prominence ≥ 0.05 | 1 | 2 |
| Final maximum locations relative to the initial incumbent | 5.85 | −0.10 and 16.00 |
| Final fixed old-core mean / basal | 0.7552 | 0.6541 |

The local native fields support prompt challenger activation in both cases.
The sampled early responses are very similar; a large difference in initial
activation is not the evident explanation for the different outcomes.

At d = 12, both sites are still visible at withdrawal and 100 s afterward.
A bridge then fills the intervening low-activity region. By the 500-s recovery
sample, only one sufficiently prominent maximum remains; the final cap lies
between the two original centers. Calling this extinction of an unchanged old
front followed by takeover at the cue would misdescribe the displayed history.
The final profile also closely matches a translated basal cap.

At d = 16, the activity near both original sites remains separated by a valley
through the recorded recovery. The final peak heights are approximately
1.484687 and 1.484464. Their local half-contrast widths are about 8.523 and
7.921, compared with the one-cap basal width of 16.538. Thus the old site
survives as a narrower region; it does not retain its original spatial extent.

The basal integrated active-Rac amount is 33.129311. It returns to 33.129311 at
d = 12 and ends at 33.045086 at d = 16, a decrease of about 0.25% from basal.
These are observed active amounts, **not** a claim that active Rac itself is
conserved. Total active-plus-inactive Rac and the corresponding Rho pool are
the conserved quantities. The useful physical observation is that nearly the
same active amount occupies one broad region or two narrower regions.

The old-core scalar reverses the apparent interpretation: it is higher in the
merged d = 12 case than in the d = 16 case that retains a distinct old site.
An integral over a fixed original region measures regional activity, not the
identity or survival of a front. This is a strong reason to keep full spatial
profiles in the main candidate.

The other models provide different supporting examples:

- Wave-pinning d = 8 rapidly makes a connected activity region, with movement
  and merger. The other distances show two-maxima transients before ending
  with one displaced cap. A single endpoint or original-core-loss time hides
  these differences.
- Goryachev develops a distinct cue-local maximum while the original maximum
  falls, ending with one cap near the cue at every tested distance. At 1 native
  time unit the new peak is about 0.389 against an incumbent peak near 64.95.
  It is present in the native fields but nearly invisible on the shared
  0–65 color scale. This grid should not be read as delayed absence of local
  activation merely because the second site is initially dark.

## What separation means here

The intervention holds total circumference, conserved material pools, native
local kinetics and the entire incumbent state fixed. This successfully removes
the change in total material that accompanies the original length comparison.
It tests how the same incumbent-organized spatial state responds when the same
local cue is applied elsewhere.

Both ring routes matter: the d = 12 case has routes 12 and 28, whereas d = 16
has routes 16 and 24. The nearer edge of the width-3 cue is respectively about
2.23 and 6.23 units beyond the basal incumbent half-contrast edge. Consequently,
the experiment changes the gap between activity regions and both periodic
interaction routes. It is not a measurement of travel along a single path.

Changing cue location also samples the basal chemical landscape. Here that
limitation is measurable rather than fatal: the Holmes basal cue-region Rac
means differ by only about 0.44%, and the initial inactive-Rac concentrations
at the two cue centers differ by less than 0.000001. The nearly matching early
responses further constrain a simple local-activation explanation. They do not
prove that one pathway, diffusivity or front interaction alone causes merger.

The supported physical statement is therefore narrower and more useful than
“farther requires a longer cue”: **after similar local activation, separation
changes whether the activity regions merge during recovery or remain distinct
over the measured window, while drawing on the same conserved pools.** This
can motivate a spatial competition mechanism. A statement about an inhibitory
signal taking longer to travel, or about a specific molecular transport
bottleneck, would require an additional measured field or intervention.

## Symmetry, slow competition and the minimum follow-up

The reflected d = −12 cases adequately address left–right numerical bias for
this pilot. Positive/negative controls agree after reflection to approximately
3.6 × 10⁻¹⁴ for Wave-pinning, 1.8 × 10⁻⁸ for Goryachev and 1.3 × 10⁻¹³ for
Holmes on the stated fixed field scales. Initial reflection asymmetries are
also recorded. These are numerical controls, not independent biological
replicates. More reflected distances are not a priority.

Using d = 16 in the main candidate is preferable to relying only on d = 20:
the two routes are unequal, the final sites are not antipodal, and their
heights and widths are not identical. Exact antipodal symmetry therefore
cannot by itself explain the displayed two-site result. Near-equal plateau
heights alone still do not establish stable coexistence.

The late full-field diagnostics pass, but the surviving patterns continue to
change slightly. For Holmes d = 16, the fixed-basin signed active-amount
imbalance changes from −0.0204013 to −0.0204061 over the last 1000 s, with
fitted drift approximately −4.78 × 10⁻⁹ per second. This is a finite-window
description, not an eigenvalue. A very slow competitive mode remains possible.

The follow-up should be proportionate to the claim:

1. **Prioritize two challenge-mesh checks at d = 12 and d = 16.** The existing
   N400/N800 preparation comparison does not establish challenge accuracy.
   Preserve cue support, peak, physical centers, phase durations, native
   parameters and conserved-pool means. Compare full profiles and the
   qualitative merger/two-site outcome; report any difference in merger time.
   If a remapped restart is used, document conservative remapping separately
   from a newly qualified basal state. The same-array condition applies within
   each mesh and cannot literally apply between different meshes.
2. **If inexpensive, perturb the d = 16 two-site state with a small signed
   transfer of active Rac between sites, conserving the total Rac pool, and
   run a bounded cue-free continuation.** Opposite perturbation signs are
   useful. If starting at the 13,000-s endpoint, include a matched unperturbed
   continuation so all trajectories share a clock and observation window.
   Record perturbation size, spatial weighting, unchanged Rho fields and exact
   pool accounting. Survival would show robustness over that added window;
   loss would expose competitive sensitivity. Neither result alone supplies
   a stable-branch theorem. This test is optional for a strictly finite-window
   observation and should not delay delivery of a clearly scoped draft.

No duration optimization, dense distance scan or global bifurcation campaign
is required before evaluating this candidate. If the claim remains “two sites
were observed for 13,000 s,” an unlimited longevity test would pursue a stronger
question than the one the figure needs to answer.

## Figure recommendation

The compact four-panel figure is substantially clearer than the full survey.
The matched heatmaps show bridge formation; basal, withdrawal and final
profiles reveal cap narrowing and the final location. The revised version's
common origin at the original incumbent makes the unchanged initial state
easier to compare. The Holmes µm, µM and seconds labels agree with its native
unit metadata. Keep the raw common Rac color scale.

The d = 12 profile 200 s after withdrawal shows that both narrowed regions
initially survive after the cue ends; it does not itself show the filled
bridge. The gap minimum is then 0.428, rising to 0.518 at 400 s and 1.462 at
450 s. Either label the 200-s profile with that purpose or use a later merger
snapshot if the bridge needs additional emphasis. This is an editorial choice,
not a reason to add panels automatically.

Three small additions would strengthen the result without adding simulations:

- State “circumference 40; routes 12/28 and 16/24; width-3 cue, peak 1” in the
  caption or a small geometry sketch. The revised common origin is at the
  original incumbent; the two plotted edges remain periodically connected.
- Put the near-matched 1-s or 10-s activation observation in the adjacent prose
  or a compact linked trace. The current 0–2000-s heatmaps do not resolve the
  first few seconds well enough to carry that claim by themselves.
- Make clear that the heatmaps show only the first 1000 s of recovery, whereas
  the teal profile comes from 13,000 s after withdrawal. Keep the full-history
  companion linked and label the final profile as the recorded endpoint.

An appropriate leading sentence is: “In Holmes, the same local cue activates
Rac at similar early levels at both tested positions; after withdrawal the
nearer regions merge, while the farther regions remain separate for the
13,000-s observation.” It should not be expanded into a general distance law
or a direct claim about WGD migration.

## Anticipated user critique

**Anticipated user critique — hypothetical, not an actual user quotation:**
“This is closer to what I wanted: the second site activates, and the difference
is what happens to the two sites afterward. But what physical process makes
the nearer sites merge? Are the farther sites really coexisting, or have you
just stopped before one wins? And why would two surviving fronts explain more
persistent migration rather than competing directions?”

The current data answer the first part at the level of the spatial response:
prompt activation, narrowing, bridge formation and finite-window separation
are directly visible. A challenge-mesh check strengthens the numerical basis;
a bounded imbalance test can strengthen the finite-window robustness claim.
The data do not yet identify a unique causal pathway or provide the behavioral
bridge from multiple Rac caps to migration persistence. That boundary should
be stated rather than replaced with an additional threshold plot.
