# Independent review: three spatial fates after challenger activation

17 October 2026. Fresh-context scientific and editorial critique of the
[six-panel candidate 2, version 2](../main_candidate_2_20261017.md) and its
[actual rendered figure](../../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/main_candidate_2_v2_20261017/main_candidate_2_v2.png).
This reviews the combined figure independently of the earlier size and numerical
reviews. The regenerated image and updated candidate overview were inspected
after the author’s final corrections. No integration was run and no candidate
files were edited by this reviewer.

## Decision

**This is a substantially stronger main-text candidate than an endpoint or
cue-duration curve for the proposed question about spatial competition.** It
shows three physically different responses that such a curve can conflate:
old-site extinction with a cue-centered survivor, filling of the gap into one
intersite cap, and two distinct sites retained through the observation window.
The six panels are justified: the histories identify how the patterns form,
while the profiles reveal the incumbent architecture and the later outcome.

The most convincing controlled comparison remains **C40,d12 versus C40,d16**:
both restart the exact same fine-mesh basal state. Adding C30,d12 gives the
figure a visible enlargement intervention and a third informative response
mode. It does not convert the figure into a causal explanation in terms of
incumbent width, a diffusion-distance mechanism, or an explanation of increased
migration persistence. Those are separate scientific steps. The candidate is
acceptable for discussing a bounded spatial result; final main-text selection
still depends on the message agreed with the user.

## What this figure actually establishes

The three compact native archives and exported profiles support the labels.
The C30,d12 terminal maximum is at 11.95 µm, close to the cue at 12 µm; the old
position returns to approximately 0.365 µM. Its history shows a distinct old
region disappearing near its starting position before the survivor broadens.
Calling this replacement is reasonable when defined as that spatial sequence.
It does not track molecular identity or show the incumbent translating into
the new cap.

C40,d12 instead fills the intervening valley and ends with one broad maximum
near 5.85 µm. Active Rac at the original position is still approximately
1.478 µM, but there is no separate old cap. Thus a high final old-core signal
would misclassify this case if interpreted as incumbent survival. The figure
shows why spatial fields are needed, rather than merely asserting their value.

C40,d16 ends with resolved maxima near −0.10 and 16.00 µm. The original cap
has narrowed while retaining approximately its basal plateau concentration.
Two retained sites are a clear finite-window observation. They do not mean
that the original state is preserved, that the pattern is asymptotically
stable, or that one migration direction persists.

All three withdrawal profiles show substantial cue-local activation. The
figure therefore locates an important part of the contrast in what happens
after activation, including after cue withdrawal. The 0–4000-s heatmaps do
not resolve first-second kinetics, so “all activate” is directly visible,
whereas “identical activation kinetics” would need the linked early traces.

A more informative leading sentence than “activation does not determine the
spatial outcome” would be:

> At fixed native pool densities, increasing circumference from 30 to 40 µm
> changes old-site extinction into merger for a cue 12 µm away; moving the cue
> to 16 µm on the same larger ring instead leaves two distinct sites through
> 13,000 s of cue-free recovery.

This states the actual two comparisons. It should remain a proposed wording
for discussion, rather than an editorial choice imposed on the manuscript.

## The physical clue is useful, but is not yet a mechanism

The basal profiles make enlargement physically concrete: in this Holmes
regime the incumbent broadens from 12.38 to 16.54 µm at almost unchanged
plateau concentration, approximately 1.484 µM. A comparably activated
challenger leaves an original-side local half-prominence width of 3.94 µm at
C30,d12 versus 8.16 and 8.23 µm in the two C40 cases at withdrawal. These
measurements are more useful than a generic claim that the larger domain
requires a longer cue. They suggest a hypothesis about how much active-region
architecture remains available after the challenger is established.

That hypothesis must not be promoted into “a wider incumbent maintains itself
better” as a demonstrated causal result. Total Rac/Rho material increases
from 225/93 to 300/124 µM·µm, the complementary route increases from 18 to
28 µm in the d12 comparison, and the freshly prepared chemical state changes.
Native pool densities and local kinetics are fixed; the size intervention is
still meaningful, but it contains several physical consequences of enlargement.
The local width is also a readout of that coupled chemistry.

Most decisively, the two C40 cases have nearly equal old-region widths at
withdrawal yet different subsequent modes. A width or “capacity” measurement
alone cannot explain merger versus separation. The fixed-C comparison supplies
a complementary observation about interaction across the gap. Both periodic
routes change, from 12/28 to 16/24 µm, and moving the cue also samples a
different part of the basal chemical landscape. No uniquely identified
transported inhibitor, diffusion travel time, width threshold, or independent
chemical maintenance rate follows from these panels.

The defensible physical interpretation is therefore **organization of an
established active region and its interaction with another activated region**.
The figure exposes those ingredients in measurable form. It has not isolated
their causal contributions. Active Rac alone is not conserved, so the similar
plateau heights and redistributed widths should not be described as a proven
partition of a fixed active-Rac budget.

## Final corrections and editorial boundary

**The essential presentation corrections are resolved.** The regenerated image
labels “Cue off” at 1000 s and distinguishes the 0–4000-s histories from the
14,000-s-after-onset profiles, explicitly giving their 13,000-s cue-free time.
The candidate and caption now state that the nearly equal C40 withdrawal
widths do not explain their different fates. The current pair overview embeds
the six-panel revision and correctly describes both interventions.

No essential figure correction remains for presenting this as a concrete,
reviewable main-text candidate about altered spatial competition. Three
interpretive boundaries remain essential when choosing the accompanying prose:

- The left-to-middle comparison changes circumference at fixed d12; the
  middle-to-right comparison changes cue location on one exact C40 basal
  state. This is not a complete circumference-by-distance experiment.
- The caption correctly describes periodic edges and both routes. Moving the
  cue changes local chemistry and the route geometry, not a uniquely isolated
  molecular travel time.
- WGD-associated enlargement motivates this test. Two retained Rac caps can
  also encode competing directions; without a motion readout, their retention
  has no demonstrated sign of effect on directional persistence.

The performed controls adequately support discussion of the bounded result;
no additional integration is necessary for that claim. Upgrading it to a
causal width mechanism or a migration mechanism would require new evidence,
rather than stronger wording.

## Supporting evidence and optional polish

The fine-grid C40 contrasts agree in endpoint mode with h = 0.05 µm, while
the merger transient has larger mesh differences. The ±1% local excess-Rac
redistribution continuations were performed on the coarse d16 endpoint, not
the fine profiles in this figure. They support retention under that specified
small redistribution over another finite window. They do not demonstrate
stability or directly qualify C30/C50. The current candidate discloses these
boundaries appropriately; they need not become additional main panels.

C50,d20 usefully confirms two-site retention along the predefined d/C = 0.4
size path and belongs in linked support. Its omission from the six-panel
layout does not hide a contrary result. The archived four-panel version and
the complete distance survey preserve selection context. Neither a broader
parameter survey nor a precise transition distance is necessary to present
these three observations.

Optional presentation improvements are a modest ring/cue schematic, a cue-on
bracket from 0 to 1000 s, and slightly more direct labels for the final modes.
“Old-site extinction; cue-centered cap” is more literal than “Replacement,”
although the shorter existing label works once defined. Keep the common
physical axes, raw concentrations and one heatmap color scale. Additional
rate-budget or stability panels would overcrowd this figure unless the agreed
message changes.

> This is much closer to what I asked for. I can see the old region disappear

> automatically more persistent migration. Let us decide whether the main
> message is altered competition after enlargement or a particular maintenance

The six-panel revision answers the request for a visible size intervention
and physically interpretable spatial evidence. Its next editorial decision
is which bounded message belongs in the manuscript, in collaboration with the
user. Replacing it with an endpoint/timing curve would remove the most useful
evidence and would not resolve the remaining causal or biological gap.
