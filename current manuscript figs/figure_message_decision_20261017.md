# Figure message decision: incumbent scale and competition

For discussion, 17 October 2026. This note connects the [current integrated draft](main_text_figure_candidates_20261017.md) to the [reviewed three-model ring results](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/chemical_ring_functional_20261017/RESULTS.md). It proposes an emphasis for discussion; it does not select a new panel layout.

## The message I would put first

**Incumbents grow with cell size; initial cue gain and conditional repair change in model-specific ways; spatial arrangement modifies the subsequent competition.** A concrete outcome is that an enlarged incumbent can persist despite activation of a challenger. The challenger may subsequently disappear or remain as a second active site. Under the tested ring protocols, Goryachev supplies the first outcome and larger Wave-pinning/Holmes rings supply the second. Here activation means distinct challenger-associated activity, without implying autonomous persistence after withdrawal. This is a conditional result of the declared size and cue interventions, rather than a universal claim about larger cells.

The shared starting observation remains incumbent growth, as in the accepted panel A. Local gain and conditional repair help characterize what changes, but neither alone determines the outcome. Goryachev changes both substantially with size; Wave-pinning and Holmes broaden their incumbents with much smaller changes in those local readouts. Including all three therefore tests the breadth of the proposed explanation.

The size comparison also changes total material, incumbent architecture and absolute separation (20 versus 40); it does not isolate their causal contributions.

Conditional repair measures immediate reduction of a deficit relative to the paired sham after return. It does not itself establish maintenance: every early unperturbed original core loses material despite a positive conditional coefficient. The native histories supply the separate evidence for incumbent retention.

## Where distance fits

Distance modifies competition after the incumbent has been established. The [Holmes C40 d12/d16 comparison](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/chemical_ring_functional_20261017/ring_functional_Holmes_C40_location_control.png) starts from the exact same complete state and gives merger versus two distinct retained sites. Nearly unchanged starting cue gain cannot explain that contrast by itself. Moving the cue changes both routes around the ring. This supports a position effect within the protocol, not a monotonic distance law.

## The alternative emphasis

A narrower main message would foreground **model-specific changes in initial gain and conditional repair**, with spatial outcomes as consequences still to explain. That has a closer connection to the PDF's existing mechanistic analysis. My preference is to foreground the actual competition result and use those assays to explain part of it. This makes the larger-incumbent argument concrete while keeping the unexplained part visible.

For freely migrating cells, the corresponding readout is the change in direction following a detected challenger, classified by whether that activity disappears, merges or persists. Establishment frequency remains a separate candidate contribution. The [migration note](migration_consequences_bridge_20261017.md) specifies the observational comparisons; a physical activity-to-movement mapping remains unestablished.
