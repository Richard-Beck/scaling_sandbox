# Experimental readouts for incumbent–challenger competition

**Simulated behaviours suggest two questions for observation: does enlargement alter challenger establishment, and/or alter competition after establishment?** Separation may modify the latter. The endpoint here is possible measurements of local polarity behaviour in migrating cells. Greater migration persistence in WGD-associated enlarged cells supplies [biological motivation](README.md). The [current checkpoint](updated_checkpoint_20261017.md) separates all-five linear organization, input gain and the new recovery pilot in Figure 1 from ring separation tests and global activity vectors in Figure 2. This note proposes measurement logic, without establishing spontaneous event rates or a universal size effect. Numerical gain/repair examples below come from the separately linked [supporting ring study](../polarity1d/manuscript/figures/length_mechanisms_20261008/dev/chemical_ring_functional_20261017/RESULTS.md), and must remain distinct from Figure 1's interval values and new recovery assay.

| Readout | Useful measurement logic | Model motivation and an informative contrary finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Incumbent organization before and during competition:** physical extent, peak reporter signal and integrated excess reporter signal. | Declare the site boundary, background reference and imaging normalization. Keep extent, peak and integrated signal separate; an integrated reporter signal requires calibration before interpretation as molecular amount. Record cell size and total sampled perimeter alongside site geometry. | All five retained preparations have larger incumbent active amounts, with different width/peak organization. In cells, absent size-associated organization changes would weaken this proposed correspondence. An increase in raw integrated fluorescence alone would not establish the modeled molecular change. |
| **Activation branch:** early challenger growth, incidence of detectable excursions, and the fraction becoming distinct sustained sites. | Measure early changes in peak, extent and integrated signal over common post-detection windows. Report event rates per cell-time and per available nonincumbent boundary-length–time; more perimeter can offset lower local incidence. Use comparable spatial resolution, temporal sampling and detection sensitivity. Separate births from splits/extensions. Matching detected excursion amplitudes estimates establishment among detected events, rather than response to equal upstream triggers; undetected attempts remain unknown. | Goryachev's initial equal-cue gain falls to **0.528** over C19→39; Wave-pinning's and Holmes's remain approximately **1**. Predicting fewer spontaneous births additionally requires comparable endogenous trigger/noise statistics entering through the same activation channel. Independently comparable upstream inputs yielding no reduced early response despite lower inactive availability would weaken this activation explanation. Equal observed birth counts alone would not resolve it. |
| **Post-activation branch:** actual incumbent loss/rebound, event fate and lifetime. | Follow both the initial incumbent region and the tracked site. Record absolute/fractional signal loss and subsequent rebound; loss from a fixed region can reflect redistribution. Distinguish challenger extinction, incumbent replacement, merger and two retained regions through their histories. Report times to these outcomes and two-site lifetimes; ongoing or incompletely observed events remain censored. Compare sizes at comparable challenger burden and starting fractional loss where overlap permits; then ask whether incumbent organization mediates the association, instead of matching that pathway away. | Basal conditional deficit damping rises **4.267-fold**, **1.159-fold** and **1.015-fold** in Goryachev, Wave-pinning and Holmes over C19→39. The global inactive-profile withdrawal/return assay contains neither a challenger nor local active-site injury; actual competition loss/rebound must be measured separately. Goryachev C80 retains its incumbent through transient challenger activation whereas C40 replaces it; larger Wave-pinning and Holmes rings can retain two sites. Holmes C40,d12 merges while C40,d16 retains two sites from the exact same basal state. No association of organization or separation with observed fate would weaken those experimental correspondences without refuting the native simulations. |

Relate these readouts to **measured size and physical site separations**: retain center separation, edge-to-edge gaps, site extent and total perimeter. Where two boundary routes are meaningful, record both, and distinguish boundary arclength from straight-line distance. A ring circumference is a model coordinate; any proposed comparison to a measured cell perimeter or perimeter segment needs an explicit mapping. The fixed-state Holmes comparison supports position-dependent chemical fate over a finite window, without a monotonic distance law. Across ring sizes, material, incumbent organization and routes change together, so their individual causes remain unresolved. Native active-species concentration and cellular reporter signal are different observables; comparisons should preserve that distinction. Paired reporter imaging and local boundary extension/retraction could assess whether activity sites correspond to protrusions; modeled chemical activity alone does not establish this local correspondence.

> **Proposed caption footer:** Measure incumbent organization, early challenger growth, actual local loss/rebound, and event fates and lifetimes across cell sizes and boundary separations. Separate altered establishment from altered competition after activation.
