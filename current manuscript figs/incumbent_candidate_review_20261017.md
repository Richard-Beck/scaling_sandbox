**Candidate message.** Increasing cell scale changes the incumbent, with a
model-dependent meaning: an existing site can broaden or concentrate more
activity. Two possible consequences are a stronger incumbent restoring response
and reduced competitor activation. The four-panel draft displays the strongest
current chemical evidence for these consequences, using Goryachev and
Wave-pinning as contrasting cases. It is a provisional main-text candidate,
not an agreed final figure.

**Evidence already in the supplied PDF.** Figures 2–4 establish basal
organization, initial local activation per unit cue, and the immediate basal
reaction after an actual inactive-pool perturbation. At length 39 relative to 9,
Goryachev's local activation gain is 0.333 times and restoring reaction per unit
damage is 15.7 times. Wave-pinning is approximately 1.00 and 1.28 times.
Goryachev's incumbent mainly becomes taller; Wave-pinning's mainly broadens.
The initial mean conserved-pool concentration stays fixed, so total material
increases with length by construction. Amount alone is not the mechanistic
claim.

**Why this design might carry the message.** Profiles expose the changed
starting state. The two lower measurements then distinguish reduced rival
responsiveness from maintenance of the incumbent. Both models are displayed
under comparable within-model procedures, instead of interpreting a reversal
duration curve alone.

**Unresolved scientific and editorial points.** The association between site
organization and response is not a causal separation of contributions. Initial
activation does not establish a new site or its fate. Immediate restoring gain
is not a global stability measure or a mechanical force. Wave-pinning does not
share the Goryachev suppression effect. deBelly can instead exhibit rapid
challenged-end activation alongside incumbent retention. A main-text story
must decide whether the two-model chemical comparison is enough, whether a
five-model structural overview is needed, and which one or two insights deserve
the limited panel space.
